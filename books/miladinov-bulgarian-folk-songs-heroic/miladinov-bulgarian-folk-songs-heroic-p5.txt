Where she pastures geese on the road.

Then Yovan the good hero says:

"Alas, mother, alas, dear mother!

As we uttered our curses,

So the curses, mother, seized us.

Teach me now, what shall I do?

For I have no good horse."

And his old mother said to him:

"O my son, o my dear son!

Let me tell you, my son, what to do:

Go to the old mare.

She, my son, has been driven out —

Here she is, for nine years —

She, my son, is not embittered —

Although we drove her out, my son —

Drove her out for the wolves to eat her.

Beg her with great begging;

She is wise and prudent."

As his mother taught him,

Yovan went to Stara Mountain;

And he said to the old mare:

"Mare, my dear mother!

As you live, by the memory you have spent —

Spent in our house —

Come now, go with me,

That we go to Korun's,

To seek both wealth and people."

They set out, and went off;

And he called out from far away:

"Come out, Korun the Highwayman!

Come, once, let us try ourselves —

Try ourselves, hero with hero!

How do you destroy my dear brother —

My dear brother — like a tender lamb?

How do you seize my dear wealth?"

Korun was deceived; he came out.

Yovan the good hero rushed at him;

He seized him by his slender arms;

Bent him down; pinned him to the earth with his knees;

With the mare, under her feet, he trampled him;

And he took Korun's sabre;

He cut off his good head.

And he took the three young brides;

And he took all his own wealth;

Afterwards he took his wealth too;

He came out outside, onto the roads;

He took his old mother;

He took his foolish children;

He went off to his own house.

And Yovan the good hero says to it:

"Rejoice in me, my dear house!

Be honorable, as you have been to me!"



Song 200. Elin Doyka

They asked Elin Doyka:

"When will you bring out Doyka?"

Upper, lower Rumelia —

To bring in the black Plague —

Three years, from far away.

The black Plague, and the ... —

And the fine smallpox —

Mother Doyka did not give;

Nine brothers they gave;

Seven to gather nine;

For she was far away,

With all your foolish children.

"When they go for a first visit..."

The horses are to be mounted;

The rifles are to be loaded;

... they are to dance.

As his mother cursed him,

So the curse seized him.

When they brought out Doyka,

Her mother fiercely cursed them:

"Man, my son, dear son!

Why have you taken Doyka away,

Three years, far away —

With all their foolish children —

And the mother alone remains?"

"How should I be fair-faced as a sister?"

His dear mother had become

A wretched little cuckoo;

She lies sick on her bed;

Cuckoos day and night.

"Only come, take him —

And your little male child;

I will carry you home,

That you see our house."

Elin Doyka had waited —

Had waited three years —

"That my nine brothers come,

And invite me for a first visit."

Elin Doyka wept —

Wept most pitifully:

"Alas, God, dear God!

Since my brothers gave me —

Gave me far away —

To go for a first visit —

How did my brothers not call me!"

And they heard, and came...

The black Plague came to them;

The black Plague, and the ...,

And the fine smallpox —

Three years from far away.

She alone, the mother, remained.

His dear mother became

A wretched little cuckoo:

"Here she is, for three years;

She lies sick on her bed;

She cuckoos day and night."

Elin Doyka was still waiting —

Waiting for her nine brothers

"To come to her,

And invite her for a first visit."

They took his little male child;

They took their swift horse;

They set out, to go off;

And they went and went.

When they came beside the church,

A bird flew past them —

One bird from the trees:

"O sorrowful Elin Doyka!

By whom have you set out —

By the dead Kostadin!

"How have you forgotten me?

What was in me, that you were so eager?"

Elin Doyka was astonished:

"What bird is this? What is it singing to me?

Little or much ... Doyka!

God took pity;

And he sent her an angel —

One angel from the heavens —

And lifted Kostadin up:

"What has the angel done for him —

A loaf from the slab;

A little mortar from the...?"

Poor Doyka was astonished:

What to do, how to do it?

She went as far as home;

What a wonder, a great wonder, she would see!

The empty gates shut;

The courtyards, full and overgrown.

And in the courtyard, her dear mother,

Where she cuckoos day and night.

When Doyka saw him,

She rushed down; she embraced him:

"Alas, brother, dear brother!

Why have you fallen apart so?

For you were the fairest

Of all the nine brothers!"

When Doyka had gone up to her window,

Her dear mother —

Her mother embraced her;

As they embraced each other,

So they fainted away —

From grieving, from weeping.

And Doyka's foolish child —

They trampled it underfoot.

Such was the evil curse.



Song 201. Todora the Daughter

A levy was proclaimed in the City:

"Whoever is fit for me, and of worth,

To go on a strong campaign?

To go on a strong campaign?

Whoever will not go on campaign,

From the fief I will drive him."

All of them were sent on campaign;

Some, a son; some, a dear grandson.

And I have no one at all —

Poor Petko had

Neither a son, nor a dear grandson.

"From the fief they will drive me!"

All that Petko had

[Was] for exactly nine maidens.

Poor Petko walked about the courtyard;

He wrung his white hands

From his white arms;

And he shed small tears

Down his white cheeks —

Until he flooded the courtyard.

Todora saw him —

Todora, his only daughter.

Todora spoke finely:

"O hey, poor father!

What need has driven you,

That you shed small tears for me

Down your white cheeks,

And break your head

From your white arms,

And wring your white hands

From your white knees?"

"O daughter Todora!

How should I not shed tears?

How should I not wring my hands?

A levy has been proclaimed in the City:

Whoever is fit for me, and of worth,

To go on a strong campaign;

Whoever will not go on campaign,

From the fief I will drive him.

All of them have been sent on campaign;

Some, a son; some, a dear grandson.

And I have no one at all —

Poor Petko had

Neither a son, nor a dear grandson.

From the fief they will drive me!"

What Petko had [was] Todora.

Todora spoke finely:

"O hey, poor father!

Has that care found you?

Go into the ground-floor cellars,

And into the dark stables;

Saddle the painted horse;

I will go on campaign for you!"

Poor Petko arose;

He went into the dark stables;

He saddled the painted horse.

She went into the cellars;

She brought out hero's clothes —

Clothes for exactly nine maidens [years'];

And she dressed herself finely;

She mounted the painted horse;

She took the crested banner;

On her belt, a yellow mace;

In her bosom, a Damascus sabre.

She went off on campaign.

She warred as she warred —

For exactly nine years.

When she was coming from the campaign,

She ran down the middle of the market;

Herself, she sang a song:

"Where has it been heard, and seen,

That a maiden-girl wars a war

For exactly nine years?"

When they heard the song,

All of them were astonished;

No one recognized her —

Until she told it herself.

Song 203. The Bride Is Lost to the Tatars

Since the cocks crowed...

Who passed by me through the forest?

They pass by, they have passed by —

The garlanded wedding guests —

With the fairest bride.

Tatars approached —

Tatars, Black Arabs.

The wedding guests were frightened;

They set down the young bride,

With one young bride-man.

And the Tatars lost her —

The Tatars, the Black Arabs.

The wedding guests gathered;

And they went off home.

"Bride, young bride!

I have not drawn my sabre!"...

Young bride then

Tucked up her skirts and sleeves;

She flung her veil from her head;

She flung a rifle on her arm;

And a sharp sabre in her mouth.

They flung what they flung;

She drew her sabre from her mouth...

"Have you flung a rifle?

Have you drawn a sabre?

Neither have I flung a rifle,

Nor..."

She swept around, left and right;

She cut them all down...

Only the Turkish Voivode remained;

And he set about begging truly:

"I beg you, Kralyovitsa!

Do not... me [=do not destroy me]!"

And she seized the fair Voivode;

She cut his feet at the knees;

She cut his hands at the elbows;

She dug out his black eyes —

His black eyes from his head;

She set him on a swift horse;

She charged him to walk himself,

And to beg for God's sake.
