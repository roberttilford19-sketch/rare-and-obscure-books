## The eight repairs

1. **Meaning reversal, C.4 §1 (worst fault).** Was: "No one has power over **the wicked** except what is given from above." Now: "No one has power over **the saints** except what is given from above." Basis: Friedberg, col. 893, read on the page image: "non nocet sanctis, **in quos** non habet quisquam potestatem, nisi desuper datam." The error came from the Internet Archive OCR, which fused "in quos" into "iniquos" ("the wicked"); the draft had translated the OCR ghost. Desk-verified three ways (page image, shelf text, OCR text).

2. **Invented phrase, same §1.** Was: "keeping the order of peace **in place of private peace**." Now: "keeping the order of **civic peace**." Basis: the Latin "si, uice pacis ordinem seruans" with Friedberg's note 126: Augustine's original reads "civicae pacis ordinem" — the order of civic peace. Nothing in the Latin supports "private peace."

3. **Garbled premise, opening dictum.** Was: "**injury**, whether to one's own person or repelled from an ally, is in either case forbidden by the Gospel law." Now: "**the repelling of injury**, whether from one's own person or from an ally, is in either case forbidden by the Gospel law." Basis: "iniuria autem vel a propria persona, vel a socio **repellitur**, quod utrumque euangelica lege prohibetur" — the Gospel law forbids the repelling, and that premise is what the whole quaestio turns on.

4. **False status banner.** Was: "DRAFT — LOCAL ONLY. NOT PUBLISHED, NOT SHELVED, NOT SENT... It has not yet had the final checks required before any public use." Now: "DRAFT — PUBLISHED ON THIS SHELF AS A DRAFT... It stands on the shelf as a draft, open to correction; the translation corrections of 24 October 2026 are listed in the correction record kept with this book." Basis: the file stands on the public shelf; the old banner was false about the file's own status. The AI-assistance disclosure is retained.

5. **False primacy claim (primacy note and dedication).** The note's claim that no published English translation of this question was found is **withdrawn as false**: Causa 23 q.1 is translated in Reichberg, Syse & Begby (eds.), *The Ethics of War* (Blackwell, 2006), ch. 10, pp. 104–124, q.1 from p. 109. The note now states the bounded finding only — no complete English Decretum found after research — and names Thompson (1993, DD.1–20) and Larson (2016, *De penitentia*) accurately. The dedication's "the one modern English volume (Thompson, 1993)" is corrected the same way. **Standing caveat, carried in the note itself:** whether Reichberg translates q.1 complete or in excerpts was NOT established by the audit and is not claimed either way; no counter-overclaim replaces the withdrawn one.

6. **Unsourced historical claim (dedication).** Was: "Before Gratian, church law was a heap of **more than 3,800 canons**..." Now: "...a heap of canons that contradicted one another... Gratian drew **some 3,800 texts** into a single work..." Basis: ~3,800–4,000 is the received count of texts *in Gratian's own Decretum*, not of pre-Gratian law; the shelf's placement was a conflation, unsourced.

7. **Friedberg's own corrections now passed on (six attribution flags).** Gratian's inscriptions stand; each carries a bracketed flag from Friedberg's apparatus:
   - (a) C.1's "Gregory": the passage is **Origen's**, homilies on Joshua, hom. 16 (note 22).
   - (b) "in Proverbs: 'Vengeance is mine'": the words are **Deuteronomy 32:35**, quoted at Romans 12:19 (Correctores note).
   - (c) C.2's "sermon on the centurion's servant": the passage is **Augustine's Letter 138, § 12** (note 35).
   - (d) C.5's sermon "on the Lord's words": **not Augustine's**, per the Maurists; printed as Sermo 82 in the appendix (note 129).
   - (e) C.6's "on the diverse observances of the Church": **no such book of Augustine's exists** (note 153).
   - (f) C.3's letter to Boniface: **[Letter 207] corrected to [Letter 189]**, with a flag recording that the inscription's 207 numbering stands unexplained; the dedication already named 189.
