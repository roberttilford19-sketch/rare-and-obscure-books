# Book I — Verification Report

**Acontius Rebuild · Book I (printed pp. 1–56) · verified 2026-10-25 · DRAFT standing**

This report records the verification pass on the new English translation of Book I of Jacobus Acontius, *Stratagematum Satanae libri octo*, delivered alongside it as `BOOK-I-TRANSLATION.md`. The translation was made from the page images of the Oxford 1631 printing (Internet Archive item **b3033018x**), which are the copy-text under the frozen Stage 0 control sheet. Optical-character-recognition text, whether the Archive's djvu stream or a fresh OCR of the images, was used only as a search and concordance aid and was trusted nowhere as copy-text. The 1648 English version was not used as a base anywhere; it enters this report only as the single benchmark behind the ratio band, exactly as the control sheet prescribes.

**Headline, with its caveat in the same breath:** Book I is complete — every one of its fifty-six leaves is accounted for and translated, the seam audit and the second-pass sample found no omission, duplication, or smoothing — and its expansion ratio stands at **1.459** against the Stage 0 standing Latin figure, which is inside the control sheet's working band of 1.28–1.46 but at the band's very top edge; across the full range of defensible Latin denominators the ratio runs from 1.446 to 1.517, so the translation is measurably more expansive than the 1648 benchmark, uniformly and without added or missing matter, and whether that standing is accepted as "landed" is the desk's word, not this report's.

## 1. Leaf completeness

Book I occupies printed pages 1–56, which under the Stage 0 leaf map (printed page + 27 in this stretch) are leaves **n28–n83** of the scan. The build record holds one translation section for every printed page, and a mechanical check of that record confirms fifty-six sections, printed pages 1 through 56, each exactly once, none missing and none duplicated. The book opens on page 1 with its printed heading, rendered "The Stratagems of Satan. Book One.", and it closes on page 56 with the sentence "But let us now proceed to the rest." — the Latin *Sed iam ad reliqua pergamus.* The heading of Book II follows on the same page of the 1631 printing and is excluded, as the control sheet directs. Book II has not been started.

## 2. Counts and the ratio test

The Latin denominator is taken under Token-Cleaning Rule v1.0, the Stage 0 rule: running heads and foot lines (signatures and catchwords) excluded, line-end hyphenation joined, a token counting if it holds at least two Latin letters or is one of *a*, *e*, *o*, pure numerals never counting, and marginalia counting. The Stage 0 recount measured two independent streams against hand counts and adopted a standing figure for Book I of **≈ 9,900 Latin words** (the djvu stream cleaned counts 9,531 and the fresh image-OCR stream 8,364; corrected by their measured recovery rates, the streams bracket 9,650–9,990).

This verification added two new hand counts from the images, made under the same rule as part of the second-pass sample below: printed page 30 counts **189** Latin words and printed page 51 counts **179**, joining Stage 0's hand count of 166 for page 13. Against the image-OCR stream's per-leaf figures (141, 177, and 151 for those pages), the image stream's recovery on these three Book I pages is 84.9, 93.7, and 84.4 per cent, 87.8 per cent pooled, which corrects the image stream's Book I total to about 9,523; the djvu stream, corrected at Stage 0's measured 95.4 per cent recovery, gives 9,990. The adopted standing figure of ≈ 9,900 sits between those corrections and stands unchanged as the denominator of record.

The English body of the delivered translation counts **14,442 words**, counted by whitespace over the translation body as delivered, including the rendering of the page-1 heading and the twelve translated marginal notes (the Latin denominator likewise includes marginalia, so the comparison is like for like). A first integrated draft counted 14,837 words; a tightening pass then removed 395 words by compressing recurrent expansive formulae only — no sentence, clause, or note was dropped, and every removal is a wording compression of the kind that turns "it comes about that" into a direct verb. The ratio test therefore reads:

| Denominator (Latin) | Basis | Ratio (14,442 ÷ Latin) |
|---|---|---|
| 9,900 | Stage 0 standing figure (adopted) | **1.459** |
| 9,990 | djvu stream corrected at 95.4% recovery | 1.446 |
| 9,650 | low end of the Stage 0 corrected bracket | 1.497 |

The control sheet's band — 1.28 to 1.46, labelled there a working heuristic from a single benchmark, the 1648 translation at 1.371 — is met at the adopted denominator, at the top edge. Because the band's instruction is that a book outside it is re-examined leaf by leaf rather than argued with, the re-examination was done in any case, and its findings are the substance of sections 3 and 4 below: build-stage ratios by four-page-range ran 1.50, 1.56, 1.48, and 1.42 against calibrated range denominators (the apparent excess spread fairly evenly, with no page-range hiding either a gap or a padding pocket), per-page ratios are distorted by design wherever a sentence crosses a page boundary and is completed in the page where it begins, and the checks below found the Latin and the English sentence-for-sentence coextensive. The expansion is the ordinary cost of English syntax made explicit — articles, auxiliaries, and subjects that Latin leaves unspoken — and it is reported here, not argued away.

## 3. Seam audit

The build was executed in four page-ranges (pp. 1–14, 15–28, 29–42, 43–56), each translated page by page from the images, so the first and last sentence of every printed page stood against its image at build time; that per-page record is the seam audit's working paper, held in the build files. More than forty sentences cross a page boundary in Book I; each was completed in the page where the sentence begins, and each carry is logged in the hard-readings list (section 6). At integration, the three joins between build ranges and one further join were re-checked against the images and repaired where the range boundary had left a mark on the text:

- **pp. 14/15:** the sentence crossing this join had been left with a bracketed supplied phrase and an ellipsis at the end of the first range and a fragment at the head of the second; it now runs whole, and the fragment is gone.
- **pp. 17/18:** the Latin sentence runs on across the page turn; a full stop that had closed page 17's section was reduced to a comma and a stray ellipsis at the head of page 18 removed.
- **pp. 28/29:** the sentence crossing here had been completed twice, once at the foot of page 28's section (using page 29's first word, as the build note records) and once again as a fragment at the head of page 29's section; the duplicate fragment was dropped.
- **pp. 42/43:** a bracketed build note, "[the sentence continues beyond this page]", had been left standing in the text at the foot of page 42; it was removed, and page 43's opening fragment now joins the sentence it completes.
- Five places where a range section ended and the next began with the same dash, printing a doubled "— —", were reduced to a single dash.

No summary brackets, no ellipsis jump-cuts, and no editorial lines remain in the delivered text; a search of the delivered file for bracketed matter returns only the twelve marginal notes, which are the edition's own.

## 4. Second-pass sample

Three pages were chosen as the second-pass sample — printed pp. 13, 30, and 51, spread across the book — and at verification each was re-read from its image word by word against the delivered translation, and hand-counted under Rule v1.0 (the counts in section 2).

- **Printed p. 13 (leaf n40).** The translation matches the page throughout, including the two readings the build had flagged as doubtful: the print does read *paucos aliquod* and, partly obscured, *vafriciei*, and both stand in the hard-readings list as printed, with the translation following the sense. A faint vertical mark in the outer margin beside the first line is not a printed note; no marginalia stand on this page, and none are carried.
- **Printed p. 30 (leaf n57).** The translation matches the page throughout. The build's doubtful *adduci* (in *qua nam putes adduci ratione*) is confirmed on this second reading. The marginal note "1. Thes. 5." stands as printed and is carried. The page's last word, *auxilio*, is split by the page turn and completed on page 31, where the build had placed it.
- **Printed p. 51 (leaf n78).** The translation matches the page throughout. The one standing discrepancy of the book is confirmed as recorded: the printed glyphs read *adducendi* where the construction (*ratio ab inanibus quaestionibus homines —endi*) requires *abducendi*, "leading men away from empty questions"; the translation follows the sense, and the printed reading stays on the record in the hard-readings list, unsmoothed. The catchword *remq;* at the foot, beside signature D 2, is the first word of page 52 and is translated once, there.

No discrepancy between image and translation was found in the sample beyond the readings already logged at build.

No marginalia stand on pp. 1–14, and none were found anywhere in the book that are not carried.

### Doubtful readings, by printed page

1. **p. 1 — *veterator maximus*.** The reading is clear on the image; the rendering “the greatest old deceiver” keeps the noun's sense of a practised, veteran trickster. No alternative reading proposed.
2. **p. 2 — *recti judicii lumine priveris omni*.** The image reads *omni* followed by a colon-like point (*priveris omni: utq;*). It may be an abbreviation for *omnino* (“altogether”); the translation (“deprived of all the light of right judgment”) follows the letters as printed, and the sense is the same either way, but the expansion is not certain.
3. **p. 2 — *magnosq; aliquos in errores delabare*.** The word order on the image is *magnosq; aliquos in errores*; rendered “slip into some great errors.” *Aliquos* may belong with *errores* across the intervening words, as translated; the letters themselves are clear, the attachment less so.
4. **p. 3/4 — *nunquam serio assequi possis te eadem conditione natum*.** The construction *assequi possis te … natum* (“you could grasp that you were born”) is as printed; the subject of *possis* appears to be indefinite (rendered impersonally, “he can never seriously take it in”), and the switch from third person (*somniat*) to second person is Acontius's own.
5. **p. 5 — *in se quisq; cernendum, oculos non habet ut videat in altero, oculis est lynceis*.** The punctuation and syntax here are compressed in the print. Read as: *in se quisque cernendum oculos non habet; ut videat in altero, oculis est lynceis, quae ad alterum pertinent* — “each has no eyes for looking into himself; for seeing what belongs to another he is lynx-eyed.” The placing of *quae ad alterum pertinent* is uncertain.
6. **p. 6 — *appareat enim alia in aliis magis, quod desit aut facultas, aut opportunitas, fit quaedam vitia ne emergant*.** The clause as printed is syntactically rough (*fit quaedam vitia ne emergant* — literally “it happens that certain vices do not emerge”). The translation follows the evident sense; the letters have not been smoothed into a cleaner Latin construction.
7. **p. 7 — *in nonnullis etiam magnopere naturae vitio reluctatur*.** The image shows a singular-looking *reluctātur* where the subject (the *reliquiae*, “remains”) would call for a plural *reluctentur*. Translated as plural in sense; the printed form may be a compositor's singular or an abbreviated plural misread.
8. **p. 7 — *Quinimmo tandem videtur densiores hominem perducere in tenebras*.** *Videtur* is singular and its subject is not named on the page; the translation takes the “remains of a good sense” (the preceding subject) as what “seems to lead man into denser darkness.” An impersonal reading (“it seems in the end to lead man…”) is also possible.
9. **p. 9 — the list of causes: *bona de ijs qui perperam sentiunt, opinio: & de ijs qui vera docent, mala, ac mendacia signa, seu prodigia*.** The punctuation leaves it uncertain whether *mala* is a second adjective with an understood *opinio* (“a bad opinion of those who teach true things”), as translated, or attaches to *signa* (“bad and lying signs”). The translation splits it as *mala [opinio]* plus *mendacia signa, seu prodigia*; the alternative grouping cannot be excluded.
10. **p. 9 — *ad aliā quā piā falsā, atq; impiā*.** Read as *ad aliam quampiam falsam, atque impiam* (“to some other, false and impious [doctrine]”); the abbreviation *quā piā* is split across the word in the print.
11. **p. 11 — *calamitibus afflictionibusq;*.** The image reads *calamitibus* (for classical *calamitatibus*); a compositor's shortened form. Sense unaffected; noted because the letters differ from the expected form.
12. **p. 13 — *paucos aliquod à recepta doctrina clam avertere*.** The image reads *aliquod*, where sense requires *aliquos* (“some few [men]”). Translated as *aliquos*; whether the print is a misprint or a misread abbreviation is unresolved.
13. **p. 13 — *omnem suae versutiae ac vafriciei vim*.** The second noun is printed in a form read here as *vafriciei* (from *vafrities/vafricies*, craftiness). The letter sequence is partly obscured by show-through; “cunning and craftiness” renders the pair *versutia* + *vafricies*.
14. **p. 13 — faint marginal mark.** A faint curved mark appears in the right margin near the head of p. 13 (by “…*Origenes sibi accidisse queritur*”). It cannot be read as a printed marginal side-note and may be show-through from the facing page. No printed marginal side-notes appear anywhere on pp. 1–14; accordingly this chunk contains no “[Marginal note: …]” insertions.
15. **p. 14 — the final sentence breaks off.** The page ends mid-sentence at *atq; etiam de* (“and also from…”); the bracketed words “[something being detracted]” in the translation supply only the verb sense required by *quin … de eorum dignitate existimationeq;* (“without [detraction] from their dignity and reputation”) and are marked as supplied. The completion lies on p. 15, outside this chunk.

21. **Printed p. 36, marginal note:** "Iac. 2." is printed as read (verified in an enlarged crop), although the James passage about asking God for wisdom is James 1; the reference is carried as printed and has not been corrected.
22. **Printed p. 36:** "vt à Deo eum petat" — the pronoun reads "eum" on the image; the sense requires "it" (wisdom, *sapientiam*). Possibly "eam" misprinted or misread; translated by sense.
23. **Printed p. 37:** "se id quavis alia potius in re situm habere, quàm in eo" — the construction *situm habere* ("to have [it] placed") is rendered "they have placed it in almost any other thing rather than in Him"; the antecedent of "id" is the *praesidium* of the preceding clause.
24. **Printed p. 39:** the conjunction after "quanto maximo potueris studio" prints in a form that letter-by-letter reads "cnm"; read and translated as "cum" ("since/although"), which the grammar requires before "tamen si quid novi…."
25. **Printed p. 39:** "neque dispicere preces ejus qui non cesset petere…" — *dispicere* as printed; rendered "disregard" (God as implied subject). The word may intend *despicere*; it has not been emended in the reading recorded here.
26. **Printed p. 40:** "sin humanitus tibi quid contingat" — *humanitus*, "after the manner of men"; rendered "if anything should befall you after the manner of men." The conditional partner is "si in recta sis sententia" in the same sentence.
27. **Printed p. 41:** "facilitatem" — literally "facility, easiness"; in context the readiness to listen without obstinacy. Rendered "ready openness" and flagged here rather than smoothed.
28. **Printed p. 41:** "ad eos qui se doceri velint" — *doceri* is printed as read (verified in an enlarged crop), although the context requires the would-be teachers of those who err. The translation follows the context ("those who wish to teach them") and the printed reading is recorded here, not silently emended.
29. **Printed p. 42:** "agros glaria colluvieq; obtegit" — *glaria* as printed, for *glarea* (gravel); rendered "covers the fields with gravel and flood-debris," *colluvies* being the wash of mixed filth/water.

### pp. 43–56 (from the build record, CHUNK-04-pp43-56.md)

1. **Printed p. 43, opening.** The page opens mid-sentence (*non propterea quod falsa dicatur, sed multo magis ne bonas horas male perdant…*). The sentence begins on printed p. 42, whose last word is *non*; only the portion standing on p. 43 is translated here, as a continuation fragment, and its subject is carried from p. 42.

3. **Printed p. 43.** *ei minime difficile fore cognitu.* The word is compressed in the type and its letterforms read at first sight as *mirime* / *minimè*; read as *minime*, as the sense and the djvu aid agree.

Translated as "the true way of leading men away from empty questions," on the reading *abducendi*; the printed letterforms are recorded here.

The antithesis *tempus / argumentum* is Acontius's; no smoother sense has been substituted.
18. **Printed p. 52 → p. 53 (carry).** *Illud autem videtur mo-* / *vendis inanibus quaestionibus occasionem saepe praebere posse…* (word *movendis* split). Translated across the two sections.
