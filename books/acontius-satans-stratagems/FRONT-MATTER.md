# THE STRATAGEMS OF SATAN

## *Stratagematum Satanae libri octo* — the eight books, freshly translated from the Latin

### by Jacobus Acontius (Giacopo Aconcio, c. 1520–c. 1566/67)

**DRAFT — UNREVIEWED.** This is a working draft of a new English translation, made at the Tilford desk in 2026. It has not been read by an outside Latinist and is not a finished edition. It is published on this shelf in draft standing, under its own verification record, so that the work and the evidence for it stand open together. Do not cite it as a finished translation. Each book also retains its own draft banner and front matter exactly as delivered.

**The source.** The Latin text translated here is *Stratagematum Satanae libri octo* as printed at Oxford in 1631 (for W. Webb); the first edition appeared at Basle in 1565. The copy-text is the page images of that 1631 printing, Internet Archive item **b3033018x**. The translation was made leaf by leaf from the page images of all eight books — printed pp. 1–426, scan leaves n28–n451 — with the edition's marginal notes carried in brackets at their places in the text. Machine-readable text of the scan was used only as a search and concordance aid and was trusted nowhere as copy-text.

**Prior translations, named truthfully.** The prior complete English translation of all eight books is that of **Walter T. Curtis (Sutro Branch, California State Library, 1940)**. Curtis was consulted only to check sense; no rendering in this draft rests on his work, and he is nowhere quoted into the text. The first Englishing of any part of the work is the anonymous **1648** translation, which covers **Books I–IV only**; it was not used as a base, a crib, or a control anywhere in this draft. It enters the record in one place only: as the single benchmark (ratio 1.371) behind the working expansion band used in the verification notes — a band the notes themselves label a single-benchmark heuristic, with every ratio stated as description, not verdict.

**Note on pagination.** The 1631 edition passes from p. 192 directly to p. 195; no leaves are wanting. The text is continuous across the joint: p. 192 closes with a completed sentence, its catchword "Sed" is the first word of p. 195, the gathering signature N opens at p. 195, and three independent copies of the edition show the identical jump. The skip is the edition's own, and the translation follows the text across it without supply or interruption. (Ruled at the Tilford desk, 2026-10-25, on the Stage 0 joint read; the evidence stands in the build record.)
