## 7. Fences, discrepancies, and what this note does not claim

The fences stood: copy-text was the page images of IA item b3033018x and nothing else; the djvu text served as finding aid and denominator source only; neither the 1648 English nor Curtis (1940) was opened as a base; no shelf or clone file was touched, nothing was pushed or uploaded; Book VI does not start — its heading leaf was read only to fix the boundary. The one write outside the rebuild directory is the boundary-and-denominator correction appended to the control-sheet addendum (its §5), as the standing instruction directs; the frozen control sheet was not rewritten.

Discrepancies, none of them smoothed:

1. **The control sheet's end boundary is wrong** (§1): Book VI's heading stands at the head of p. 291, not the foot of p. 290. Corrected on the record in addendum §5.
2. **Stage 0's Book V denominator excludes the book's last leaf** (§2): the 11,971 figure aggregates leaves n246–n314 and leaves leaf n315's 142 words to Book VI's figure. Flagged for Book VI's crew in addendum §5.
3. **Book IV's verification mis-describes this book's opening page, in two particulars.** Its §1 and §5 state that Book V's opening on p. 221 carries a marginal note *An virtutes & vitia in medio sita sint*, and its §1 quotes Book V's opening sentence as "*CONTRA legem qui committunt, difficillimum est, recta illos incedere via.*" Neither matches this copy. Both margins of leaf n246 were examined on enlarged crops: the outer margin of p. 221 carries nothing but faint reversed show-through ghosts of the verso's italics, and the note stands nowhere in pp. 221–234 — nor anywhere else in the book: the whole-span inventory of §5 lists all 23 notes that stand in Book V of this copy, and that note is not among them. The opening sentence is the *partim intelligunt* sentence quoted in §1. The desk's reading of the discrepancy: Book IV's two assertions were drawn from some source other than this copy's page (the desk that built them was reading the same images under time pressure, and the show-through on this leaf is heavy); the images govern. Book IV's own translation is unaffected — the note was never inside Book IV's span, and its verification only pointed at it across the boundary — but its two descriptive sentences about Book V stand uncorrected in a closed verification file, and this note does not rewrite it; the correction stands here and in chunk 1's entry 2, where the point was first settled against the page.
4. **Interim figures are not build figures** (the lesson of Book IV's §7, applied): one chunk crew reported its section words on a different basis (3,963, its section headings included) than the uniform basis of §2 (3,907 for the same file, reconciling exactly), and another crew's summary cited two of its own hard-reading entries by the wrong numbers (the clipped-edge record in its file stands at entries 40 and 8, not 28 and 34 as its summary said). Both differences are reporting slips, not text differences — the consolidated readings themselves are correct — and every figure and entry citation in this note is built from the files, the consolidated counts reconciling against the chunk records to the word.

What this note does not claim: no independent collation of this copy against a second copy of the 1631 edition was performed; the one doubtful letter-form in the sample that rests on construction rather than a clean glyph (chunk 5, entry 15, the *fi-/dem* ligature reading on p. 280) is recorded as such in the readings and stands on the sense of the passage. The translation remains a draft, pending the desk's review pass, like Books I–IV at this stage of their builds.
