PART I — THE GREEK (AKHMIM) FRAGMENT

This part comes from a Greek fragment found at Akhmim in Egypt. It was buried in a tomb alongside part of the Gospel of Peter, in a manuscript now kept in Cairo. Only part of the book survives here, beginning in the middle of a speech by the Lord and then showing the place of the righteous and the place of punishment. A tiny separate Greek leaf in the Bodleian Library adds a few more lines about the same punishments.

1 Many of them will be false prophets. They will teach paths and teachings of many kinds that lead to ruin.

2 And they will become children of ruin.

3 Then God will come to my faithful people, who hunger and thirst and suffer and test their souls in this life, and he will judge the children of wrongdoing.

4 The Lord added and said: Let us go to the mountain and pray.

5 We, the twelve disciples, went with him. We begged him to show us one of our righteous brothers who had died, so that we could see what he looks like, take courage ourselves, and give courage to those who will hear us.

6 While we were praying, suddenly two men appeared standing before the Lord — perhaps to the east — and we could not look directly at them.

7 A ray like sunlight came from their faces. Their clothing shone in a way no human eye has ever seen. No mouth can tell and no heart can grasp the glory they wore or the beauty of their faces.

8 When we saw them we were stunned. Their bodies were whiter than any snow and redder than any rose.

9 The red was mixed in with the white. In short, I cannot describe their beauty.

10 Their hair was curly and blooming. It fell in a fair way around their faces and shoulders like a wreath woven from spikenard and flowers of many kinds, or like a rainbow in the air. That was how lovely they were.

11 We were stunned at them because they appeared so suddenly.

12 I came near the Lord and said: Who are these?

13 He said to me: These are your righteous brothers, the ones you wanted to see.

14 I said to him: Where are all the righteous? What kind of world do they live in, where they have this glory?

15 The Lord showed me a very large place outside this world. It was brighter than bright with light. The air there was lit by the sun's beams. The earth itself flowered with blossoms that never fade. It was full of spices and plants, fair-flowering and never rotting, bearing blessed fruit.

16 The blossom was so great that its smell reached us even where we stood.

17 The people who lived in that place wore the shining clothes of angels, and their clothing was like their land.

18 Angels moved about among them there.

19 The glory of those who lived there was the same for all. With one voice they praised the Lord God, rejoicing in that place.

20 The Lord said to us: This is the place of your leaders, the righteous men.

21 I also saw another place, opposite that one, very foul and dirty. It was a place of punishment. Those being punished there, and the angels who punished them, wore dark clothing that matched the air of the place.

22 Some people there were hanging by their tongues. These were the ones who had spoken evil against the way of righteousness. Fire was laid under them, flaming and tormenting them.

23 There was a great lake full of flaming mire. Certain men were in it who had turned away from righteousness. Tormenting angels were set over them.

24 There were also other women hanging by their hair above that boiling mire. These were the ones who had dressed themselves up for adultery. The men who had joined with them in the defilement of adultery were hanging by their feet, with their heads hidden down in the mire, and they said: We did not believe we would come to this place.

25 I saw the murderers, and those who had agreed with them, thrown into a narrow place full of evil creeping beasts. The beasts struck them, and they twisted about in that torment. Worms like dark clouds were set on them. The souls of those who had been murdered stood and watched the torment of their murderers and said: O God, your judgment is righteous.

26 Close by that place I saw another narrow place. The discharge and the stench of those in torment ran down there, and it was like a lake. Women sat in that fluid up to their necks. Facing them sat many children who had been born before their time, crying. Rays of fire went out from the children and struck the women in the eyes. These were the women who had conceived outside marriage [?] and had caused the unborn child to be destroyed.

27 Other men and women were being burned up to their middles and thrown down in a dark place and beaten by evil spirits. Worms that never rest ate their insides. These were the ones who had persecuted the righteous and handed them over.

28 Near them again were women and men biting their lips in torment. Iron heated in fire was set against their eyes. These were the ones who had spoken evil against and slandered the way of righteousness.

29 Facing these were still others, men and women biting their tongues, with flaming fire in their mouths. These were the false witnesses.

30 In another place there were gravel-stones sharper than swords or any spike, heated with fire. Men and women in filthy rags rolled on them in torment. These were the rich who trusted in their riches. They had no pity on orphans and widows and paid no attention to God's commandments.

31 In another great lake full of pus, blood, and boiling mire, men and women stood up to their knees. These were the ones who lent money and demanded interest on top of interest.

32 Other men and women were thrown down from a great cliff. They fell to the bottom. Those set over them drove them back up onto the cliff, and they were thrown down to the bottom again. They had no rest from this torment. These were the ones who defiled their bodies, acting as women. The women with them were the ones who lay with one another as a man lies with a woman.

33 Beside that cliff was a place full of much fire. There stood men who with their own hands had made images for themselves in place of God. Beside them were other men and women holding rods of fire, striking one another, never resting from this kind of torment.

34 Yet others near them, men and women, were burning and turning about, roasted as if in a pan. These were the ones who had forsaken the way of God.

The Bodleian Leaf

PART II — THE ETHIOPIC VERSION

This part comes from a fuller copy kept in Ethiopic, inside a collection said to contain revelations that Peter gave to Clement. It tells the story from the beginning — the Lord on the Mount of Olives speaking about the end, the resurrection, and the judgment — and then gives a longer list of punishments and, at the end, a picture of paradise. It has some added material at its start and close that the Greek fragment does not have.

The Second Coming of Christ and the Resurrection of the Dead

Which Christ revealed to Peter — concerning those who died because of their sins, because they did not keep the commandment of God their Creator.

Peter thought on these things, so that he might understand the mystery of the Son of God, the merciful one who loves mercy.

When the Lord was sitting on the Mount of Olives, his disciples came to him.

We begged him, each of us, and prayed to him, saying: Tell us what the signs of your coming and of the end of the world are, so that we may know and mark the time of your coming. We must teach those who come after us, to whom we preach the word of your gospel and whom we set over your church, so that when they hear it they may take care and mark the time of your coming.

Our Lord answered us and said: Take care that no one leads you astray, and do not doubt or serve other gods. Many will come in my name, saying, I am the Christ. Do not believe them and do not go near them. For the coming of the Son of God will not be clear ahead of time. But as lightning shines from east to west, so I will come on the clouds of heaven with a great host, in my glory. With my cross going before my face I will come in my glory, shining seven times more than the sun. I will come in my glory with all my saints, my holy angels. My Father will set a crown on my head, so that I may judge the living and the dead and pay back every person according to his works.

Learn the meaning from the fig tree. As soon as its shoot has come out and its twigs have grown, the end of the world will come.

I, Peter, answered and said to him: Explain the fig tree to me, so we can understand. For all through its life the fig tree puts out shoots, and every year it bears fruit for its master. What then does the parable of the fig tree mean? We do not know.

The Lord answered and said to me: Do you not understand that the fig tree is the house of Israel? Think of a man who planted a fig tree in his garden. It bore no fruit. He looked for its fruit for many years, and when he did not find it he said to the keeper of his garden: Pull up this fig tree, so it does not make our ground unfruitful. The gardener said to God: Let us clear the weeds from around it, dig the ground about it, and water it. If it still bears no fruit, we will at once pull its roots out of the garden and plant another in its place. Have you not understood that the fig tree is the house of Israel? Truly I say to you, when its twigs have sprouted in the last days, then false Christs will come. They will stir up hope, saying: I am the Christ, who has now come into the world. And when they — Israel — see the wickedness of their deeds, they will turn away from them and deny him whom our fathers praised, the first Christ, whom they crucified. In that they sinned a great sin. But this deceiver is not the Christ. [Something seems wrong in the copying here. The sense needed is that Israel sees the wickedness of the Antichrist and does not follow him.] When they reject him, he will kill with the sword, and there will be many martyrs. Then the twigs of the fig tree — that is, the house of Israel — will shoot out. Many will become martyrs by his hand. Enoch and Elijah will be sent to teach them that this is the deceiver who must come into the world and do signs and wonders in order to deceive. And so those who die by his hand will be martyrs. They will be counted among the good and righteous martyrs who have pleased God in their lives.

Then he showed me, in his right hand, the souls of all people. And on the palm of his right hand was a picture of what will happen on the last day: how the righteous and the sinners will be separated, how those who are upright in heart will be kept, and how the evildoers will be pulled up by the roots forever. We saw how the sinners wept in great trouble and sorrow, until everyone who saw it with their eyes wept — the righteous, the angels, and he himself also.

I asked him and said: Lord, let me speak your word about the sinners. It would have been better for them if they had never been made. The Saviour answered and said to me: Peter, why do you say that it would have been better for them not to have been made? You are standing against God. You would not show more pity than he does toward his own image. For he made them and brought them out of nothing. Now, because you have seen the weeping that will come on the sinners in the last days, your heart is troubled. But I will show you their works, by which they sinned against the Most High.

Look now at what will come on them in the last days, when the day of God comes, the day of the decision of God's judgment. From east to west all the children of men will be gathered together before my Father, who lives forever. He will order hell to open its bars of hard metal and give up everything that is in it.

He will order the wild beasts and the birds to give back all the flesh they have eaten, because he wants people to appear again. For nothing is lost before God, and nothing is impossible with him, because all things are his.

For all things will happen on the day of decision, on the day of judgment, at the word of God. Just as all things were done when he made the world and ordered all that is in it and it was done — so it will be in the last days. For all things are possible with God. And so he says in the Scripture: Son of man, prophesy over these bones and say to the bones: bone to bone, joined together, with sinews, nerves, flesh, and skin and hair on them, and soul and spirit.

The great Uriel will give them soul and spirit at God's command. For God has set him over the rising of the dead at the day of judgment.

Think about grains of wheat sown in the earth. People sow them dry and without soul in the earth. They live again and bear fruit, and the earth gives them back like something entrusted to it for safekeeping.

[And this thing that dies, that is sown as seed in the earth and will become alive and be given life again, is man. This is probably an added note in the margin.]

How much more will God raise up on the day of decision those who believe in him and are chosen by him, for whose sake he made the world? The earth will give back all things on the day of decision, for it too will be judged along with them, and heaven along with it.

This will come on the day of judgment on those who have fallen away from faith in God and have committed sin. Floods of fire will be let loose. Darkness and blackness will come up and cover and hide the whole world. The waters will be changed and turned into coals of fire, and everything in them will burn. The sea will become fire. Under heaven there will be a sharp fire that cannot be put out, flowing to carry out the judgment of wrath. The stars will fly apart in flames of fire, as if they had never been made. The powers — the firmaments — of heaven will pass away for lack of water and will be as if they had never been. The lightnings of heaven will be no more, and by their spell they will frighten the world [probably: heaven will turn to lightning, and its lightnings will frighten the world]. The spirits of the dead bodies will also be like them and will become fire at God's command.

As soon as the whole creation comes apart, the people in the east will flee to the west, and those in the west to the east. Those in the south will flee to the north, and those in the north to the south. In every place the wrath of a fearful fire will catch up with them. An unquenchable flame will drive them and bring them to the judgment of wrath, to the stream of unquenchable fire that flows flaming with fire. When its waves separate from one another, burning, there will be great gnashing of teeth among the children of men.

Then they will all see me coming on an everlasting cloud of brightness. The angels of God who are with me will sit — probably: I will sit — on the throne of my glory at the right hand of my heavenly Father. He will set a crown on my head. When the nations see it, they will weep, each nation apart by itself.

Then he will order them to enter the river of fire, while the works of each of them stand before them — something is missing here — to each person according to his deeds. As for the elect who have done good, they will come to me and will not see death by the devouring fire. But the unrighteous, the sinners, and the hypocrites will stand in the depths of darkness that does not pass away. Their punishment is the fire. Angels will bring forward their sins and prepare a place for them where they will be punished forever, each one according to his wrongdoing.

Uriel, the angel of God, will bring out the souls of those sinners who died in the flood, and of all who lived in idols — in every melted image, in every object of love, in pictures, and of those who lived on all the hills and in stones and by the roadside, whom people called gods. They will burn them together with them — with the things they lived in, or with their worshippers — in everlasting fire. After all of them and their dwelling places are destroyed, they will be punished forever.

Here begins the description of the torments, which we also have in another copy, in the Akhmim fragment.

Gr. 22 Then men and women will come to the place prepared for them. By their tongues — the tongues they used to speak evil against the way of righteousness — they will be hung up. Unquenchable fire is spread under them, so that they cannot escape it.

Gr. 23 See, another place. In it is a great pit, full of ——. In it are those who denied righteousness. Angels of punishment beat them there and kindle on them the fire of their torment.

Gr. 24 And see again women [the copy is damaged here: perhaps "two women"]. They hang them up by their necks and by their hair. They will throw them into the pit. These are the ones who braided their hair, not to make themselves beautiful in a good way, but to turn men toward fornication, so they could trap the souls of men and lead them to ruin. And the men who lay with them in fornication will be hung by their loins in that place of fire. They will say to one another: We did not know we would come into everlasting punishment.

Gr. 25 The murderers and those who joined with them will be thrown into the fire, in a place full of poisonous beasts. They will be tormented without rest and will feel their pains. Their worms will be as many as a dark cloud. The angel Ezrael will bring out the souls of those who were killed, and they will look at the torment of those who killed them and say to one another: Righteousness and justice are the judgment of God. For we heard, but we did not believe, that we would come into this place of eternal judgment.

Gr. 26 Close by this flame there will be a pit, great and very deep. Into it flows down from above every kind of torment, filth, and discharge. Women are swallowed in it up to their necks and tormented with great pain. These are the ones who caused their children to be born before their time and spoiled the work of God who made them. Facing them will be another place where their children sit alive, and they cry out to God. Flashes — lightnings — go out from those children and strike the eyes of those who, for the sake of fornication, caused their destruction.

Other men and women will stand naked over them. Their children will stand facing them in a place of delight. They sigh and cry to God because of their parents, saying: These are the ones who despised and cursed and broke your commandments and gave us over to death. They cursed the angel who formed us, and hung us up, and kept back from us — begrudged us — the light you have given to all creatures. And the milk of their mothers, flowing from their breasts, will harden, and from it will come flesh-eating beasts. These will come out and turn and torment them forever, together with their husbands, because they forsook God's commandments and killed their children. As for their children, they will be given to the angel Temlakos — that is, a care-taking angel. And those who killed them will be tormented forever, for God wills it so.

Gr. 27 Ezrael, the angel of wrath, will bring men and women with half their bodies burning and throw them into a place of darkness, the very hell of men. A spirit of wrath will punish them with every kind of torment, and a worm that never sleeps will eat their insides. These are the persecutors and betrayers of my righteous ones.

Gr. 28 Beside those who are there will be other men and women biting their tongues. They will torment them with red-hot iron and burn their eyes. These are the ones who slander and doubt my righteousness.

Gr. 29 Other men and women whose works were done in deceit will have their lips cut off. Fire will enter their mouths and their insides. These are the false witnesses. (Another reading: these are the ones who caused the martyrs to die by their lying.)

Gr. 30 Beside them, in a place close by, on the stone there will be a pillar of fire, and the pillar is sharper than swords. There will be men and women dressed in rags and filthy clothes. They will be thrown onto it, to suffer the judgment of a torment that never stops. These are the ones who trusted in their riches and despised widows and the woman with fatherless children before God.

Gr. 31 Into another place close by, full of filth, they throw men and women up to their knees. These are the ones who lent money and took interest.

Gr. 32 Other men and women throw themselves down from a high place and come back again and run, and devils drive them. [These are the worshippers of idols.] They drive them to the end of their wits — drive them up to the top of the height — and they throw themselves down. They do this again and again and are tormented forever. These are the ones who cut their flesh as [apostles?] of a man. And the women who were with them . . . And these are the men who defiled themselves together as women do. (This part is badly damaged in the copying, but the sense is clear in the Greek.)

Gr. 33 Beside them there will be — a brazier? — . . . and beneath them the angel Ezrael will prepare a place of much fire. All the idols of gold and silver, all idols, the work of men's hands, and the images in the shape of cats and lions, of creeping things and wild beasts, and the men and women who made those images, will be in chains of fire. They will be punished for their error in front of the idols. This is their judgment forever. (In the Greek they beat one another with rods of fire, which is the better sense.)

Gr. 34 Beside them will be other men and women burning in the fire of the judgment. Their torment is everlasting. These are the ones who forsook God's commandment and followed the promptings of devils.

Parts of these last two sections are also in the Bodleian fragment. At this point the Akhmim fragment ends. The Ethiopic copy continues:

There will be another place, very high. (Some sentences here are damaged. One translator leaves them out. Another renders them doubtfully: There will be a furnace and a brazier where fire will burn. The fire that burns will come from one end of the brazier.) The men and women whose feet slip will go rolling down into a place where there is fear. While the prepared fire flows, they climb up and fall down again and keep rolling down. (This sounds like a narrow bridge over a stream of fire that they keep trying to cross.) So they will be tormented forever. These are the ones who did not honour their father and mother and of their own will kept themselves away from them. And so they will be punished forever.

Further, the angel Ezrael will bring children and young women, to show them those who are being tormented. They will be punished with pains, with hanging up [?], and with many wounds that flesh-eating birds will tear into them. These are the ones who pride themselves in — trust in — their sins, and do not obey their parents, do not follow the teaching of their fathers, and do not honour those who are older than they.

Beside them will be girls dressed in darkness as a garment. They will be badly punished and their flesh will be torn in pieces. These are the ones who did not keep their virginity until they were given in marriage. With these torments they will be punished, and they will feel them.

Again, other men and women will bite their tongues without stopping and be tormented with everlasting fire. These are the servants — the slaves — who were not obedient to their masters. This, then, is their judgment forever.

Close by this place of torment will be men and women who are dumb and blind. Their clothing is white. They will crowd against one another and fall on coals of unquenchable fire. These are the ones who give alms and say: We are righteous before God — when in fact they have not sought after righteousness.

Ezrael, the angel of God, will bring them out of this fire and set a judgment of decision. This, then, is their judgment. A river of fire will flow, and all who are judged will be pulled down into the middle of the river. Uriel will set them there.

There are wheels of fire, and men and women are hung on them by the force of their turning. And those who are in the pit will be burned. Now these are the sorcerers and sorceresses. Those wheels will be in every decision — judgment, punishment — by fire without number.

After that, the angels will bring my elect and righteous ones, who are perfect in all uprightness. They will carry them in their hands and clothe them with the clothing of the life that is above. They will see what they desire happen to those who hated them, when he punishes them. The torment of each one will be forever, each according to his works.

All those in torment will say with one voice: Have mercy on us. For now we know the judgment of God, which he told us beforehand, and we did not believe. And the angel Tatirokos — Tartaruchus, keeper of hell — will come and punish them with still greater torment and say to them: Now you repent, when it is no longer the time for repentance and nothing of life is left. And they will say: Righteous is the judgment of God. For we have heard and understood that his judgment is good. For we are being paid back according to our deeds.

Then I will give to my elect and righteous ones the washing — the baptism — and the salvation they have begged me for, in the field of Akrosja — Acherousia, a lake in other writings, for example the Apocalypse of Moses, where the soul of Adam is washed in it; see also Paul 22, 23 — which is called Aneslasleja — Elysium. They will decorate the portion of the righteous with flowers, and I will go . . . I will rejoice with them. I will make the peoples enter my everlasting kingdom and show them that eternal thing — life? — on which I have made them set their hope, even I and my Father who is in heaven.

I have spoken this to you, Peter, and told it to you. Go out, then, and go to the land — or city — of the west. (The next sentences are left out by one translator as unintelligible. Others render them like this: and enter the vineyard I will tell you about, so that through the sickness — the sufferings — of the Son who is without sin, the deeds of corruption may be made holy. As for you, you are chosen according to the promise I have given you. So spread my gospel through all the world in peace. Truly, men will rejoice. My words will be a spring of hope and life, and suddenly the world will be snatched away.)

Now comes the section about paradise, which in the Akhmim copy stands before the part about hell.

Gr. 4 My Lord Jesus Christ our King said to me: Let us go to the holy mountain. His disciples went with him, praying.

Gr. 6 And look, there were two men there. We could not look at their faces. A light came from them, shining more than the sun. Their clothing was also shining and cannot be described. Nothing in this world is enough to be compared to them. And their sweetness . . . no mouth can speak the beauty of their appearance. For their look was astonishing and wonderful. And the other — I mean the great one — (probably: and, in a word, I cannot describe it) shines in his face more than crystal.

Gr. 8, 9 Like the flower of roses is the look of the colour of his face and of his body . . . his head (another copy: their head) was a marvel. And on his — their — shoulders (something about their hair has plainly fallen out)

Gr. 10 and on their foreheads was a crown of spikenard woven from fair flowers. As the rainbow in the water, so was their hair. Such was the beauty of their faces, adorned with every kind of ornament.

Gr. 11 And when we saw them suddenly, we marvelled.

Gr. 12 And I came near the Lord — God — Jesus Christ and said to him: O my Lord, who are these?

Gr. 13 And he said to me: They are Moses and Elijah.

Gr. 14 And I said to him: Where then are Abraham and Isaac and Jacob and the rest of the righteous fathers?

Gr. 15 And he showed us a great garden, open, full of fair trees and blessed fruits and the smell of perfumes. Its fragrance was pleasant and reached even to us.

Gr. 16 And of it — or, of that tree — . . . I saw much fruit.

Gr. 20 And my Lord and God Jesus Christ said to me: Have you seen the companies of the fathers? As their rest is, so also is the honour and glory of those who are persecuted for the sake of my righteousness. And I rejoiced and believed and understood what is written in the book of my Lord Jesus Christ. And I said to him: O my Lord, do you want me to make three shelters here, one for you, one for Moses, and one for Elijah? And he said to me in anger: Satan is making war on you and has covered your understanding, and the good things of this world get the better of you. So your eyes must be opened and your ears unstopped, so that you may see a shelter not made with men's hands, which my heavenly Father has made for me and for the elect. And we looked at it and were full of gladness.

And look, suddenly there came a voice from heaven, saying: This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. He has kept my commandments. And then a great, exceedingly white cloud came over our heads and carried away our Lord and Moses and Elijah. And I shook and was afraid. We looked up. Heaven opened. We saw men in the flesh. They came and greeted our Lord and Moses and Elijah and went into another heaven. And the word of Scripture was fulfilled: This is the generation that seeks him and seeks the face of the God of Jacob. And there was great fear and stirring in heaven, and the angels pressed against one another so that the word of Scripture might be fulfilled which says: Open the gates, you princes.

After that, heaven, which had been open, was shut.

And we prayed and went down from the mountain, praising God, who has written the names of the righteous in heaven, in the book of life.
