Prologue — Title and Blessing

§1. The Testament of Solomon, son of David, king in Jerusalem. He ruled and mastered all the spirits of the air, on the earth, and under the earth. By their help he did all the great works of the Temple. This book also tells what power each demon has over men, and by what angel each demon is stopped and brought to nothing.

Of Solomon the wise.

Blessed are you, Lord God, who gave Solomon this authority. Glory and power be yours for ever. Amen.

How the Ring Was Given

§2. When the Temple in the city of Jerusalem was being built and the workmen were at their work, the demon Ornias came among them at sunset. He took half the pay of the chief workman's little boy, and half his food. Every day he also sucked the thumb of the boy's right hand. The boy grew thin, though the king loved him very much.

§3. One day King Solomon called the boy and asked him, "Do I not love you more than all the workmen in the Temple of God? Do I not give you double pay and double food? Why then do you grow thinner day by day and hour by hour?"

§4. The boy said to the king, "I beg you, king, hear what has happened to your child. When we are let go from work on the Temple of God, after sunset, when I lie down to rest, one of the evil demons comes and takes half my pay and half my food from me. Then he takes my right hand and sucks my thumb. My soul is crushed, and so my body grows thinner every day."

§5. When I, Solomon, heard this, I went into the Temple of God and prayed with all my soul, night and day, that the demon might be put into my hands and that I might have power over him. Through my prayer grace was given to me from the Lord Sabaoth by Michael his archangel. Michael brought me a small ring. It had a seal made of a cut stone, and he said to me, "Take this, Solomon, king, son of David. It is the gift the Lord God, the Most High Sabaoth, has sent you. With it you shall shut up all the demons of the earth, male and female, and with their help you shall build up Jerusalem. You must wear this seal of God. The cutting on the seal of the ring sent to you is a Pentalpha."

§6. I, Solomon, was full of joy, and I praised and glorified the God of heaven and earth. The next morning I called the boy, gave him the ring, and said to him, "Take this. At the hour when the demon comes to you, throw this ring at the demon's chest and say to him, 'In the name of God, King Solomon calls you here.' Then run to me at once. Do not be afraid of anything you hear from the demon, and do not doubt."

§7. The boy took the ring and went away. At the usual hour Ornias the fierce demon came like a burning fire to take the pay from the child. But the child did as the king had told him. He threw the ring at the demon's chest and said, "King Solomon calls you here." Then he ran off to the king. The demon cried out loud, "Child, why have you done this to me? Take the ring off me, and I will give you the gold of the earth. Only take this off me, and do not lead me away to Solomon."

§8. But the child said to the demon, "As the Lord God of Israel lives, I will not listen to you. Come here." The child came running, glad, to the king and said, "I have brought the demon, king, as you commanded me, my master. Look, he stands before the gates of the court of your palace, crying out and begging with a loud voice. He offers me the silver and gold of the earth if I will only not bring him to you."

§9. When Solomon heard this, he rose from his throne and went out into the court of his palace. There he saw the demon shaking and trembling. He said to him, "Who are you?" The demon answered, "I am called Ornias."

The Catalogue of Demons

What follows is the catalogue. Each demon is brought before Solomon, gives his name, his form, what he does to men, the star he belongs to, and the angel who stops him. Solomon then seals each one and sets him to work on the Temple, or shuts him up.

§10 Ornias — in the shape of a man, coming like burning fire at sunset; he steals half the pay and half the food of workmen and sucks the thumb of the right hand until his victim wastes away, and he takes the shape of a fair woman to play with men in sleep, and also the shape of a lion; thwarted by Uriel, the archangel, the power of God

Solomon said to him, "Tell me, demon, to what sign of the zodiac do you belong?" He answered, "To the Water-pourer. Those on earth who burn with desire for noble virgins — them I strangle. When there is no sleep, I change into three forms. When men fall in love with women, I change myself into a fair woman. I take hold of the men in their sleep and play with them. After a while I take my wings again and go to the heavenly places. I also appear as a lion, and all the demons are under my command. I am the offspring of the archangel Uriel, the power of God."

§11. When I, Solomon, heard the name of the archangel, I prayed and glorified God, the Lord of heaven and earth. I sealed the demon and set him to cut stones for the Temple — the stones that lay along the shore and had been brought by the Sea of Arabia. But he was afraid of iron, and he went on and said to me, "I beg you, King Solomon, let me go free, and I will bring you all the demons." He was not willing to obey me, so I prayed to the archangel Uriel to come and help me, and at once I saw the archangel Uriel coming down to me from the heavens.

§12. The angel told the whales of the sea to come out of the deep. He cast his lot upon the ground, and that lot made the great demon obey him. He commanded the great and bold demon Ornias to cut stones at the Temple. So I, Solomon, glorified the God of heaven and Maker of the earth. I told Ornias to come with his lot, and I gave him the seal and said, "Go, and bring me here the prince of all the demons."

§13 Beelzeboul, prince of the demons — the exarch, first angel of the first heaven before his fall; he destroys kings, joins himself to foreign tyrants, sets his demons on men so they trust them and are lost, and stirs even God's chosen servants to evil desire, evil teachings, and lawless deeds, to envy, murder, war, and sodomy; thwarted by the holy name of Almighty God whose number is 644, which in Greek is Emmanuel, and by the great name of the power Eleëth

§13. Ornias took the finger-ring and went to Beelzeboul, who has kingship over the demons. He said to him, "Come here. Solomon calls you." Beelzeboul heard this and said to him, "Tell me, who is this Solomon you speak of?" Then Ornias threw the ring at Beelzeboul's chest and said, "Solomon the king calls you." Beelzeboul cried out with a mighty voice and shot out a great burning flame of fire. He rose up, followed Ornias, and came to Solomon.

§14. When I saw the prince of demons, I glorified the Lord God, Maker of heaven and earth, and said, "Blessed are you, Lord God Almighty, who have given to Solomon your servant wisdom, the companion of the wise, and have put all the power of the devil under me."

§15. I questioned him and said, "Who are you?" The demon replied, "I am Beelzeboul, the exarch of the demons. All the demons have their chief seats close to me. I am the one who makes each demon appear." He promised to bring all the unclean spirits to me in bonds. I again glorified the God of heaven and earth, as I always give thanks to him.

§16. Then I asked the demon if there were females among them. When he told me there were, I said I wished to see them. Beelzeboul went off fast and brought to me Onoskelis, who had a very pretty shape and the skin of a fair woman, and she tossed her head.

§17 Onoskelis — a fair woman with a mule's leg, lurking in a golden cave, now here, now there; she strangles men with a noose, creeps from below up to the arms, dwells most in cliffs, caves, and ravines, and lies with men as a woman, above all with dark-skinned men who worship her star; thwarted by the angel Joel

§17. When she had come, I said to her, "Tell me, who are you?" She said to me, "I am called Onoskelis, a spirit worked into shape, lurking on the earth. There is a golden cave where I lie. But I have a place that always shifts. At one time I strangle men with a noose. At another time I creep up from below to the arms. My most common dwelling-places are cliffs, caves, and ravines. Often I lie with men in the shape of a woman, and above all with those of dark skin. For they share my star with me, since they are the ones who secretly or openly worship my star, not knowing that they harm themselves and only sharpen my hunger for more evil. They wish to get money by remembrance, but I give a little to those who worship me fairly."

§18. I, Solomon, asked her about her birth, and she replied, "I was born of an untimely voice — the so-called echo of a man's dung dropped in a wood."

§19. I said to her, "Under what star do you pass?" She answered me, "Under the star of the full moon, because the moon travels over most things." Then I said to her, "And what angel stops you?" She said to me, "He who reigns in you." I thought she mocked me and told a soldier to strike her. But she cried out loud and said, "I am made subject to you, king, by the wisdom of God given to you, and by the angel Joel."

§20. So I commanded her to spin the hemp for the ropes used in building the house of God. When I had sealed and bound her, she was so overcome and brought to nothing that she stood night and day spinning the hemp.

§21 Asmodeus — born of an angel's seed by a daughter of man, his star bright in heaven, called by some the Wain and by others the dragon's child; he plots against the newly married so husband and wife do not come together, wastes the beauty of virgins and turns their hearts cold, and drives married men mad with desire for other men's wives until they sin and fall into murder; thwarted by Raphael the archangel who stands before the throne of God, and driven off by the liver and gall of the sheatfish smoked over tamarisk ash

§21. At once I told them to lead another demon to me. The demon Asmodeus came near, bound, and I asked him, "Who are you?" He shot a look of anger and rage at me and said, "And who are you?" I said to him, "Punished as you are, do you answer me so?" With rage he said to me, "How shall I answer you? You are a son of man. I was born of an angel's seed by a daughter of man, so no word of our heavenly kind spoken to the earth-born can be too proud. My star is bright in heaven. Men call it, some the Wain, and some the dragon's child. I keep close to this star. Do not ask me many things. Your kingdom also, after a little time, will be broken up, and your glory is only for a season. Your rule over us will be short. Then we shall again range free over mankind, so that they will honour us as if we were gods, not knowing, men that they are, the names of the angels set over us."

§22. When I, Solomon, heard this, I bound him more tightly and ordered him to be beaten with ox-hide thongs and to tell me humbly his name and his work. He answered me, "I am called Asmodeus among men. My work is to plot against the newly married, so that they do not know one another. I split them apart by many troubles. I waste away the beauty of virgin women and make their hearts strange."

§23. I said to him, "Is this your only work?" He answered, "I throw men into fits of madness and desire when they have wives of their own, so that they leave them and go night and day to women who belong to other men. In the end they commit sin and fall into acts of murder."

§24. I bound him by the name of the Lord Sabaoth and said, "Fear God, Asmodeus, and tell me by what angel you are stopped." He said, "By Raphael, the archangel who stands before the throne of God. The liver and gall of a fish also put me to flight, when smoked over the ashes of the tamarisk." I asked him again, "Hide nothing from me. I am Solomon, son of David, King of Israel. Tell me the name of the fish you fear." He answered, "It is called Glanos, and it is found in the rivers of Assyria. That is why I roam about in those parts."

§25. I said to him, "Is there nothing else about you, Asmodeus?" He answered, "The power of God knows, who has bound me with the unbreakable bonds of that one's seal, that all I have told you is true. I beg you, King Solomon, do not condemn me to go into water." But I smiled and said to him, "As the Lord God of my fathers lives, I will lay iron on you to wear. You shall also make the clay for the whole building of the Temple, treading it down with your feet." I ordered them to give him ten water-jars to carry water in. The demon groaned terribly and did the work I ordered him to do. I did this because that fierce demon Asmodeus knew even the future. I, Solomon, glorified God, who gave wisdom to me, Solomon his servant. I hung the liver of the fish and its gall on the spike of a reed and burned it over Asmodeus, because he was so strong, and so his unbearable malice was stopped.

§26. I called Beelzeboul, the prince of demons, to stand before me again. I set him on a raised seat of honour and said to him, "Why are you alone, prince of the demons?" He said to me, "Because I alone am left of the angels of heaven who came down. I was the first angel in the first heaven and was called Beelzeboul. Now I control all those who are bound in Tartarus. I also have a child, and he haunts the Red Sea. Whenever the right time comes he comes up to me again. He obeys me and tells me what he has done, and I support him."

§27. I, Solomon, said to him, "Beelzeboul, what is your work?" He answered me, "I destroy kings. I join myself with foreign tyrants. I set my own demons on men, so that men believe in them and are lost. God's chosen servants, priests and faithful men, I stir up to desire for wicked sins, evil teachings, and lawless deeds. They obey me, and I carry them on to destruction. I put envy into men, and desire for murder, and for wars and sodomy, and other evil things. I will destroy the world."

§28. I said to him, "Bring me your child, who, as you say, is in the Red Sea." He said to me, "I will not bring him to you. But another demon will come to me, called Ephippas. Him I will bind, and he will bring him up from the deep to me." I said to him, "How does your son come to be in the depth of the sea, and what is his name?" He answered me, "Do not ask me, for you cannot learn it from me. He will come to you by my command and will tell you openly."

§29. I said to him, "Tell me by what angel you are stopped." He answered, "By the holy and precious name of Almighty God, called by the Hebrews by a row of numbers whose sum is 644, and among the Greeks it is Emmanuel. If one of the Romans swears at me by the great name of the power Eleëth, I vanish at once."

§30. I, Solomon, was astonished when I heard this. I ordered him to saw up Theban marbles. When he began to saw the marbles, the other demons cried out with a loud voice, howling because of their king Beelzeboul.

§31. I, Solomon, questioned him and said, "If you would gain a rest, tell me about the things in heaven." Beelzeboul said, "Hear, king. If you burn gum and incense and bulbs of the sea with nard and saffron, and light seven lamps in an earthquake, you will firmly fix your house. If, being pure, you light them at dawn in the sunlight, then you will see the heavenly dragons, how they wind themselves along and drag the chariot of the sun."

§32. When I, Solomon, had heard this, I rebuked him and said, "Be silent for now, and go on sawing the marbles as I commanded you." I, Solomon, praised God and commanded another demon to present himself to me. One came before me who carried his face high up in the air, but the rest of the spirit curled away like a snail. It broke through the few soldiers and raised a terrible dust on the ground and carried it upwards, then hurled it back down to frighten us, and asked what questions I could ask as a rule. I stood up and spat on the ground in that spot and sealed it with the ring of God. At once the dust-wind stopped. Then I asked him, "Who are you, wind?" He shook up dust once more and answered me, "What would you have, King Solomon?" I answered him, "Tell me what you are called. I would ask you a question. So far I give thanks to God, who has made me wise to answer their evil plots."

§33 Tephras, spirit of the ashes — a dust-wind that lifts his face high while the rest curls like a snail; he brings darkness on men, sets fire to fields, ruins homesteads, is most busy in summer, and creeps into corners of walls by night and day, and he also holds back the fits of the half-tertian fever; thwarted by the archangel Azael

§33. The demon answered me, "I am the spirit of the ashes, Tephras." I said to him, "What is your work?" He said, "I bring darkness on men and set fire to fields, and I bring homesteads to nothing. I am most busy in summer. When I get a chance, I creep into corners of the wall by night and day. I am the offspring of the great one, and nothing less." I said to him, "Under what star do you lie?" He answered, "In the very tip of the moon's horn, when it is found in the south. There is my star. I have been told to hold back the fits of the half-tertian fever, and this is why many men pray to the half-tertian fever, using these three names: Bultala, Thallal, Melchal. And I heal them." I said to him, "I am Solomon. When you would do harm, by whose help do you do it?" He said to me, "By the help of the angel by whom the third day's fever is also lulled to rest." I questioned him, "And by what name?" He answered, "That of the archangel Azael." I called the archangel Azael and set a seal on the demon and commanded him to seize great stones and toss them up to the workmen on the higher parts of the Temple. Being forced, the demon began to do what he was told to do.

§34. I glorified God afresh, who gave me this authority, and ordered another demon to come before me. There came seven spirits, females, bound and woven together, fair in appearance and comely. I, Solomon, seeing them, questioned them and said, "Who are you?" With one accord they said with one voice, "We are of the thirty-six elements of the ruler of the world of darkness." The first said, "I am Deception." The second, "I am Strife." The third, "I am Klothod, which is battle." The fourth, "I am Jealousy." The fifth, "I am Power." The sixth, "I am Error." The seventh, "I am the worst of all. Our stars are in heaven — seven stars, low in brightness, and all together. We are called as it were goddesses. We change our place all together and live together, sometimes in Lydia, sometimes on Olympus, sometimes on a great mountain."

§35. I, Solomon, questioned them one by one, beginning with the first and going down to the seventh.

§35 Deception, first of the seven sisters — fair female spirit bound with her sisters; she deceives and weaves snares here and there and sharpens and stirs up false teachings; thwarted by Lamechalal

§35. The first said, "I am Deception. I deceive and weave snares here and there. I sharpen and stir up false teachings. But I have an angel who stops me, Lamechalal."

§36 Strife, second of the seven sisters — fair female spirit bound with her sisters; she brings timbers, stones, and hangers, her weapons, onto the spot and makes strife upon strife; thwarted by Baruchiachil

§36. The second said, "I am Strife, strife of strifes. I bring timbers, stones, hangers — my weapons — onto the spot. But I have an angel who stops me, Baruchiachil."

§37 Klothod, third of the seven sisters — fair female spirit bound with her sisters, called Battle; she makes well-behaved men scatter and fall foul of one another; thwarted by Marmamth

§37. The third said, "I am called Klothod, which is battle, and I make well-behaved men scatter and fall foul of one another. Why do I say so much? I have an angel who stops me, Marmamth."

§38 Jealousy, fourth of the seven sisters — fair female spirit bound with her sisters; she makes men forget sobriety and good sense, splits them into parties, tears husband from wife, children from parents, and brothers from sisters, with Strife at her side; thwarted by the great Balthial

§38. The fourth said, "I make men forget their sobriety and good sense. I part them and split them into parties, for Strife follows me hand in hand. I tear the husband from the sharer of his bed, and children from parents, and brothers from sisters. Why tell so much against myself? I have an angel who stops me, the great Balthial."

§39 Power, fifth of the seven sisters — fair female spirit bound with her sisters; by power she raises up tyrants and tears down kings and gives power to all rebels; thwarted by Asteradth

§39. The fifth said, "I am Power. By power I raise up tyrants and tear down kings. To all rebels I give power. I have an angel who stops me, Asteradth."

§40 Error, sixth of the seven sisters — fair female spirit bound with her sisters; she leads men into error, into prying into graves, teaches those who dig there, and leads wandering souls away from all godliness; thwarted by Uriel

§40. The sixth said, "I am Error, King Solomon. I will make you err, as I made you err before when I made you kill your own brother. I will lead you into error, so as to pry into graves. I teach them that dig, and I lead wandering souls away from all godliness, and many other evil traits are mine. But I have an angel who stops me, Uriel."

§41 The Worst, seventh of the seven sisters — fair female spirit bound with her sisters, the worst of all; she makes men worse than they were and lays the bonds of Artemis on them, and the locust shall set her free; thwarted by no angel named here — she says the wise man will not turn his steps toward her

§41. The seventh said, "I am the worst, and I make you worse off than you were, because I will lay the bonds of Artemis on you. But the locust will set me free, for by means of it it is fated that you shall gain my desire. If a man were wise, he would not turn his steps toward me."

§42. When I, Solomon, had heard this and wondered, I sealed them with my ring. Since they were so mighty, I told them to dig the foundations of the Temple of God. The length of it was 250 cubits. I told them to be busy at their work, and with one murmur of joint protest they began to do the tasks laid on them.

§43 Envy, the headless demon — a man in all his limbs but without a head, wholly voice, having taken the voices of many men; he longs to devour heads to get a head of his own, smashes the heads of dumb children on the eighth day, glides in by a child's voice at night, seizes a man's head at the crossways and cuts it off as with a sword to put it on himself, and sends grave injuries and incurable sores on men's feet; thwarted by the fiery flash of lightning

§43. I glorified the Lord and told them to bring another demon before me. There was brought to me a demon who had all the limbs of a man but no head. Seeing him, I said to him, "Tell me, who are you?" He answered, "I am a demon." I said to him, "Which one?" He answered me, "I am called Envy. I love to devour heads. I long to get a head for myself. I do not eat enough, but I am eager to have such a head as you have."

§44. When I, Solomon, heard this, I sealed him, stretching out my hand against his chest. The demon leapt up and threw himself down and groaned, saying, "Woe is me! Where have I come to? O traitor Ornias, I cannot see!" I said to him, "I am Solomon. Tell me then, how do you manage to see?" He answered me, "By my feelings." I, Solomon, having heard his voice come up to me, asked him how he managed to speak. He answered me, "I, King Solomon, am wholly voice, for I have taken the voices of many men. In the case of all men who are called dumb, I am the one who smashed their heads when they were children and had reached their eighth day. When a child is crying in the night, I become a spirit and glide in by means of his voice. At the crossways also I have many services to do, and to meet me brings harm. I seize a man's head in an instant and with my hands, as with a sword, I cut it off and put it on myself. In this way, by means of the fire that is in me, it is swallowed up through my neck. I am the one who sends grave injuries that cannot be healed on men's feet, and I put sores on them."

§45. I, Solomon, on hearing this, said to him, "Tell me how you send forth the fire. From what sources do you send it out?" The spirit said to me, "From the Day-star. Here Elburion has not yet been found, to whom men offer prayers and kindle lights. His name is called on by the seven demons before me, and he cherishes them."

§46. I said to him, "Tell me his name." He answered, "I cannot tell you. If I tell his name, I make myself incurable. But he will come in answer to his name." On hearing this, I, Solomon, said to him, "Tell me then, by what angel are you stopped?" He answered, "By the fiery flash of lightning." I bowed myself before the Lord God of Israel and told him to stay in the keeping of Beelzeboul until Iax should come.

§47 Rhabdos, the Staff — a huge hound who was once a man of great learning who did many unholy deeds and could hold back the stars of heaven; he harms men who follow his star and seizes frenzied men by the throat and destroys them, and he knows where a green stone lies that can adorn the Temple; thwarted by the great Brieus

§47. Then I ordered another demon to come before me. There came into my presence a hound of very large shape. It spoke with a loud voice and said, "Hail, Lord, King Solomon!" I, Solomon, was astonished. I said to it, "Who are you, hound?" It answered, "I do seem to you to be a hound, but before you were, King Solomon, I was a man who did many unholy deeds on earth. I was very learned in letters and so mighty that I could hold the stars of heaven back. I prepared many divine works. I do harm to men who follow after our star and turn them aside. I seize frenzied men by the throat and so destroy them."

§48. I, Solomon, said to him, "What is your name?" He answered, "Staff — Rhabdos." I said to him, "What is your work? What can you do?" He replied, "Give me your man, and I will lead him away into a mountain spot and will show him a green stone, tossed to and fro, with which you may adorn the Temple of the Lord God."

§49. When I, Solomon, heard this, I ordered my servant to go with him and to take the finger-ring with the seal of God with him. I said to him, "Whoever shows you the green stone, seal him with this finger-ring. Mark the spot with care, and bring the demon here to me." The demon showed him the green stone. He sealed it and brought the demon to me. I, Solomon, decided to bind with my seal the two — the headless demon, and likewise the huge hound — on my right hand. I told the hound to keep safe the fiery spirit, so that by day and night lamps might cast their light through its mouth on the workmen at work.

§50. I, Solomon, took from the mine of that stone 200 shekels for the supports of the table of incense, which was like it in appearance. I, Solomon, glorified the Lord God, and then I closed round the treasure of that stone. I ordered the demons afresh to cut marble for the building of the house of God. I, Solomon, prayed to the Lord and asked the hound, "By what angel are you stopped?" The demon replied, "By the great Brieus."

§51 Leophoros, the lion-bearer — in the form of a roaring lion, a spirit that cannot be clearly seen; he leaps unseen on men lying sick and makes them weak and enfeebled, he casts out demons and has legions under his control; thwarted by Emmanuel, the great among men, whose number is 644, who is to suffer much at men's hands and will plunge him and his legions from the steep into the water

§51. I praised the Lord God of heaven and earth and told them to bring another demon forward to me. There came before me one in the form of a roaring lion. He stood and answered me, "King, in the form I have, I am a spirit that cannot be plainly seen. On all men who lie low with sickness I leap, coming unseen, and I make the man weak, so that his body is enfeebled. I have also another glory, king. I cast out demons, and I have legions under my control. I can be received in my dwelling-places along with all the demons of the legions under me." I, Solomon, on hearing this, asked him, "What is your name?" He answered, "Lion-bearer, Rath in kind." I said to him, "How are you to be stopped, along with your legions? What angel stops you?" He answered, "If I tell you my name, I bind not myself alone, but also the legion of demons under me."

§52. I said to him, "I bind you in the name of the God Sabaoth to tell me by what name you are stopped, along with your host." The spirit answered me, "The 'great among men,' who is to suffer many things at the hands of men, whose name is the figure 644, which is Emmanuel — he is the one who has bound us, and who will then come and plunge us from the steep under water. He is made known in the three letters that bring him down."

§53. When I, Solomon, heard this, I glorified God. I condemned his legion to carry wood from the thicket. I condemned the lion-shaped one himself to saw up the wood small with his teeth, for burning in the fire that is never quenched, for the Temple of God.

§54 Koryphē, crest of dragons — a three-headed dragon of fearful colour, like a three-spiked caltrop; with one head he blinds children in the womb and twists their ears and makes them deaf and mute, with another he slips in, and with the third he strikes men in the limbless part of the body and makes them fall down, foam, and grind their teeth; thwarted by the angel of great counsel who shall dwell openly on the cross at the place of the head, Golgotha

§54. I worshipped the Lord God of Israel and told them to bring another demon forward. There came before me a dragon with three heads, of fearful colour. I questioned him, "Who are you?" He answered me, "I am a caltrop-like spirit, whose work runs in three lines. I blind children in women's wombs and twist their ears round. I make them deaf and mute. With my third head I have means of slipping in. I strike men in the limbless part of the body and make them fall down and foam and grind their teeth. But I have my own way of being stopped. Jerusalem is shown in writing, to the place called 'of the head.' There the angel of great counsel is appointed beforehand, and now he will dwell openly on the cross. He stops me, and to him I am subject."

§55. "But in the place where you sit, King Solomon, there stands a column in the air, of purple. The demon called Ephippas has brought it up from the Red Sea, from inner Arabia. He is the one who shall be shut up in a skin-bottle and brought before you. At the entrance of the Temple, which you have begun to build, King Solomon, there lies stored much gold. Dig it up and carry it off." I, Solomon, sent my servant and found it was just as the demon told me. I sealed him with my ring and praised the Lord God.

§56. I said to him, "What are you called?" The demon said, "I am the crest of dragons." I told him to make bricks in the Temple. He had human hands.

§57 Obizuth — a woman's spirit, all head and no limbs, with wild hair like a dragon's, her body in darkness but her look bright and green, who sleeps not by night; she goes round the world visiting women in childbirth, and if she finds the hour she strangles the child, and she destroys children and makes their ears deaf, harms their eyes, binds their mouths, ruins their minds, and pains their bodies; thwarted by the angel of God called Apharōph, which is Raphael, whose name, if a man knows it and writes it on a woman in childbirth, keeps her out — the number of the name is 640

§57. I adored the Lord God of Israel and told another demon to present himself. There came before me a spirit in woman's form who had a head without any limbs, and her hair was loose and wild. I said to her, "Who are you?" She answered, "No — who are you? Why do you want to hear about me? Since you would learn, here I stand bound before your face. Go into your royal storehouses and wash your hands. Then sit down afresh before your seat of judgment and ask me questions, and you shall learn, king, who I am."

§58. I, Solomon, did as she told me and held myself back because of the wisdom dwelling in me, so that I might hear of her deeds and blame them and make them known to men. I sat down and said to the demon, "Who are you?" She said, "I am called among men Obizuth. By night I do not sleep, but go my rounds over all the world and visit women in childbirth. Watching for the hour, I take my stand. If I am lucky, I strangle the child. If not, I go to another place. I cannot for a single night go away without success. I am a fierce spirit, of many names and many shapes. Now here, now there I roam. To the western parts I go my rounds. As it now is, though you have sealed me round with the ring of God, you have done nothing. I am not standing before you, and you will not be able to command me. I have no work other than the destruction of children, and making their ears deaf, and working evil to their eyes, and binding their mouths with a bond, and ruining their minds, and paining their bodies."

§59. When I, Solomon, heard this, I wondered at her appearance, for I saw all her body was in darkness. Her look was altogether bright and green, and her hair was tossed wildly like a dragon's, and all her limbs were unseen. Her voice was very clear as it came to me. I said cunningly, "Tell me by what angel you are stopped, evil spirit." She answered me, "By the angel of God called Apharōph, which is explained as Raphael, by whom I am stopped now and for all time. If any man knows his name and writes it on a woman in childbirth, then I shall not be able to enter her. The number of this name is 640." When I, Solomon, had heard this and had glorified the Lord, I ordered her hair to be bound and her to be hung up in front of the Temple of God, so that all the children of Israel, as they passed, might see her and glorify the Lord God of Israel, who had given me this authority, with wisdom and power from God, by means of this signet.

§60 The winged dragon — rolling in like a dragon but with the face and hands of a man, all its other limbs a dragon's and wings on its back; he lies only with a few fair women who bear the name of his star, and the child born becomes Eros, and he breathes out fire that burns the wood gathered for the Temple; thwarted by the great angel who sits in the second heaven, called in Hebrew Bazazath

§60. I again ordered another demon to come before me. There came one rolling itself along, in appearance like a dragon, but having the face and hands of a man. All its limbs except its feet were those of a dragon, and it had wings on its back. When I saw it, I was astonished and said, "Who are you, demon, and what are you called? Where have you come from? Tell me."

§61. The spirit answered, "This is the first time I have stood before you, King Solomon. I am a spirit made into a god among men, but now brought to nothing by the ring and wisdom given to you by God. I am the so-called winged dragon. I lie not with many women, but only with a few who are of fair shape and bear the name of this star. I lie with them in the shape of a winged spirit. She on whom I have leapt grows heavy with child, and what is born of her becomes Eros. Since such offspring cannot be carried by men, the woman breaks wind. Such is my work. Only let me be satisfied, and all the other demons troubled and disturbed by you will speak the whole truth. But those made of fire will burn up with fire the wood gathered by them for the building in the Temple."

§62. As the demon said this, I saw the spirit going out of his mouth. It ate up the wood of the frankincense-tree and burned up all the logs we had placed in the Temple of God. I, Solomon, saw what the spirit had done and wondered.

§63. Having glorified God, I asked the dragon-shaped demon, "Tell me, by what angel are you stopped?" He answered, "By the great angel who has its seat in the second heaven, called in Hebrew Bazazath." When I, Solomon, had heard this and had called on his angel, I condemned him to saw up marbles for the building of the Temple of God. I praised God and commanded another demon to come before me.

§64 Enēpsigos — a woman with two extra heads on her shoulders, each with hands, a spirit of a thousand names who changes shape like the goddess she is called, dwelling in the moon and called Kronos by the wise; she prophesies that Solomon's kingdom and Temple will be broken by Persians, Medes, and Chaldaeans, the vessels broken and the demons let loose over the world until the Son of God is stretched on the cross; thwarted by the angel Rathanael, who sits in the third heaven

§64. There came before my face another spirit, a woman in the form she had, but on her shoulders she had two other heads with hands. I asked her, "Tell me, who are you?" She said to me, "I am Enēpsigos, who also have a thousand names." I said to her, "By what angel are you stopped?" She said to me, "What do you seek? What do you ask? I go through changes, like the goddess I am called. I change again and pass into another shape. Do not wish to know all that concerns me. Since you are before me for this much, listen. I have my dwelling in the moon, and for that reason I have three forms. At times I am called on by magic by the wise as Kronos. At other times, with those who bring me down, I come down and appear in another shape. The measure of the element cannot be explained or defined and cannot be stopped. Changing into these three forms, I come down and become such as you see me. I am stopped by the angel Rathanael, who sits in the third heaven. This is why I speak to you. That Temple cannot hold me."

§65. I, Solomon, therefore prayed to my God and called on the angel of whom Enēpsigos spoke to me, and used my seal. I sealed her with a triple chain and put the fastening of the chain beneath her. I used the seal of God, and the spirit prophesied to me, saying, "This is what you, King Solomon, do to us. After a time your kingdom shall be broken, and in due season this Temple shall be torn apart, and all Jerusalem shall be undone by the king of the Persians and Medes and Chaldaeans. The vessels of this Temple, which you make, shall be put to low uses for the gods. With them all the jars in which you shut us up shall be broken by the hands of men. Then we shall go out in great power here and there and be scattered all over the world. We shall lead the inhabited world astray for a long season, until the Son of God is stretched upon the cross. Never before does a king arise like him, one who stops us all, whose mother shall not have contact with man. Who else can receive such authority over spirits, except he whom the first devil will seek to tempt but will not prevail over? The number of his name is 644, which is Emmanuel. So, King Solomon, your time is evil, and your years are short and evil, and to your servant shall your kingdom be given."

§66. When I, Solomon, had heard this, I glorified God. Though I wondered at the defence of the demons, I did not believe it until it came true. I did not believe their words. But when they were fulfilled, then I understood, and at my death I wrote this Testament to the children of Israel and gave it to them, so that they might know the powers of the demons and their shapes, and the names of their angels, by which these angels stop them. I glorified the Lord God of Israel and commanded the spirit to be bound with bonds that cannot be broken.

§67 Kynopēgos, the sea-demon — in front a horse, behind a fish, with a mighty voice; a fierce spirit of the sea greedy for gold and silver who rounds himself into a wave, throws himself on ships, whirls men round and hurls them out of the sea, and who also rises into men and sends out sickness; thwarted by Iameth

§67. Having praised God, I commanded another spirit to come before me. There came before my face another demon. In front he had the shape of a horse, but behind, of a fish. He had a mighty voice and said to me, "King Solomon, I am a fierce spirit of the sea. I am greedy for gold and silver. I am a spirit who rounds himself and comes over the wide waters of the sea and trips up the men who sail there. I round myself into a wave and change myself, then throw myself on ships and come right in on them. That is my work, and my way of getting hold of money and men. I take the men and whirl them round with myself and hurl the men out of the sea. I am not greedy for men's bodies, but I cast them up out of the sea so far. Since Beelzeboul, ruler of the spirits of the air and of those under the earth, and lord of earthly ones, shares kingship with us in the deeds of each one of us, I went up from the sea to get a sight in his company."

§68. "I also have another character and work. I change myself into waves and come up from the sea. I show myself to men, so that those on earth call me Kynospastos, because I take the human form. My name is a true one. By my passing up into men I send out a kind of sickness. I came to take counsel with the prince Beelzeboul, and he bound me and put me into your hands. I am here before you because of this seal, and you now torment me. In two or three days the spirit that talks with you will fail, because I shall have no water."

§69. I said to him, "Tell me by what angel you are stopped." He answered, "By Iameth." I glorified God. I commanded the spirit to be thrown into a jar with ten jugs of sea-water, of two measures each. I sealed them round above with marble and asphalt and pitch in the mouth of the jar. Having sealed it with my ring, I ordered it to be laid up in the Temple of God. I ordered another spirit to come before me.

§70 The giant-born spirit — a man's shape seen dimly, with gleaming eyes and a blade in his hand, born of a giant who died in the slaughter in the time of the giants; he sits beside men who pass among the tombs, takes the form of the dead, destroys whom he catches with his sword, or else makes him possessed and eat his own flesh and lose the hair of his chin; thwarted by him who is to become Saviour, a man whose number, if a man writes it on his forehead, routs him at once

§70. There came before my face another spirit in bonds, having dimly the form of a man, with gleaming eyes, and holding a blade in his hand. I asked, "Who are you?" He answered, "I am a lustful spirit, born of a giant man who died in the slaughter in the time of the giants." I said to him, "Tell me what you are set to do on earth, and where you dwell."

§71. He said, "My dwelling is in fruitful places, but my way of working is this. I sit beside men who pass along among the tombs, and out of season I take the form of the dead. If I catch anyone, I destroy him at once with my sword. If I cannot destroy him, I make him possessed by a demon and eat his own flesh, and the hair falls off his chin." I said to him, "Fear the God of heaven and earth, and tell me by what angel you are stopped." He answered, "He destroys me who is to become Saviour — a man whose number, if anyone writes it on his forehead, will defeat me, and in fear I shall quickly flee. If anyone writes this sign on him, I shall be in fear." When I, Solomon, heard this and had glorified the Lord God, I shut up this demon like the rest.

The Thirty-six World-rulers

§72. I commanded another demon to come before me. There came before my face thirty-six spirits. Their heads were shapeless like dogs, but in themselves they were human in form, with faces of asses, faces of oxen, and faces of birds. I, Solomon, hearing and seeing them, wondered and asked them, "Who are you?" With one accord and one voice they said, "We are the thirty-six elements, the world-rulers of this darkness. King Solomon, you will not wrong us nor shut us up nor lay command on us. The Lord God has given you authority over every spirit, in the air and on the earth and under the earth, so we also present ourselves before you like the other spirits — from Ram and Bull, from Twin and Crab, Lion and Virgin, Scales and Scorpion, Archer, Goat-horned, Water-pourer, and Fish."

§73. Then I, Solomon, called on the name of the Lord Sabaoth and questioned each in turn what his nature was. I told each one to come forward and tell of his deeds. The first came forward and said, "I am the first decan of the circle of the zodiac, and I am called Ram, and these two are with me." I put the question to them, "What are you called?"

Each of the thirty-six now speaks in turn. The pattern is the same: the demon gives his name, the pain or trouble he lays on the body, and the words a man must speak or write to make him flee.

Decan 1 — Rhyax (§73) — human in form with a dog-like head, first decan of the Ram; he makes men's heads idle and pains their foreheads and temples; thwarted by Michael — if he hears “Michael, shut up Rhyax,” he flees at once

“I, lord, am called Rhyax. I make the heads of men ache and shake their temples. If I only hear the words, ‘Michael, shut up Rhyax,’ I flee at once.”

Decan 2 — Barsaphael (§74) — human in form with a dog-like head; he lays half-head pain (migraine) on those who lie under his hour; thwarted by Gabriel — if he hears “Gabriel, shut up Barsaphael,” he flees at once

“I am called Barsaphael. I make those under my hour feel the pain of migraine. If I only hear, ‘Gabriel, shut up Barsaphael,’ I flee at once.”

Decan 3 — Aratosael (§75) — human in form with a dog-like head; he harms the eyes and hurts them badly; thwarted by Uriel — if he hears “Uriel, shut up Aratosael,” he flees at once

“I am called Aratosael. I harm eyes and hurt them badly. If I only hear, ‘Uriel, shut up Aratosael,’ I flee at once.”

Decan 4 — Oropel (§76a) — human in form with a dog-like head; he sends pains of the throat, quinsy, and rotting sores; thwarted by Raphael — if he hears “Raphael, shut up Oropel,” he flees at once

“I am called Oropel. I send throats and quinsy and rotting sores. If I hear, ‘Raphael, shut up Oropel,’ I flee at once.” [This decan stands in McCown's Greek (XVIII §8) but is missing from the text Conybeare translated, where the numbering jumps from the third to the fifth.]

Decan 5 — Kairoxanondalon, called also Iudal (§76) — human in form with a dog-like head; he blocks the ears and makes hearing deaf; thwarted by Uruel — if he hears “Uruel, shut up Kairoxanondalon” (Conybeare: “Uruel Iudal”), he flees at once

“I am called Kairoxanondalon [Conybeare's text reads Iudal]. I make blockages of the ears. If I hear, ‘Uruel, shut up Kairoxanondalon,’ I flee at once.”

Decan 6 — Sphendonael (§77) — human in form with a dog-like head; he makes swellings of the glands by the ear, inflamed tonsils, and the neck bent backward; thwarted by Sabrael — if he hears “Sabrael, shut up Sphendonael,” he flees at once

“I am called Sphendonael. I make tumours of the gland by the ear and swellings of the tonsils and the neck bent hard backward. If I hear, ‘Sabrael, shut up Sphendonael,’ I flee at once.”

Decan 7 — Sphandor (§78) — human in form with a dog-like head; he weakens the strength of the shoulders, makes them tremble, stills the nerves of the hands, and breaks and bruises the bones of the neck, and sucks out the marrow; thwarted by Araël — if he hears “Araël, shut up Sphandor,” he flees at once

“I am called Sphandor. I weaken the strength of the shoulders and make them tremble. I still the nerves of the hands and break and bruise the bones of the neck. I suck out the marrow. If I hear, ‘Araël, shut up Sphandor,’ I flee at once.”

Decan 8 — Belbel (§79) — human in form with a dog-like head; he twists the hearts and minds of men; thwarted by Araël — if he hears “Araël, shut up Belbel,” he flees at once

“I am called Belbel. I twist the hearts and minds of men. If I hear, ‘Araël, shut up Belbel,’ I flee at once.”

Decan 9 — Kourtael (§80) — human in form with a dog-like head; he sends griping pains in the bowels; thwarted by Iaoth — if he hears “Iaoth, shut up Kourtael,” he flees at once

“I am called Kourtael. I send colic in the bowels. I bring on pains. If I hear, ‘Iaoth, shut up Kourtael,’ I flee at once.”

Decan 10 — Metathiax (§81) — human in form with a dog-like head; he makes the loins (kidneys) ache; thwarted by Adonael — if he hears “Adonael, shut up Metathiax,” he flees at once

“I am called Metathiax. I make the loins ache. If I hear, ‘Adonael, shut up Metathiax,’ I flee at once.”

Decan 11 — Katanikotael (§82) — human in form with a dog-like head; he makes strife and wrongs in men's houses and sends hard temper; thwarted by the angels Iaë, Ieō, sons of Sabaoth — if a man writes “Iaë, Ieō, sons of Sabaoth, in the name of the great God shut up Katanikotael” on seven laurel leaves, washes the leaves in water, and sprinkles his house from inside to outside, he flees at once

“I am called Katanikotael. I make strife and wrongs in men's homes and send hard temper on them. If a man wants peace in his home, let him write on seven leaves of laurel the names of the angel who stops me, with these names: Iaë, Ieō, sons of Sabaoth — in the name of the great God let him shut up Katanikotael. Then let him wash the laurel leaves in water and sprinkle his house with the water, from within to the outside. At once I flee.”

Decan 12 — Saphthorael (§83) — human in form with a dog-like head; he puts party-spirit into men and delights in making them stumble; thwarted by Iaed, Ieald, Idelet, Sabaoth, Ithoth, Bae — if a man writes these names of angels on paper, folds it, and wears it round his neck or against his ear, he flees at once and the drunken fit is scattered

“I am called Saphthorael. I put partisanship into men and delight in making them stumble. If a man writes on paper these names of angels — Iaed, Ieald, Idelet, Sabaoth, Ithoth Bae — and, having folded it, wears it round his neck or against his ear, I flee at once and scatter the drunken fit.”

Decan 13 — Phobothēl, called Bobel (§84) — human in form with a dog-like head; he brings slackening sickness of the nerves by his attacks; thwarted by the great Adonael — if he hears “Adonael, shut up Phobothēl,” he flees at once

“I am called Phobothēl [Conybeare's text reads Bobel]. I make slackening sickness of the nerves by my attacks. If I hear the name of the great Adonael — ‘Adonael, shut up Phobothēl’ — I flee at once.”

Decan 14 — Kumeatel (§85) — human in form with a dog-like head; he brings cold, frost, and pain in the stomach; thwarted by Iax — if he hears “Iax, do not stay, do not be warmed, for Solomon is fairer than eleven fathers,” he flees at once

“I am called Kumeatel. I bring cold and frost and pain in the stomach. If I only hear, ‘Iax, do not stay, do not be warmed, for Solomon is fairer than eleven fathers,’ I flee at once.” [The Greek here (XVIII §19) gives this decan's name in a form close to Soubelti; Conybeare's Kumeatel is kept, and the next decan's charm in McCown belongs with this one. See NOTES.]

Decan 15 — Roikel (§86) — human in form with a dog-like head; he brings shivering fits and numbness; thwarted by Zoroel — if he hears “Zoroel, shut up Roikel,” he flees at once

“I am called Roikel. I bring shivering fits and numbness. If I only hear, ‘Zoroel, shut up Roikel,’ I flee at once.”

Decan 16 — Atrax, called Katrax (§87) — human in form with a dog-like head; he lays on men fevers that cannot be cured and do harm; thwarted by the throne of the Most High God — if a man chops coriander, smears it on the lips, and says, “The fever that comes from dirt, I bind you by the throne of the Most High God: flee from dirt and flee from the creature God has made,” he flees at once

“I am called Atrax. I lay on men fevers that cannot be cured and do harm. If you would shut me up, chop coriander and smear it on the lips, saying this charm: ‘The fever that is from dirt — I bind you by the throne of the Most High God. Flee from dirt, and flee from the creature God has made.’ At once I flee.”

Decan 17 — Ieropael (§88) — human in form with a dog-like head; he sits on men's stomachs and makes fits in the bath and on the road, and throws down any man he finds; thwarted by Iudarizē, Sabunē, Dendē — if a man says these names into the sufferer's right ear three times, “Iudarizē, Sabunē, Dendē,” he flees at once

“I am called Ieropael. I sit on the stomach of men and make fits in the bath and in the road. Wherever I am found, or find a man, I throw him down. If a man says into the sufferer's ear, into the right ear three times over, these names — Iudarizē, Sabunē, Dendē — I flee at once.”

Decan 18 — Buldumech, called Modebel (§89) — human in form with a dog-like head; he splits wife from husband and brings a grudge between them; thwarted by the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob — if a man writes down the names of Solomon's fathers on paper, “The God of Abraham and the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob commands you — leave this house in peace,” and sets it in the porch of his house, he flees at once

“I am called Buldumech. I split wife from husband and bring a grudge between them. If a man writes down the names of your fathers, Solomon, on paper and sets it in the porch of his house, I flee from there. The writing shall be this: ‘The God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob commands you — go out from this house in peace.’ At once I go out.”

Decan 19 — Mardero (§90) — human in form with a dog-like head; he sends fever that cannot be cured, and if a man writes his name in a house he flees — in the fuller text, if a man writes on a book-leaf “Sphente, Raphael, flee, drag me not about, flay me not” and ties it round his neck; thwarted by Raphael

“I am called Mardero. I send on men fever that cannot be cured. In whatever house my name is written, I flee at once.” [The fuller form in McCown, Recension B (XVIII §23): “If a man writes on the leaf of a book, ‘Sphente, Raphael, flee, drag me not about, flay me not,’ and ties it round his neck, I flee at once.” Conybeare prints the long form under his twentieth decan.]

Decan 20 — Nathath (§91) — human in form with a dog-like head; he takes his seat on men's knees (and touches the neck); thwarted by Phnounobiel — if a man writes on paper “Phnounobiel, go out, Nathath, and do not touch the neck,” he flees at once

“I am called Nathath. I take my seat on the knees of men. If a man writes on paper, ‘Phnounobiel, depart, Nathath, and do not touch the neck,’ I flee at once.”

Decan 21 — Alath (§92) — human in form with a dog-like head; he makes coughing and hard breathing in children; thwarted by Rorex — if a man writes on paper “Rorex, drive out Alath” and fastens it round the child's neck, he flees at once

“I am called Alath. I make coughing and hard breathing in children. If a man writes on paper, ‘Rorex, drive out Alath,’ and fastens it round his neck, I flee at once.”

Decan 22 — Aldameoth (§92a) — human in form with a dog-like head; he sends pain of the heart; thwarted by Raizoth — if a man writes “Raizoth” and carries it, he flees at once

“I am called Aldameoth. I send pain of the heart. If a man writes ‘Raizoth’ and carries it, I flee at once.” [This decan stands in McCown's Greek (XVIII §26) but is missing from the text Conybeare translated, where the numbering jumps from the twenty-first to the twenty-third.]

Decan 23 — Nephthada (§93) — human in form with a dog-like head; he makes the loins ache and brings painful, slow passing of water; thwarted by Iathath and Uruel — if a man writes on a plate of tin “Iathath, Uruel, Nephthada” and fastens it round the loins, he flees at once

“I am called Nephthada. I make the loins ache and bring slow and painful passing of water. If a man writes on a plate of tin, ‘Iathath, Uruel, Nephthada,’ and fastens it round the loins, I flee at once.”

Decan 24 — Akton (§94) — human in form with a dog-like head; he makes ribs and loin-muscles ache; thwarted by Marmaraoth and Sabaoth — if a man cuts on copper taken from a ship that has missed its anchorage, “Marmaraoth, Sabaoth, drive out Akton,” and fastens it round the loin, he flees at once

“I am called Akton. I make ribs and loin-muscles ache. If a man cuts on copper taken from a ship that has missed its anchorage, ‘Marmaraoth, Sabaoth, drive out Akton,’ and fastens it round the loin, I flee at once.”

Decan 25 — Anatreth (§95) — human in form with a dog-like head; he sends burnings and fevers into the inward parts; thwarted by Arara and Charara — if he hears “Arara, Charara,” he flees at once

“I am called Anatreth. I send burnings and fevers into the inward parts. If I hear, ‘Arara, Charara,’ I flee at once.”

Decan 26 — Enenuth (§96) — human in form with a dog-like head; he steals away men's minds, changes their hearts, and makes a man toothless; thwarted by Allazoel — if a man writes “Allazoel, drive out Enenuth” and ties the paper round him, he flees at once

“I am called Enenuth. I steal away men's minds and change their hearts and make a man toothless. If a man writes, ‘Allazoel, drive out Enenuth,’ and ties the paper round him, I flee at once.”

Decan 27 — Pheth (§97) — human in form with a dog-like head; he makes men wasting and brings bleeding; thwarted by the eleventh aeon — if a man binds him in sweet-smelling, unmixed wine by the eleventh aeon, saying, “I bind you by the eleventh aeon to stop, Pheth, I demand it,” and gives it to the sick man to drink, he flees at once

“I am called Pheth. I make men wasting and bring bleeding. If a man binds me in wine, sweet-smelling and unmixed, by the eleventh aeon, and says, ‘I bind you by the eleventh aeon to stop, I demand it, Pheth,’ then gives it to the sick man to drink, I flee at once.”

Decan 28 — Harpax (§98) — human in form with a dog-like head; he sends sleeplessness on men; thwarted by Kokphnedisos — if a man writes “Kokphnedisos” and binds it round the temples, he flees at once

“I am called Harpax. I send sleeplessness on men. If a man writes ‘Kokphnedisos’ and binds it round the temples, I flee at once.”

Decan 29 — Anoster (§99) — human in form with a dog-like head; he brings womb-madness and pains in the bladder; thwarted by Marmaraoth — if a man pounds three seeds of laurel into pure oil and smears it on, saying, “I bind you, Anoster. Stop, by Marmaraoth,” he flees at once

“I am called Anoster. I bring womb-madness and pains in the bladder. If a man pounds into pure oil three seeds of laurel and smears it on, saying, ‘I bind you, Anoster. Stop, by Marmaraoth,’ I flee at once.”

Decan 30 — Physikoreth (§100) — human in form with a dog-like head; he makes long-lasting sickness; thwarted by Seraphim and Cherubim — if a man throws salt, rubbed in the hand, into oil and smears it on the sick man, saying, “Seraphim, Cherubim, help me,” he flees at once

“I am called Physikoreth. I make long-lasting sickness. If you throw salt, rubbed in the hand, into oil and smear it on the sick man, saying, ‘Seraphim, Cherubim, help me,’ I flee at once.” [Conybeare prints this spirit (as Hephisikireth) after Alleborish; McCown's Greek puts Physikoreth first (XVIII §34). The order here follows McCown.]

Decan 31 — Alleborish (§101) — human in form with a dog-like head; when a man eating fish has swallowed a bone, he holds it fast; thwarted by the bone of the same fish — if the sufferer takes a bone from the fish and coughs, he flees at once

“I am called Alleborish. If, in eating fish, a man has swallowed a bone, I hold it fast. He must take a bone from the fish and cough, and at once I flee.”

Decan 32 — Ichthyon (§102) — human in form with a dog-like head; he stills the muscles and bruises them; thwarted by Adonaeth — if he hears “Adonaeth, help,” he flees at once

“I am called Ichthyon. I still the muscles and bruise them. If I hear, ‘Adonaeth, help,’ I flee at once.”

Decan 33 — Agchoniōn (§103) — human in form with a dog-like head; he lies among swaddling-clothes (and in the throat); thwarted by Lycurgos written letter by letter backward — if a man writes on fig-leaves “Lycurgos,” taking away one letter at a time and writing it backward — Lycurgos, ycurgos, kurgos, yrgos, gos, os — he flees at once

“I am called Agchoniōn. I lie among swaddling-clothes. If a man writes on fig-leaves ‘Lycurgos,’ taking away one letter at a time, and writes it backward, I flee at once: Lycurgos, ycurgos, kurgos, yrgos, gos, os.”

Decan 34 — Autothith (§104) — human in form with a dog-like head; he makes grudges and fighting; thwarted by Alpha and Omega written down

“I am called Autothith. I make grudges and fighting. I am stopped by Alpha and Omega, if written down.”

Decan 35 — Phibenoth (§105) — human in form with a dog-like head; he casts the evil eye on every man; thwarted by the much-suffering eye drawn — if the eye is drawn, it stops him

“I am called Phibenoth. I cast the evil eye on every man. The eye that has suffered much, if it is drawn, stops me.”

Decan 36 — Bianakith (§106) — human in form with a dog-like head; he has a grudge against the body, lays houses waste, and makes flesh rot; thwarted by Meltō, Ardou, Anaath — if a man writes on the front door of his house, “Meltō, Ardou, Anaath,” he flees from that place

“I am called Bianakith. I have a grudge against the body. I lay houses waste. I make flesh rot, and all else that is like it. If a man writes on the front door of his house, ‘Meltō, Ardou, Anaath,’ I flee from that place.”

§107. When I, Solomon, heard this, I glorified the God of heaven and earth. I commanded them to fetch water in the Temple of God. I prayed to the Lord God that the demons outside, who hinder mankind, might be bound and made to come to the Temple of God. Some of these demons I condemned to do the heavy work of building the Temple of God. Others I shut up in prisons. Others I ordered to wrestle with fire in the making of gold and silver, sitting down by lead and spoon, and to make ready places for the other demons in which they should be shut up.

Peace, and the Queen of the South

§108. I, Solomon, had much quiet in all the earth and spent my life in deep peace, honoured by all men and by all under heaven. I built the whole Temple of the Lord God. My kingdom prospered, and my army was with me. The city of Jerusalem had rest, rejoicing and delighted. All the kings of the earth came to me from the ends of the earth to see the Temple I had built to the Lord God. Having heard of the wisdom given to me, they did homage to me in the Temple, bringing gold and silver and precious stones, many and of all kinds, and bronze and iron and lead and cedar logs. Woods that do not rot they brought me, for the furnishing of the Temple of God.

§109. Among them also the queen of the south, who was a witch, came in great concern and bowed low before me to the earth. Having heard my wisdom, she glorified the God of Israel. She put all my wisdom to the test, all the love in which I taught her, according to the wisdom given to me. All the sons of Israel glorified God.

The Old Man, His Son, and the Laughing Demon

§110. In those days one of the workmen, a man of ripe old age, threw himself down before me and said, "King Solomon, pity me, because I am old." I told him to stand up and said, "Tell me, old man, all you will." He answered, "I beg you, king. I have an only-born son, and he insults me and beats me openly and plucks out the hair of my head and threatens me with a painful death. I beg you, take vengeance for me."

§111. When I, Solomon, heard this, I felt pity as I looked at his old age, and I told them to bring the child to me. When he was brought, I asked him whether it was true. The youth said, "I was not so filled with madness as to strike my father with my hand. Be kind to me, king. I have not dared to do such a godless thing, poor wretch that I am." When I, Solomon, heard this from the youth, I urged the old man to think the matter over and accept his son's plea. He would not, but said he would rather let him die. As the old man would not yield, I was about to pronounce sentence on the youth, when I saw Ornias the demon laughing. I was very angry at the demon's laughing in my presence. I ordered my men to take the other parties away and bring Ornias forward before my judgment seat. When he was brought before me, I said to him, "Accursed one, why did you look at me and laugh?" The demon answered, "Please, king, it was not because of you I laughed, but because of this ill-starred old man and the wretched youth, his son. After three days his son will die before his time. Look, the old man wishes foully to make away with him."

§112. I, Solomon, having heard this, said to the demon, "Is what you speak true?" He answered, "It is true, king." On hearing that, I told them to take the demon away and to bring the old man with his son before me again. I told them to make friends with one another again, and I gave them food. Then I told the old man to bring his son to me here again after three days. "And," said I, "I will attend to him." They greeted me and went their way.

§113. When they were gone, I ordered Ornias to be brought forward and said to him, "Tell me how you know this." He answered, "We demons go up into the firmament of heaven and fly about among the stars. We hear the judgments that go forth on the souls of men, and at once we come. Whether by force of influence, or by fire, or by sword, or by some accident, we hide our act of destruction. If a man does not die by some untimely disaster or by violence, then we demons change ourselves so as to appear to men and be worshipped in our human shape."

§114. Having heard this, I glorified the Lord God. I questioned the demon again, "Tell me how you can go up into heaven, being demons, and mix among the stars and holy angels." He answered, "Just as things are fulfilled in heaven, so also on earth are fulfilled the patterns of all of them. There are principalities, authorities, world-rulers, and we demons fly about in the air. We hear the voices of the heavenly beings and look on all the powers. Having no ground on which to land and rest, we lose strength and fall off like leaves from trees. Men, seeing us, think the stars are falling from heaven. It is not really so, king. We fall because of our weakness and because we have nowhere anything to lay hold of. So we fall down like lightnings in the depth of night, all of a sudden. We set cities in flames and fire the fields. The stars have firm foundations in heaven, like the sun and the moon."

§115. When I, Solomon, had heard this, I ordered the demon to be guarded for five days. After the five days I called the old man back and was about to question him. He came to me in grief, with black face. I said to him, "Tell me, old man, where is your son? What does this dress mean?" He answered, "Look, I am now childless, and I sit by my son's grave in despair. He has already been dead two days." When I, Solomon, heard that and knew the demon Ornias had told me the truth, I glorified the God of Israel.

§116. The queen of the south saw all this and wondered, glorifying the God of Israel. She saw the Temple of the Lord being built. She gave a shekel of gold and a hundred myriads of silver and choice bronze, and she went into the Temple. She saw the altar of incense and the bronze supports of this altar, and the gems of the lamps flashing out different colours, and the lamp-stand of stone, and of emerald, and hyacinth, and sapphire. She saw the vessels of gold and silver and bronze and wood, and the folds of skins dyed red with madder. She saw the bases of the pillars of the Temple of the Lord. All were of one gold, apart from the demons I condemned to labour. There was peace in the circle of my kingdom and over all the earth.

The Letter from Arabia and the Capture of Ephippas

§117. While I was in my kingdom, the King of the Arabians, Adares, sent me a letter. The writing of the letter was as follows:

"To King Solomon, all hail! We have heard — it has been heard to all the ends of the earth — of the wisdom given in you, and that you are a man merciful from the Lord. Understanding has been given you over all the spirits of the air, and on earth, and under the earth. Now there is present in the land of Arabia a spirit of this kind: at early dawn a certain wind begins to blow until the third hour. Its blast is harsh and terrible, and it kills man and beast. No spirit can live on earth against this demon. I pray you, then — since the spirit is a wind — work out something according to the wisdom given in you by the Lord your God, and be willing to send a man able to capture it. King Solomon, I and my people and all my land will serve you unto death. All Arabia shall be at peace with you, if you will do this righteous act for us. Do not despise our humble prayer, and do not let the province under your authority be utterly brought to nothing. We are your suppliants — I, my people, and all my land. Farewell to my lord. All health!"

§118. I, Solomon, read this letter. I folded it up and gave it to my people and said to them, "After seven days you shall remind me of this letter." Jerusalem was built, and the Temple was being finished. There was a stone, the end stone of the corner, lying there — great, chosen out — which I wished to lay in the head of the corner of the finished Temple. All the workmen, and all the demons helping them, came to the same place to bring up the stone and lay it on the pinnacle of the holy Temple, and they were not strong enough to stir it and lay it on the corner set for it. That stone was very great and much needed for the corner of the Temple.

§119. After seven days, being reminded of the letter of Adares, King of Arabia, I called my servant and said to him, "Saddle your camel. Take a leather flask for yourself, and take also this seal. Go into Arabia to the place where the evil spirit blows. There take the flask, and hold the signet-ring in front of the mouth of the flask, toward the blast of the spirit. When the flask is blown out, you will know the demon is in it. Then tie up the mouth of the flask quickly, seal it tight with the seal-ring, lay it carefully on the camel, and bring it to me here. If on the way it offers you gold or silver or treasure in return for letting it go, do not be persuaded. Make no oath to release it. If it points out the places where gold or silver are, mark the places and seal them with this seal. Bring the demon to me. Now go, and fare you well."

§120. The youth did as he was told. He saddled his camel, laid a flask on it, and set off into Arabia. The men of that region would not believe he would be able to catch the evil spirit. When it was dawn, the servant stood before the spirit's blast and laid the flask on the ground, with the finger-ring on the mouth of the flask. The demon blew through the middle of the finger-ring into the mouth of the flask, and going in, it blew the flask out. The man promptly stood up to it and drew the mouth of the flask tight with his hand, in the name of the Lord God of Sabaoth. The demon stayed inside the flask. After that the youth stayed in that land three days to make trial. The spirit no longer blew against that city. All the Arabs knew he had safely shut in the spirit.

§121. The youth fastened the flask on the camel, and the Arabs sent him on his way with much honour and precious gifts, praising and magnifying the God of Israel. The youth brought in the bag and laid it in the middle of the Temple. The next day I, King Solomon, went into the Temple of God and sat in deep trouble about the stone of the end of the corner.

When I entered the Temple, the flask stood up and walked round some seven steps, then fell on its mouth and did homage to me. I wondered that even with the bottle the demon still had power and could walk about. I commanded it to stand up. The flask stood up — it stood on its feet, all blown out. I questioned him, "Tell me, who are you?" The spirit inside said, "I am the demon called Ephippas, who is in Arabia." I said to him, "Is this your name?" He answered, "Yes. Wherever I will, I land and set fire and do to death."

§122 Ephippas of Arabia — a wind-demon caught in a leather flask, who blows from dawn to the third hour and kills man and beast; he can remove mountains, overthrow the oaths of kings, wither trees and make their leaves fall, and with the demon of the Red Sea he can lift the corner-stone and the pillar of air; thwarted by the only-ruling God who is to be born of a virgin and crucified by the Jews on a cross, whom angels and archangels worship

§122. I said to him, "By what angel are you stopped?" He answered, "By the only-ruling God, who has authority over me even to be heard. He who is to be born of a virgin and crucified by the Jews on a cross — him the angels and archangels worship. He stops me and weakens me from my great strength, which was given me by my father the devil." I said to him, "What can you do?" He answered, "I am able to remove mountains and overthrow the oaths of kings. I wither trees and make their leaves fall off." I said to him, "Can you raise this stone and lay it for the beginning of this corner in the fair plan of the Temple?" He said, "Not only raise this, king, but with the help of the demon who rules over the Red Sea, I will also bring up the pillar of air and stand it where you will in Jerusalem."

§123. Saying this, I pressed him hard, and the flask became as if emptied of air. I placed it under the stone. The spirit girded himself up and lifted it on top of the flask. The flask went up the steps, carrying the stone, and laid it down at the end of the entrance of the Temple. I, Solomon, seeing the stone raised aloft and placed on a foundation, said, "Truly the Scripture is fulfilled which says, 'The stone which the builders rejected on trial — that same is become the head of the corner.' It is not mine to grant, but God's, that the demon should be strong enough to lift up so great a stone and set it in the place I wished."

§124. Ephippas led the demon of the Red Sea with the column. They both took the column and raised it aloft from the earth. I outwitted these two spirits, so that they could not shake the whole earth in a moment of time. I sealed round with my ring on this side and that and said, "Watch." The spirits have stayed upholding it until this day, as proof of the wisdom given to me. There the pillar was hanging, of enormous size, in mid-air, held up by the winds. The spirits appeared underneath, like air, holding it up. If a man looks steadily, the pillar leans a little, being held up by the spirits. It is so to this day.

§125 Abezithibod, demon of the Red Sea — a fierce, winged spirit with a single wing, descendant of the archangel, who sat in the first heaven called Ameleouth, was present when Moses went in before Pharaoh and hardened his heart, was called on by Iannes and Iambres against Moses with wonders and signs, and stayed under the pillar in the Red Sea after the Egyptians drowned until Ephippas fetched him up; bound with Ephippas to hold the pillar until the end of the world

§125. I, Solomon, questioned the other spirit, who had come up with the pillar from the depth of the Red Sea. I said to him, "Who are you, and what are you called? What is your work? I hear many things about you." The demon answered, "I, King Solomon, am called Abezithibod. I am a descendant of the archangel. Once, as I sat in the first heaven, whose name is Ameleouth — I am a fierce spirit and winged, and with a single wing — I plotted against every spirit under heaven. I was there when Moses went in before Pharaoh, king of Egypt, and I hardened his heart. I am the one whom Iannes and Iambres called on against Moses in Egypt. I am he who fought against Moses with wonders and signs."

§126. I said to him, "How were you found in the Red Sea?" He answered, "In the going-out of the sons of Israel I hardened the heart of Pharaoh. I stirred up his heart and the hearts of his ministers. I made them chase after the children of Israel. Pharaoh followed with me and all the Egyptians. I was there, and we followed together. We all came up upon the Red Sea. When the children of Israel had crossed over, the water came back and hid all the host of the Egyptians and all their might. I stayed in the sea, kept under this pillar. When Ephippas came, sent by you, shut up in a flask, he fetched me up to you."

§127. I, Solomon, having heard this, glorified God and bound the demons by oath not to disobey me, but to stay holding up the pillar. They both swore, saying, "The Lord your God lives — we will not let go this pillar until the world's end. On whatever day this stone falls, then shall be the end of the world."

Solomon's Fall

§128. I, Solomon, glorified God and adorned the Temple of the Lord with all that is fair to see. I was glad in spirit in my kingdom, and there was peace in my days. I took wives of my own from every land. They were beyond number. I marched against the Jebusites, and there I saw a Jebusite, the daughter of a man. I fell violently in love with her and wished to take her to wife along with my other wives. I said to their priests, "Give me the Shunammite to wife." The priests of Moloch said to me, "If you love this maiden, go in and worship our gods — the great god Raphan and the god called Moloch." I was in fear of the glory of God and did not go to worship. I said to them, "I will not worship a strange god. What is this proposal, that you force me to do so much?" They said, "Follow our fathers."

§129. When I answered that I would on no account worship strange gods, they told the maiden not to sleep with me until I obeyed and sacrificed to the gods. I was moved. Crafty Eros brought and laid by her for me five grasshoppers, saying, "Take these grasshoppers and crush them together in the name of the god Moloch, and then I will sleep with you." This I actually did. At once the Spirit of God departed from me, and I became weak as well as foolish in my words. After that I was forced by her to build a temple of idols to Baal, and to Raphan, and to Moloch, and to the other idols.

§130. Then I, wretch that I am, followed her advice, and the glory of God quite departed from me. My spirit was darkened, and I became the sport of idols and demons. So I have written out this Testament, that you who get hold of it may pray, and take heed to the last things, and not to the first, so that you may find grace for ever and ever. Amen.
