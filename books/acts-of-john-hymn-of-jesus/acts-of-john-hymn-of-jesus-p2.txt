[The last episode of these Acts was preserved separately, for reading in church on the Saint's day; it survives in at least nine Greek manuscripts and in many versions. — James]

106 John therefore remained with the brethren, rejoicing in the Lord. And on the next day, being the Lord's day, and all the brethren being gathered together, he began to say to them: "Brethren and fellow-servants and fellow-heirs and partakers with me in the kingdom of the Lord: you know the Lord — how many mighty works he has granted you through me; how many wonders, healings, signs; how great spiritual gifts, teachings, governings, refreshings, ministries, knowledges, glories, graces, gifts, faiths, fellowships — all of which you have seen given to you by him before your sight, though not seen by these eyes nor heard by these ears. Be, therefore, established in him, remembering him in your every deed, knowing the mystery of the dispensation which has come to pass toward men, for what cause the Lord accomplished it. He begs you through me, brethren, and entreats you, desiring to remain without grief, without insult, not plotted against, not chastised — for he knows even the insult that comes from you; he knows even dishonor; he knows conspiracy; he knows even chastisement, from those who do not listen to his commandments.

107 "Let not, then, our good God be grieved — the compassionate, the merciful, the holy, the pure, the undefiled, the immaterial, the only One, the unchangeable, the simple, the guileless, the free from wrath — even our God Jesus Christ, who is above every name we can utter or conceive, and more exalted. Let him rejoice with us because we walk rightly; let him be glad because we live purely; let him be refreshed because our way of life is sober; let him be free of care because we live in continence; let him be pleased because we share with one another; let him smile because we are chaste; let him be merry because we love him. These things I now speak to you, brethren, because I am hurrying to the work set before me, and already am being perfected by the Lord. For what else could I have to say to you? You have the pledge of our God; you have the earnest of his goodness; you have his presence, which cannot be escaped. If, then, you sin no more, he forgives you what you did in ignorance; but if, after you have known him and he has had mercy on you, you walk again in the same deeds, both the former will be laid to your charge, and you will also have no part or mercy before him."

108 And when he had spoken this to them, he prayed in this way: "O Jesus, who have woven this crown with your weaving; who have joined together these many blossoms into the unfading flower of your countenance; who have sown these words in them — you, only tender toward your servants, and physician who heals freely; only doer of good, who despises none; only merciful and lover of men; only savior and righteous; only seer of all; who are in all and everywhere present, containing all things and filling all things: Christ Jesus, God, Lord, who with your gifts and your mercy shelter those who trust in you, who know clearly the wiles and the assaults of him who is everywhere our adversary, which he devises against us — you only, O Lord, help your servants by your coming. Even so, Lord."

And he took bread and said: "What praise, what offering, what thanksgiving shall we break in this bread, other than you only, O Lord Jesus? We glorify your name, spoken by the Father; we glorify your name, spoken through the Son; we glorify your entrance through the door; we glorify your resurrection, shown to us through you; we glorify your seed, your word, your grace, your faith, your salt, your son, your vessel, your altar, your ass, your flower, [your] star, [your] stone."

[The Greek text and the copy before us are disordered and partly illegible in this passage (see Translator Notes). The substance — the breaking of the bread, the distribution to every brother present, and the prayer for each by name — is secure.]

And he broke the bread and gave to each of the brethren, and he prayed for each one, saying: "[…] And so let each of you be made worthy." And when he had given the eucharist to all the brethren in peace, he spoke to them about the word of the Lord, and exhorted those who had come with him out [of the city], strengthening and perfecting them.

112 [The prayer continued:] "…who proclaimed yourself even among beasts; who, when it [the soul] had sunk into lawlessness, manifested yourself to it; who […]; who, when it had been vanquished by Satan, gave it your hand and raised it up from the things of the body; who did not allow it to walk after a bodily sort; who showed it its own enemy; who gave it knowledge of yourself; O God — Jesus, the Father of those above the heavens; the Lord of those in the heavens; the law of those in the ether; the course of those on the earth; the fear of those under the earth; the grace of those who are your own — receive also the soul of your servant John, which may be accounted worthy by you.

"You who have kept me until this hour for yourself, untouched by union with a woman; who, when in my youth I desired to marry, appeared to me and said to me, 'John, I have need of you'; who prepared for me also a sickness of body; who, when for the third time I would marry, at once prevented me, and then at the third hour of the day [said to me on the seashore], 'John, if you had not been mine, I would have allowed you to marry'; who for two years blinded my eyes and granted me to mourn and be sorry; who in the third year opened the eyes of my mind, and also granted me my visible eyes; who, when I saw clearly, ordained that it should be grievous to me to look upon a woman; who saved me from the temporal fancy and led me to that which endures always; who rid me of the bitter madness that is in the flesh; who took me from bitter death and established me on you alone; who healed the disease of my soul and cut off the open deed [of sin]; who […] and banished him who raised tumult in me; who made my love of you spotless; who made my journey to you free; who gave me undoubting faith in you; who ordered and made clear my inclination [toward you]; who give to every man the due reward of his works; who put into my soul that I should have nothing more precious than you, and what is more precious than you? Now therefore, Lord, whereas I have accomplished the [service] entrusted to me, account me worthy of your rest, and grant me that end in you which is salvation unspeakable and unutterable.

114 "And as I come to you, let the fire fall back; let the darkness be overcome; let the gulf be powerless; let the furnace die out; let Gehenna be quenched. Let angels […] ; let devils fear; let rulers be broken; let powers fall; let the places of the right hand stand fast; let those of the left hand […]; let the devil be muzzled; let Satan be derided; let his wrath be burned out; let his madness be stilled; let his vengeance be ashamed; let his assault be in pain; let his children be struck down and all his roots plucked up. And grant me to accomplish the journey to you without suffering insolence or provocation, and to receive what you have promised to those who live purely and have loved you only."

115 And having sealed himself in every part, he stood and said: "You are with me, O Lord Jesus Christ." And he laid himself down in the trench where he had spread his garments; and having said to us, "Peace be with you, brethren," he gave up his spirit, rejoicing.

[The less good Greek manuscripts and some versions are not content with this simple ending. The Latin says that after the prayer a great light appeared over the apostle for the space of an hour, so bright that no one could look at it; then he laid himself down and gave up the ghost. "We who were there rejoiced, some of us, and some mourned… And at once manna coming out of the tomb was seen by all — which manna that place produces even to this day." Perhaps the best conclusion is that of one Greek manuscript: "We brought a linen cloth and spread it over him, and went into the city. And on the following day we went out and did not find his body, for it was taken away by the power of our Lord Jesus Christ, to whom be glory." Another says: "On the next day we dug in the place, and found not him but only his sandals, and the earth heaving up like a spring." Augustine, on John 21, reports the belief that in his time the earth over the grave was seen to move as if stirred by John's breathing. — from James's closing notes]
