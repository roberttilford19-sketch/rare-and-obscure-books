THE ACTS OF JOHN

[John goes from Miletus to Ephesus]

18 Now John was hurrying to Ephesus, moved by a vision. Damonicus, therefore, and Aristodemus his kinsman, and a very rich man named Cleobius, and the wife of Marcellus, barely persuaded him to stay one day with them in Miletus. And when they had set out very early in the morning, and about four miles of the journey were done, a voice came from heaven in the hearing of them all, saying: "John, you are about to bring glory to your Lord in Ephesus, as you shall know — you and all the brethren who are with you, and some of those there who will believe through you." John therefore pondered, rejoicing within himself, what it might be that would happen to him at Ephesus, and said: "Lord, see, I go according to your will; let what you desire be done."

19 And as we drew near the city, Lycomedes the praetor of the Ephesians, a man of great wealth, met us, and falling at John's feet begged him, saying: "Is your name John? The God whom you preach has sent you to do good to my wife, who has been struck with palsy for seven days now and lies incurable. Glorify your God by healing her, and have compassion on us. For as I was considering what decision to make in this matter, someone stood by me and said: 'Lycomedes, stop this thought that fights against you, for it is evil; do not give yourself to it. For I have compassion on my handmaid Cleopatra, and I have sent from Miletus a man named John, who will raise her up and restore her to you whole. Do not delay, then, you servant of the God who has shown himself to me, but hurry to my wife, who has nothing left but breath.'" And at once John went from the gate, with the brethren who were with him and Lycomedes, to his house. But Cleobius said to his servants: "Go to my kinsman Callippus and receive generous entertainment from him — for I have come here with his son — so that we may find all things respectable."

20 Now when Lycomedes came with John into the house where his wife lay, he took hold of his feet again and said: "See, lord, the withering of her beauty; see the youth; see the renowned flower of my poor wife, at which all Ephesus used to marvel. Wretched me! I have suffered envy; I have been humbled; the eye of my enemies has struck me. I have never wronged anyone, though I could have harmed many, for I looked ahead to this very thing, and took care never to see any evil or any such ill fortune as this. What profit, then, does Cleopatra have from my anxiety? What have I gained by being known as a godly man until this day? No — I suffer more than the ungodly, in that I see you, Cleopatra, lying in such a state. The sun in its course shall see me talking with you no more; I will go before you, Cleopatra, and rid myself of life. I will not spare my own safety, young though it is. I will defend myself before Justice, that I did right to desert, for I can charge her with judging unrighteously. I will be avenged on her when I come before her as a ghost stripped of life. I will say to her: 'You forced me to leave the light when you robbed me of Cleopatra; you made me become a corpse when you sent me this ill fortune; you forced me to insult Providence, by cutting off my joy in life.'"

21 And with still more words Lycomedes, addressing Cleopatra, came near the bed and cried aloud and lamented. But John pulled him away and said: "Stop these lamentations and these unfitting words; you must not disobey the one who appeared to you. Know that you will receive your wife again. Stand, therefore, with us who have come here for her sake, and pray to the God whom you saw showing himself to you in dreams. What then, Lycomedes? Wake up, you too, and open your soul. Shake off the heavy sleep from you. Beg the Lord, entreat him for your wife, and he will raise her up." But he fell on the floor and lamented, fainting.

Then John said, with tears: "Alas for the fresh betrayal of my vision! For the new temptation prepared for me! For the new device of the one who schemes against me! The voice from heaven that was carried to me on the road — was it this it devised for me? Was it this it foretold would happen here, betraying me to this great crowd of the citizens because of Lycomedes? The man lies here without breath, and I know well they will not let me leave this house alive. Why do you delay, Lord — or what will you do? Why have you shut us off from your good promise? Do not, I beg you, Lord, do not give cause to exult to him who rejoices in the suffering of others; give no cause to dance to the one who always mocks us; but let your holy name and your mercy make haste. Raise up these two dead, whose death is against me."

22 And even as John cried out in this way, the city of the Ephesians ran together to the house of Lycomedes, having heard that he was dead. And John, seeing the great crowd that had come, said to the Lord: "Now is the time of refreshment and of confidence toward you, O Christ; now is the time for us who are sick to have the help that is from you, O physician who heals freely. Keep my coming in here safe from mockery. I beg you, Jesus, help this great crowd, so that it may come to you who are Lord of all things. See the affliction; see those who lie here. Prepare from among those assembled for that very purpose holy vessels for your service, when they see your gift. For you yourself have said, O Christ: 'Ask, and it will be given you.' We ask of you, therefore, O King — not gold, not silver, not property, not possessions, nor anything that is on earth and perishes, but two souls, by whom you will convert those who are here to your way, to your teaching, to your freedom, to your unfailing promise; for when they see your power in those who died being raised, they will be saved — some of them. You yourself, therefore, give them hope in you. And so I go to Cleopatra and say: 'Rise, in the name of Jesus Christ.'"

23 And he came to her and touched her face and said: "Cleopatra, he says — whom every ruler fears, and every creature and every power, the abyss and all darkness, and unsmiling death, and the height of heaven, and the whole power of the prince of this world, and the ruler's pride — 'Rise, and do not be an offense for many who do not want to believe, or an affliction for souls who are able to hope and be saved.'"

And Cleopatra cried out at once with a loud voice: "I rise, master — save your handmaid!"

Now when she had arisen, who had lain incurable for seven days, the city of the Ephesians was stirred at the unexpected sight. And Cleopatra asked about her husband Lycomedes, but John said to her: "Cleopatra, if you keep your soul unmoved and steadfast, you will at once have Lycomedes your husband standing here beside you — if you are not disturbed nor shaken at what has happened, having believed in my God, who through me will grant him to you alive. Come with me, therefore, into your other bedroom, and you will see him — a dead corpse indeed, but raised again by the power of my God."

24 And Cleopatra, going with John into her bedroom and seeing Lycomedes dead for her sake, could not speak; she ground her teeth and bit her tongue and closed her eyes, raining tears; and in calmness she gave heed to the apostle. But John had compassion on Cleopatra when he saw that she neither raged nor was beside herself, and called on the perfect, condescending mercy, saying: "Lord Jesus Christ, you see the pressure of her sorrow; you see the need; you see Cleopatra crying her soul out in silence — for she holds within her the frenzy that cannot be borne. And I know that for Lycomedes' sake she will die upon his body too." And she said quietly to John: "That, master, is in my mind, and nothing else."

And the apostle went to the couch where Lycomedes lay, and taking Cleopatra's hand he said: "Cleopatra, because of the crowd that is present, and your relatives who have come in, say to your husband with a loud cry: 'Rise and glorify the name of God, for he gives back the dead to the dead.'" And she went to her husband and said to him as she had been taught, and raised him right up. And when he arose, he fell on the floor and kissed John's feet, but John raised him, saying: "Man, kiss not my feet, but the feet of God, by whose power you both have risen."

25 But Lycomedes said to John: "I beg you and adjure you by the God in whose name you have raised us, stay with us — you together with all those who are with you." Cleopatra likewise took hold of his feet and said the same. And John said to them: "Tomorrow I will be with you." And they said to him again: "We shall have no hope in your God, and will have been raised for nothing, if you do not stay with us." And Cleobius and Aristodemus and Damonicus were touched in their souls and said to John: "Let us stay with them, so that they continue without offense toward the Lord." So he remained there with the brethren.

[The portrait of John]

26 There came together, therefore, a very great gathering on John's account; and as he spoke to those who were there, Lycomedes — who had a friend, a skillful painter — went hastily to him and said: "You see me in a great hurry. Come quickly to my house and paint the man I show you, without his knowing." And the painter, giving someone the necessary tools and colors, said to Lycomedes: "Show him to me, and about the rest have no anxiety." And Lycomedes pointed John out to the painter, and brought him near, and shut him up in a room from which the apostle of Christ could be seen. And Lycomedes was with the blessed man, feasting on the faith and the knowledge of our God, and rejoiced all the more at the thought that he would possess him in a portrait.

27 The painter, then, on the first day made an outline of him and went away; and on the next day he painted him in with his colors, and so delivered the portrait to Lycomedes, to his great joy. And he took it and set it up in his own bedroom and hung it with garlands — so that later John, when he noticed it, said to him: "My beloved child, what is it you always do when you come in from the bath into your bedroom alone? Do I not pray with you and the rest of the brethren? Or is there something you are hiding from us?" And as he said this and talked playfully with him, he went into the bedroom and saw the portrait of an old man crowned with garlands, with lamps and altars set before it. And he called him and said: "Lycomedes, what do you mean by this matter of the portrait? Could it be one of your gods that is painted here? For I see that you are still living in heathen fashion." And Lycomedes answered him: "My only God is the one who raised me from death with my wife; but if, next to that God, it is right that men who have benefited us should be called gods — it is you, father, whom I have had painted in this portrait, whom I crown and love and reverence as having become my good guide."

28 And John, who had never at any time seen his own face, said to him: "You mock me, child. Am I like that in form — surpassing your Lord? How can you persuade me that the portrait is like me?" And Lycomedes brought him a mirror. And when he had seen himself in the mirror and looked intently at the portrait, he said: "As the Lord Jesus Christ lives, the portrait is like me — yet not like me, child, but like my fleshly image. For if this painter, who copied this face of mine, wanted to draw me in a portrait, he would be at a loss: he would need more than the colors now given him — boards and plaster and glue, and the arrangement of my shape, and old age and youth, and all things that are seen with the eye.

29 "But you, Lycomedes — become a good painter for me. You have colors which he gives you through me, who paints all of us for himself — Jesus — who knows the shapes and appearances and postures and dispositions and patterns of our souls. And the colors with which I bid you paint are these: faith in God, knowledge, godly fear, friendship, fellowship, meekness, kindness, brotherly love, purity, simplicity, tranquility, fearlessness, freedom from grief, sobriety — the whole company of colors that paints the likeness of your soul, and even now raises up your members that were cast down, and levels those that were lifted up, and tends your bruises, and heals your wounds, and puts your disheveled hair in order, and washes your face, and trains your eyes, and purges your body, and empties your belly, and cuts off what is beneath it. And in a word: when the whole company and mixture of such colors has come together into your soul, it will present it to our Lord Jesus Christ unafraid, whole, and firm in shape. But this thing you have now done is childish and imperfect: you have drawn a dead likeness of the dead."

[No portion of the text need be lost at this point, but some few sentences may have been omitted. The transition is abrupt, and the new episode has no title of its own, as episodes do elsewhere. — James]

[The aged women healed in the theater]

30 And he commanded Verus, the brother who served him, to gather the aged women who were in all Ephesus, and he and Cleopatra and Lycomedes made all things ready for their care. Verus, then, came to John and said: "Of the aged women who are here over sixty years old, I have found only four sound in body; of the rest, some are […] and some palsied and others sick." And when John heard that, he was silent a long time; and he rubbed his face and said: "O the slackness of those who dwell in Ephesus! O the state of ruin, and the weakness toward God! O devil, who has this long time mocked the faithful in Ephesus! Jesus, who gives me grace and the gift of trusting in him, says to me in silence: 'Send for the old women who are sick, and be with them in the theater, and heal them through me; for some of them who will come to this spectacle I will, by these healings, convert and make useful for some purpose.'"

31 Now when the whole crowd had come together at Lycomedes' house, he sent them away on John's behalf, saying: "Tomorrow come to the theater, as many as desire to see the power of God." And the crowd, the next day, while it was still night, came to the theater, so that the proconsul also heard of it and hastened and took his seat with all the people. And a certain praetor, Andronicus, who was then the first man of the Ephesians, put it about that John had promised impossible and unbelievable things: "But if," he said, "he is able to do any such thing as I hear, let him come into the public theater when it is open, naked, and holding nothing in his hands, and let him not use that magic name which I have heard him utter."

32 John, therefore, having heard this and being moved by these words, ordered the aged women to be brought into the theater. And when they were all brought into the midst — some of them on beds, others lying in a deep sleep — and the whole city had run together, and a great silence fell, John opened his mouth and began to speak:

33 "Men of Ephesus, learn first of all why I am visiting your city, and what this great confidence is that I have toward you, so that it may be plain to this general assembly and to all of you. I have been sent, then, on a mission not of human ordering, and not on any empty journey; neither am I a merchant who makes bargains and exchanges. But Jesus Christ, whom I preach — being compassionate and kind — desires through me to convert all of you who are held in unbelief and sold to evil lusts, and to deliver you from error. And by his power I will confound even the unbelief of your praetor, by raising up those who lie before you, whose condition and illnesses you all can see. And to do this is impossible for me if they perish; therefore they shall be healed.

34 "But first I have wanted to plant this in your ears: that you should take care for your souls — for which purpose I have come to you — and not expect that this time will last forever, for it is only a moment; and not lay up treasures on earth, where all things fade. Do not think that when you have children you can rest upon them, and for their sake do not try to defraud and overreach. Nor you, poor men, be troubled if you have nothing with which to serve pleasures; for well-to-do men in sickness call you the lucky ones. Nor you, rich men, rejoice that you have much money; for by possessing these things you provide yourselves with a grief you cannot get rid of when you lose them, and besides, while it is with you, you are afraid someone may attack you because of it.

35 "You also, who are puffed up over the shapeliness of your body and are of lofty looks — you shall see the end of that promise in the grave. And you who rejoice in adultery, know that both law and nature, and before these conscience, avenge it on you. And you, adulteress, adversary of the law — you do not know where you will end up. And you who do not share with the needy, but have money laid up: when you depart from this body and need some mercy while you burn in fire, you will have no one to pity you. And you, the wrathful and hot-tempered — know that your way of life is like the wild beasts'. And you, drunkard and brawler — learn that you lose your senses by being enslaved to a shameful and filthy desire.

36 "You who rejoice in gold and delight yourself with ivory and jewels — when night falls, can you see what you love? You who are conquered by soft clothing and then leave life — will those things profit you in the place you are going to? And let the murderer know that the fitting punishment is laid up for him twofold after his departure from here. Likewise you too, poisoner, sorcerer, robber, cheat, sodomite, thief, and as many as are of that band — you shall come at last, as your works lead you, to unquenchable fire, and utter darkness, and the pit of punishment, and eternal threatening. Therefore, men of Ephesus, turn yourselves, knowing this also: that kings, rulers, tyrants, boasters, and those who have conquered in wars, stripped of all things when they depart from here, suffer pain, lodged in eternal misery."

37 And having said this, John by the power of God healed all the diseases.

[This sentence must be a shortening of a much longer account. The manuscript shows no break here, but we must suppose a considerable loss of text. For one thing, Andronicus, who is an unbeliever here, appears as a convert a few lines later: he is, as will be seen, the husband of an eminent believer, Drusiana, and his and her conversion must have been told at some length. — James]

37 (continued) Now the brethren from Miletus said to John: "We have stayed a long time at Ephesus; if it seems good to you, let us go to Smyrna as well, for we already hear that the mighty works of God have reached there too." And Andronicus said to them: "Whenever the teacher wishes, let us go then." But John said: "Let us first go to the temple of Artemis, for there too, if we show ourselves, the servants of the Lord may perhaps be found."

[The temple of Artemis]

38 Two days later, then, was the birthday festival of the idol temple. John, therefore, when everyone was dressed in white, alone put on black clothing and went up into the temple. And they seized him and tried to kill him. But John said: "You are mad to set on me, a man who is the servant of the only God." And he got up onto a high pedestal and said to them:

39 "You run the risk, men of Ephesus, of being like the sea in character: every river that flows in and every spring that runs down, and the rains, and waves pressing on each other, and torrents full of rocks — all are made salt together by the bitter element that is in it. So you also, remaining unchanged to this day toward true godliness, have been corrupted by your ancient rites of worship. How many wonders and healings of diseases have you seen worked through me? And yet you are blinded in your hearts and cannot recover your sight. What is it, then, men of Ephesus? I have ventured now and come up even into this idol temple of yours. I will prove you most godless, and dead to understanding. Look, I stand here: you all say you have a goddess, Artemis. Pray to her, then, that I alone may die; or else I alone — if you cannot do this — will call on my own God, and for your unbelief I will cause every one of you to die."

40 But those who had already made trial of him and had seen the dead raised up cried out: "Do not kill us so, we beg you, John. We know you can do it." And John said to them: "If then you do not desire to die, let what you worship be brought to nothing — and when it is brought to nothing, you too depart from your ancient error. For now is the time either for you to be converted by my God, or for me myself to die by your goddess; for I will pray in your presence and entreat my God that mercy be shown to you."

41 And having said this, he prayed in this way: "O God, who are God above all who are called gods; who until this day have been treated with contempt in the city of the Ephesians; who put it into my mind to come into this place, of which I had never thought; who convict every form of worship by turning men to yourself; at whose name every idol flees and every evil spirit and every unclean power — now also, by the flight at your name of the evil spirit here, even of the one who deceives this great crowd, show your mercy in this place, for they have been led astray."

42 And as John spoke these words, immediately the altar of Artemis split into many pieces, and all the offerings in the temple fell, and […] was torn apart, and likewise more than seven of the images of the gods. And half the temple fell down, so that the priest was killed at one blow by the falling roof-beam. The crowd of the Ephesians therefore cried out: "One is the God of John, one is the God who has pity on us, for you alone are God! Now we have turned to you, seeing your marvelous works — have mercy on us, O God, according to your will, and save us from our great error!" And some of them, lying face down, made supplication; some knelt and begged; some tore their clothes and wept; and others tried to escape.

43 But John spread out his hands, and lifted up in soul, said to the Lord: "Glory to you, my Jesus, the only God of truth, because you gain your servants by various means." And having said this, he said to the people: "Rise up from the floor, men of Ephesus, and pray to my God, and recognize the unseen power that comes into manifestation, and the wonderful works worked before your eyes. Artemis ought to have helped herself; her servant ought to have been helped by her and not have died. Where is the power of the evil spirit? Where are her sacrifices? Where are her birthdays? Where her festivals? Where the garlands? Where is all that sorcery, and the witchcraft that is its sister?"

44 But the people, rising from the floor, went quickly and threw down the rest of the idol temple, crying: "The God of John — him only we know, and him we will worship from now on, since he has had mercy on us!" And as John came down from there, much people took hold of him, saying: "Help us, John! Assist us who perish for nothing! You see our purpose; you see the crowd following you and hanging on you in hope toward your God. We have seen the way in which we went astray when we lost him; we have seen our gods set up in vain; we have seen the great and shameful mockery that has come to them. But allow us, we beg you, to come to your house and be helped without hindrance. Receive us who are in bewilderment."

45 And John said to them: "Men of Ephesus, believe that for your sakes I have stayed in Ephesus, and put off my journey to Smyrna and the other cities, so that there too the servants of Christ might turn to him. But since I am not yet fully assured about you, I have gone on praying to my God and begging him that I should leave Ephesus only when I have confirmed you in the faith. And since I see that this has come to pass and more is being fulfilled, I will not leave you until I have weaned you like children from the nurse's milk and set you on a firm rock."

46 John therefore continued with them, lodging in the house of Andronicus. And one of those who had gathered laid the dead body of the priest of Artemis down before the door and came quickly in with the rest, saying nothing of it. John, therefore, after speaking to the brethren, and the prayer and the thanksgiving and the laying of hands on every one of the congregation, said by the Spirit: "There is one here who, moved by faith in God, has laid the priest of Artemis down before the gate and come in, and in the yearning of his soul, taking thought for himself first, has thought this within himself: 'It is better for me to take thought for the living than for my dead kinsman; for I know that if I turn to the Lord and save my own soul, John will not refuse to raise the dead also.'" And John, rising from his place, went to the man who had come in, the kinsman of the priest who had thought that, and took him by the hand and said: "Did you have this thought when you came to me, my child?" And he, seized with trembling and fright, said "Yes, lord," and threw himself at his feet. And John said: "Our Lord is Jesus Christ, who will show his power in your dead kinsman by raising him up."

47 And he made the young man stand up, and took his hand and said: "It is no great matter for a man who is master of great mysteries to keep wearing himself out over small things; or what great thing is it to rid men of diseases of the body?" And holding the young man by the hand he said: "I tell you, child, go and raise up the dead man yourself, saying nothing but this: 'John the servant of God says to you: Rise.'" And the young man went to his kinsman and said this only — and much people was with him — and came back in to John, bringing the dead man alive. And John, when he saw the man who had been raised, said: "Now that you are raised, you do not truly live, nor are you yet a partaker or heir of the true life. Will you belong to him by whose name and power you were raised? Believe now, and you shall live for all ages." And he believed in the Lord Jesus at once, and from then on clung to John.

[Another manuscript (Paris Greek 1468, eleventh century) has a different form of this story. John destroys the temple of Artemis, and then "we" go to Smyrna and all the idols are broken; Bucolus, Polycarp, and Andronicus are left to preside over the district. There were two priests of Artemis there, brothers, and one died; the raising is told much as in the older text, but more shortly. "We" remained four years in the region, which was wholly converted, and then returned to Ephesus. — James]

[The young man who killed his father]

48 Now on the next day, John, having seen in a dream that he must walk three miles outside the gates, did not neglect it, but rose early and set out on the road, together with the brethren.

And a certain countryman, who had been warned by his father not to take to himself the wife of a fellow laborer who threatened to kill him — this young man would not endure his father's warning, but kicked him and left him speechless, dead. And John, seeing what had happened, said to the Lord: "Lord, was it for this reason that you told me to come out here today?"

49 But the young man, seeing death closing in and expecting to be seized, drew the sickle that was in his belt and started to run to his own home. And John met him and said: "Stand still, you most shameless devil, and tell me where you are running, carrying a sickle thirsty for blood."

And the young man was troubled and threw the iron on the ground, and said to him: "I have done a wretched and barbarous deed, and I know it, and so I have decided to do an evil still worse and more cruel — to die myself at once. For because my father was always curbing me toward sobriety, so that I would live without adultery, and chastely, I could not endure his reproving me, and I kicked him and killed him. And when I saw what was done, I was hurrying to the woman for whose sake I became my father's murderer, intending to kill her and her husband, and myself last of all — for I could not bear to be seen by the woman's husband and undergo the judgment of death."

50 And John said to him: "So that I may not, by going away and leaving you in danger, give room to the one who wants to laugh and sport with you, come with me and show me your father, where he lies. And if I raise him up for you, will you from now on keep away from the woman who has become a snare to you?" And the young man said: "If you raise my father himself alive for me, and I see him whole and going on in life, I will keep away from her from now on."

51 And while he spoke, they came to the place where the old man lay dead, and many passersby stood near. And John said to the youth: "You wretched man — did you not spare even your father's old age?" And he, weeping and tearing his hair, said that he repented of it. And John the servant of the Lord said: "You showed me, Lord, that I was to set out for this place; you knew this would happen — you from whom nothing done in life can be hidden — and you give me power to work every cure and healing by your will. Now also give me this old man alive; for you see that his murderer has become his own judge. And spare him, O only Lord, who did not spare the one who counseled his father for the best." [The Greek of this clause is obscure. — James]

52 And with these words he came near to the old man and said: "My Lord will not be too weak to spread out his kind pity and his condescending mercy even to you. Rise up, therefore, and give glory to God for what has come to pass at this moment." And the old man said: "I rise, Lord." And he rose and sat up and said: "I was released from a terrible life — I had to bear the insults of my son, dreadful and many, and his lack of natural affection — and to what end have you called me back, O man of the living God?" And John answered him: "If you are raised only for the same end, it would be better for you to die; but raise yourself to better things." And he took him and led him into the city, preaching the grace of God to him, so that before he entered the gate the old man believed.

53 But the young man, when he saw the unexpected raising of his father, and his own deliverance, took a sickle and mutilated himself, and ran to the house where he kept his adulteress, and reproached her, saying: "For your sake I became the murderer of my father, and of you two, and of myself — there you have what is guilty of it all. For God has had mercy on me, that I should know his power."

54 And he came back and told John, in the presence of the brethren, what he had done. But John said to him: "The one who put it into your heart, young man, to kill your father and become the adulterer of another man's wife — that same one made you think it a right deed to take away the unruly members as well. But you should have done away not with the place of sin, but with the thought which showed itself harmful through those members; for it is not the instruments that are harmful, but the unseen springs by which every shameful impulse is stirred up and comes to light. Repent, therefore, my child, of this fault, and having learned the wiles of Satan, you shall have God to help you in every need of your soul." And the young man was silent and attentive, having repented of his former sins, so that he should obtain pardon from the goodness of God. And he did not separate from John.

55 When, then, these things had been done by him in the city of the Ephesians, the people of Smyrna sent to him, saying: "We hear that the God whom you preach is not envious, and has charged you not to show favoritism by staying in one place. Since, then, you are a preacher of such a God, come to Smyrna and to the other cities, so that we may come to know your God, and having known him, may have our hope in him."

[The Paris manuscript has the story just told also, and continues with an incident which is also quoted in a different form — and not as from these Acts — by John Cassian. In one form John gently rebukes a priest who is scandalized that so great a man takes pleasure in watching a partridge playing in the dust before him, saying: "It would be better for you too, my child, to watch a partridge playing in the dust and not defile yourself with shameful and profane practices; for he who waits for the conversion and repentance of all men has brought you here for this reason — for the partridge is your own soul." Cassian (Conference XXIV.21) tells it with the hunter and his bow: a bow cannot be kept always stretched, or its strength is lost when the shot is needed; "just so, let not this little and brief relaxation of my mind offend you, young man." — adapted from James's note]

[The amount of text lost here is quite uncertain in length. It must have told of the doings at Smyrna, and also, it appears, at Laodicea. One of the episodes must have been the conversion of a woman of evil life — "the harlot who was chaste." — James]

Our best manuscript prefixes a title to the next section: "From Laodicea to Ephesus the second time."

[The second coming to Ephesus; the bugs]

58 Now when a long time had passed, and none of the brethren had ever been grieved by John, they were then grieved because he had said: "Brethren, it is now time for me to go to Ephesus — for so I have agreed with those who dwell there — in case they grow slack, now that they have had no one to confirm them for a long time. But all of you must keep your minds steadfast toward God, who never forsakes us."

But when they heard this from him, the brethren lamented because they were to be parted from him. And John said: "Even if I am parted from you, Christ is always with you; and if you love him purely, you will have his fellowship without reproach, for if he is loved, he himself comes first to those who love him."

59 And having said this, and bid farewell to them, and left much money with the brethren for distribution, he went out to Ephesus, while all the brethren lamented and groaned. And there went with him from Ephesus Andronicus and Drusiana and Lycomedes and Cleobius and their families; and there followed him also Aristobula, who had heard that her husband Tertullus had died on the way, and Aristippus with Xenophon, and the harlot who was chaste, and many others, whom he exhorted at all times to cling to the Lord; and they would no more be parted from him.

60 Now on the first day we arrived at a deserted inn, and when we were at a loss for a bed for John, we saw a delightful thing. There was one bedstead lying somewhere there without coverings, on which we spread the cloaks we were wearing, and we asked him to lie down on it and rest, while the rest of us all slept on the floor. But when he lay down he was troubled by the bugs; and as they kept becoming more troublesome to him, when it was now about the middle of the night, in the hearing of us all, he said to them: "I say to you, O bugs: behave yourselves, one and all, and leave your abode for this night and stay quiet in one place, and keep your distance from the servants of God." And as we laughed, and went on talking for some time, John settled himself to sleep; and we, talking low, gave him no disturbance — or rather, thanks to him, we were not disturbed.

61 But when day was now dawning, I arose first, and with me Verus and Andronicus, and we saw at the door of the house we had taken a great number of bugs standing; and while we wondered at the great sight of them, and all the brethren were roused because of them, John went on sleeping. And when he was awake, we told him what we had seen. And he sat up on the bed and looked at them and said: "Since you have behaved well in listening to my rebuke, come to your place." And when he had said this, and risen from the bed, the bugs, running from the door, hurried to the bed and climbed up its legs and disappeared into the joints. And John said again: "This creature listened to the voice of a man, and stayed by itself and was quiet and did no wrong; but we who hear the voice and commandments of God disobey and are fickle — and for how long?"

62 After these things we came to Ephesus; and the brethren there, who had long known that John was coming, ran together to the house of Andronicus — where he also came to lodge — touching his feet and laying his hands on their own faces and kissing them. (Many rejoiced even to touch his clothing, and were healed by touching the holy apostle's clothes.)

63 And although there was great love and unsurpassed joy among the brethren, a certain man — a messenger of Satan — fell in love with Drusiana, though he saw and knew she was the wife of Andronicus. Many said to him: "It is impossible for you to obtain that woman, seeing that for a long time she has even separated herself from her husband for the sake of godliness. Are you the only one who doesn't know that Andronicus, who was not formerly what he now is — a God-fearing man — once shut her up in a tomb, saying: 'Either I must have you as the wife I had before, or you shall die'? And she chose rather to die than do that foulness. If, then, she would not consent, for godliness' sake, to live with her own lord and husband as a wife, but even persuaded him to be of the same mind as herself — will she consent to you, who desire to be her seducer? Give up this madness, which has no rest in you; give up this deed which you cannot accomplish."

64 But though his close friends said these things to him, they did not convince him; but with shamelessness he courted her with messages. And when she learned of the insults and disgrace he returned her [the Greek is uncertain], she lived in heaviness; and after two days Drusiana took to her bed from heaviness, and was in a fever, and said: "Would that I had not now come home to my native place, I who have become an offense to a man ignorant of godliness! For if it had been one filled with the word of God, he would not have gone to such a pitch of madness. But now, Lord, since I have become the occasion of a blow to a soul empty of knowledge, set me free from this chain and remove me quickly to yourself." And in the presence of John — who as yet knew nothing at all of such a matter — Drusiana departed from life, not wholly happy; indeed, troubled because of the spiritual hurt done to the man.

65 But Andronicus, grieved with a secret grief, mourned in his soul and wept openly, so that John often checked him and said to him: "Drusiana has departed from this unrighteous life to a better hope." And Andronicus answered him: "Yes, I am convinced of it, John, and I do not doubt at all in trust in my God; but this very thing I hold fast: that she departed from life pure."

66 And when she was carried out, John took hold of Andronicus; and now that he knew the cause, he mourned more than Andronicus. And he was silent, considering the provocation of the adversary, and sat still for a while. Then, the brethren being gathered there to hear what word he would speak about her who had departed, he began to say:

67 "When the pilot who voyages with those sailing with him, and the ship herself, arrives in a calm and stormless harbor — then let him say that he is safe. And the farmer who has entrusted the seed to the earth, and toiled much in caring for and protecting it — let him then rest from his labors, when he lays up the seed with manyfold increase in his barns. Let the one who undertakes to run in the race then exult when he carries home the prize; let the one who writes his name down for the boxing then boast when he receives the crowns. And so it is in succession with all contests and crafts, when they do not fail at the end.

68 "And so also, I think, is it with the faith which each one of us practices: it is then discerned whether it is truly faith, when it continues true to itself even to the end of life. For many obstacles fall in the way and prepare disturbance for the minds of men: care, children, parents, glory, poverty, flattery, the prime of life, beauty, conceit, lust, wealth, anger, rising pride, slackness, envy, jealousy, neglect, fear, insolence, love, deceit, money, pretense, and other such obstacles as there are in this life — just as the pilot sailing a prosperous course is opposed by contrary winds and a great storm and mighty waves rising out of a calm, and the farmer by an untimely winter and blight and creeping things rising out of the earth, and those who strive in the games 'just fail to win,' and those who exercise crafts are hindered by their various difficulties.

69 "But above all things it is necessary that the believer look ahead to his end, and understand in what manner it will come upon him — whether it will be vigorous and sober and free of any obstacle, or disturbed and clinging to the things that are here, and bound down by desires. So it is right for a body to be praised as beautiful when it is wholly stripped; and a general as great when he has accomplished every promise of the war; and a physician as excellent when he has succeeded in every cure; and a soul as full of faith and worthy of God when it has paid its promise in full. Not the soul that began well and was dissolved into all the things of this life and fell away; not the one that is numb — having made an effort to attain better things, then borne down to temporal things; not the one that has longed after the things of time more than those of eternity; not the one that exchanges enduring things for those that do not endure; not the one that has honored works of dishonor that deserve shame; not the one that takes pledges of Satan; not the one that has received the serpent into its own house; not the one that suffers reproach for God's sake and then is ashamed; not the one that says yes with its mouth but does not approve itself in deed — but the one that has prevailed not to be made weak by foul pleasure, not to be overcome by fickleness, not to be caught by the bait of love of money, not to be betrayed by vigor of body or by wrath."

70 And while John was speaking still more to the brethren about despising temporal things in view of the eternal, the man who was in love with Drusiana — inflamed with a horrible lust and possessed by the many-shaped Satan — bribed the steward of Andronicus, who was a lover of money, with a great sum of money. And he opened the tomb and gave him the chance to work the forbidden thing on her dead body. Not having succeeded with her in life, he was still urgent after her death toward her body, and said: "If you would not have to do with me while you lived, I will outrage your corpse now that you are dead." With this design, having arranged the wicked act for himself by means of the abominable steward, he rushed with him to the tomb. They opened the door and began to strip the grave-clothes from the corpse, saying: "What have you gained, poor Drusiana? Could you not have done this in life, which perhaps would not have grieved you, if you had done it willingly?"

71 And as these men were saying this, and only her customary shift now remained on her body, a strange spectacle was seen, such as those deserve who do such deeds. A serpent appeared from somewhere and dealt the steward a single bite and killed him; but the young man it did not strike — instead it coiled about his feet, hissing terribly, and when he fell, it climbed onto his body and sat upon him.

72 Now on the next day, John came, accompanied by Andronicus and the brethren, to the tomb at dawn — it was now the third day from Drusiana's death — so that we might break bread there. And first, when they set out, the keys were searched for and could not be found, but John said to Andronicus: "It is quite right that they should be lost, for Drusiana is not in the tomb; nevertheless, let us go, so that you may not be neglectful, and the doors shall be opened of themselves, as the Lord has done many such things for us."

73 And when we were at the place, at the master's command the doors were opened, and we saw by the tomb of Drusiana a beautiful youth, smiling. And John, when he saw him, cried out and said: "Have you come here before us too, beautiful one? And for what cause?" And we heard a voice saying to him: "For the sake of Drusiana, whom you are going to raise up — for I was within a little of finding her dishonored — and for his sake who lies dead beside her tomb." And when the beautiful one had said this to John, he went up into the heavens in the sight of us all. And John, turning to the other side of the tomb, saw a young man — Callimachus, one of the chief men of the Ephesians — and a huge serpent asleep upon him, and the steward of Andronicus, Fortunatus by name, lying dead. And at the sight of the two he stood perplexed, saying to the brethren: "What does such a sight mean? Or why has the Lord not told me what was done here — he who has never neglected me?"

74 And Andronicus, seeing those corpses, leapt up and went to Drusiana's tomb, and seeing her lying in her shift only, said to John: "Now I understand what has happened, blessed servant of God, John. This Callimachus was in love with my wife, and because he never won her, though he tried it often, he has bribed this accursed steward of mine with a great sum, presumably planning, as we can now see, to carry out the tragedy of his plot by his means. For indeed Callimachus declared this to many, saying: 'If she will not consent to me while living, she shall be outraged when dead.' And it may be, master, that the beautiful one knew it and did not allow her body to be insulted, and therefore these men who made the attempt have died. And can it be that the voice which said to you 'Raise up Drusiana' foreshadowed this, since she departed this life in sorrow of mind? But I believe the one who said that this is one of the men who have gone astray — for you were ordered to raise him up. As for the other, I know he is unworthy of salvation. But this one thing I beg of you: raise Callimachus up first, and he will confess to us what has happened."

75 And John, looking at the body, said to the venomous beast: "Get away from the one who is going to be a servant of Jesus Christ!" And he stood up and prayed over him in this way: "O God, whose name is glorified by us, as is right; O God who subdue every harmful force; O God whose will is accomplished, who always hear us — now also let your gift be accomplished in this young man; and if there is some purpose to be worked through him, make it known to us when he is raised up." And at once the young man rose up, and for a whole hour he kept silence.

76 But when he came to his right senses, John asked him about his entry into the tomb, what it meant; and learning from him what Andronicus had told him — namely that he had been in love with Drusiana — John asked him again whether he had fulfilled his foul intent to insult a body full of holiness. And he answered him: "How could I accomplish it, when this fearful beast struck down Fortunatus with a single blow before my eyes? And rightly — since he encouraged my frenzy when I was already cured of that senseless and horrible madness. But me it stopped with fright, and brought me to the state in which you saw me before I rose. And I will tell you something yet more wondrous, which nearly killed me too and came within a little of making me a corpse. When my soul was stirred up with folly and the uncontrollable sickness was troubling me, and I had torn away the grave-clothes in which she was clothed and had come out of the grave and laid them aside as you see, I went back to my unholy work — and I saw a beautiful youth covering her with his cloak, and sparks of light came from his eyes into her eyes; and he spoke words to me, saying: 'Callimachus, die, so that you may live.' Who he was I did not know, O servant of God; but now that you have appeared here, I recognize that he was an angel of God — that I know well. And this I know truly: that it is a true God who is proclaimed by you, and of that I am convinced. But I beg you, do not be slow to deliver me from this calamity and this fearful crime, and to present me to your God as a man deceived by a shameful and foul deceit. Begging your help, therefore, I take hold of your feet. I would become one of those who hope in Christ, so that the voice may prove true which said to me, 'Die, so that you may live.' And that voice has also fulfilled its effect: for he is dead — that faithless, disorderly, godless man — and I have been raised by you, I who will be faithful, God-fearing, knowing the truth, which I beg you to show me."

77 And John, filled with great gladness and seeing the whole spectacle of man's salvation, said: "What your power is, Lord Jesus Christ, I do not know — bewildered as I am at your great compassion and boundless long-suffering. O what a greatness came down into bondage! O unspeakable freedom brought into slavery by us! O incomprehensible glory that has come to us! You who have kept the dead tabernacle safe from insult; who have redeemed the man who stained himself with blood and chastened the soul of the one who would defile the corruptible body; Father, who have had pity and compassion on the man who cared nothing for you — we glorify you, and praise and bless and thank your great goodness and long-suffering, O holy Jesus, for you alone are God, and no other, whose is the might that cannot be plotted against, now and for ever and ever. Amen."

78 And when he had said this, John took Callimachus and embraced him, saying: "Glory to our God, my child, who has had mercy on you, and made me worthy to glorify his power — and you too, by a good course, to depart from that abominable madness and drunkenness of yours — and has called you to his own rest and to renewal of life."

79 But Andronicus, seeing the dead Callimachus raised, begged John, with the brethren, to raise Drusiana also, saying: "O John, let Drusiana arise and spend happily that short space of life which she gave up through grief about Callimachus, when she thought she had become a stumbling-block to him; and when the Lord wills, he shall take her to himself again." And John without delay went to her tomb and took her hand and said: "You who are the only God I call upon — the more than great, the unutterable, the incomprehensible; to whom every power of the principalities is subject; to whom all authority bows; before whom all pride falls down and is silent; at word of whom devils tremble; perceiving whom, all creation keeps its bounds — let your name be glorified by us, and raise up Drusiana, so that Callimachus may be still more confirmed toward you who dispense what to men is impossible and without a way — but to you alone possible — even salvation and resurrection; and so that Drusiana may now come forth in peace, having not the least hindrance about her — now that the young man has turned to you — in her course toward you."

80 And after these words John said to Drusiana: "Drusiana, rise." And she rose and came out of the tomb; and when she saw herself in her shift only, she was perplexed at the thing, and learned the whole matter accurately from Andronicus, while John lay on his face, and Callimachus with his voice and tears glorified God; and she also rejoiced, glorifying him likewise.

81 And when she had clothed herself, she turned and saw Fortunatus lying there, and said to John: "Father, let this man rise too, even if he did try to become my betrayer." But Callimachus, when he heard her say that, said: "Do not, I beg you, Drusiana — for the voice which I heard took no thought of him, but declared concerning you only; and I saw and believed. For if he had been good, perhaps God would have had mercy on him also and would have raised him by means of the blessed John; he knew, therefore, that the man had come to a bad end — he judged him worthy to die, whom he did not declare worthy to rise again." And John said to him: "We have not learned, my child, to return evil for evil. For God, though we have done much ill and no good toward him, has not returned retribution to us, but repentance; and though we were ignorant of his name, he did not neglect us but had mercy on us; and when we blasphemed him, he did not punish but pitied us; and when we disbelieved him, he bore us no grudge; and when we persecuted his brethren, he did not repay us evil, but put repentance and abstinence from evil into our minds, and urged us to come to him — as he has you too, my son Callimachus — and, not remembering your former evil, has made you his servant, waiting on his mercy. Therefore, if you do not allow me to raise Fortunatus, it is for Drusiana to do it."

82 And she, delaying not, went with rejoicing of spirit and soul to the body of Fortunatus and said: "Jesus Christ, God of the ages, God of truth, who have granted me to see wonders and signs, and given me to become a partaker of your name; who breathed yourself into me with your many-shaped countenance, and had mercy on me in many ways; who protected me by your great goodness when I was oppressed by Andronicus, who was formerly my husband; who gave me your servant Andronicus to be my brother; who have kept me, your handmaid, pure to this day; who raised me up by your servant John; and when I was raised, showed me the one who was made to stumble, free from stumbling; who have given me perfect rest in you, and lightened me of the secret madness — whom I have loved and cherished: I pray you, O Christ, do not refuse your Drusiana, who asks you to raise up Fortunatus, even though he tried to become my betrayer."

83 And taking the hand of the dead man, she said: "Rise up, Fortunatus, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ." And Fortunatus rose; and when he saw John in the tomb, and Andronicus, and Drusiana raised from the dead, and Callimachus a believer, and the rest of the brethren glorifying God, he said: "Oh — to what have the powers of these clever men attained! I did not want to be raised; I would rather die, than see them." And with these words he fled and went out of the tomb.

84 And John, when he saw the unchanged mind of Fortunatus, said: "O nature that is not changed for the better! O fountain of the soul that remains in foulness! O essence of corruption full of darkness! O death exulting in those who are yours! O fruitless tree full of fire! O tree that bears coals for fruit! O neighbor of unbelief! You have proved who you are, and you are always convicted, together with your children. And you do not know how to praise the better things — for you do not have them. Therefore, such as your way is, such also is your root and your nature. Be destroyed from among those who trust in the Lord — from their thoughts, from their minds, from their souls, from their bodies, from their acts, their lives, their conduct, their business, their occupations, their counsel, from the resurrection and rest in God, from the sweet savor of the holy bath, from the eucharist, from the food of the flesh, from drink, from clothing, from love, from care, from abstinence, from righteousness — from all these, you most unholy Satan, enemy of God, shall Jesus Christ our God, the judge of all who are like you and have your character, make you to perish."

85 And having said this, John prayed, and took bread and carried it into the tomb to break it, and said: "We glorify your name, which converts us from error and ruthless deceit. We glorify you who have shown before our eyes what we have seen. We bear witness to your loving-kindness, which appears in various ways. We praise your merciful name, O Lord; we thank you, who have convicted those who are convicted by you. We give thanks to you, O Lord Jesus Christ, that we are convinced of your unchanging grace. We give thanks to you, who had need of our nature that was to be saved. We give thanks to you, who have given us this sure faith, for you are God alone, both now and ever. We your servants give you thanks, O holy one, who are assembled with good intent and are gathered out of the world."

86 And having prayed in this way and given glory to God, he went out of the tomb after giving to all the brethren of the eucharist of the Lord. And when he had come to Andronicus' house, he said to the brethren: "Brethren, a spirit within me has divined that Fortunatus is about to die of the blackening — the poisoning of the blood — from the bite of the serpent; but let someone go quickly and find out if it is really so." And one of the young men ran and found him dead, the blackness spreading over him, and it had reached his heart; and he came and told John that he had been dead three hours. And John said: "You have your child, O devil."

[A sentence which follows in the best manuscript — "John therefore was with the brethren, rejoicing in the Lord" — introduces, in Bonnet's edition, the last section of the Acts, which follows immediately in the manuscript; it may belong to either episode. There cannot be much of a gap between this and the next section, which is perhaps the most interesting in the whole Acts. [The Lord's many shapes — John's own memories]

87 Those who were present asked the reason, and were perplexed in their minds: "For the Lord appeared to me [Callimachus] in the tomb in the likeness of John, and also in that of a youth." Because, therefore, they were perplexed and were, in a way, not yet established in the faith so as to bear it steadfastly, John, bearing it patiently, said:

88 "Men and brethren, you have suffered nothing strange or unbelievable as concerns your perception of the Lord, seeing that we also, whom he chose for himself to be apostles, were tried in many ways. I, indeed, am neither able to set forth to you nor to write the things which I both saw and heard. And now it is needful that I should fit them to your hearing; and according as each of you is able to contain it, I will impart to you the things of which you are able to become hearers, so that you may see the glory that is about him, which was and is, both now and for ever.

"For when he had chosen Peter and Andrew, who were brothers, he came to me and James my brother, saying: 'I have need of you; come to me.' And my brother, hearing that, said: 'John, what does this child on the seashore want, who called us?' And I said: 'What child?' And he said to me again: 'The one beckoning to us.' And I answered: 'Because of the long watch we have kept at sea, you do not see right, my brother James; but do you not see the man standing there, handsome and fair and of a cheerful countenance?' But he said to me: 'Him I do not see, brother; but let us go ashore and we shall see what he wants.'

89 "And so when we had brought the ship to land, we saw him also helping along with us to settle the ship. And when we left that place, intending to follow him, again he appeared to me as rather bald of head, but with a thick and flowing beard, and to James as a youth whose beard was newly come. We were therefore perplexed, both of us, about what the thing we had seen could mean. And after that, as we followed him, both of us, little by little, were yet more perplexed as we considered the matter. Yet to me there then appeared this yet more wonderful thing: I would try to see him privately, and I never at any time saw his eyes closing — only open. And often he would appear to me as a small man and plain-looking, and then again as one reaching up to heaven. There was also another marvel in him: when I sat at meat, he would take me onto his own breast; and sometimes his breast felt smooth and tender to me, and sometimes hard like stones, so that I was perplexed in myself and said: 'Why is this so, for me?' And as I considered this, he…

90 "And at another time he took with him me and James and Peter up to the mountain where he used to pray, and we saw such a light upon him as it is not possible for a man who uses mortal speech to describe [the sentence is defective in the manuscript]. And again in the same way he brought us three up into the mountain, saying: 'Come with me.' And we went again, and we saw him at a distance, praying. I, therefore, because he loved me, drew near to him quietly, as if he could not see me, and stood looking at him from behind. And I saw that he was not clothed with garments at all, but was seen by us naked, and in no way as a man; and his feet were whiter than any snow, so that the earth there was lit up by his feet, and his head touched the heaven — so that I was afraid and cried out. And he, turning around, appeared as a man of small stature, and took hold of my beard and pulled it and said to me: 'John, be not faithless but believing, and not curious.' And I said to him: 'But what have I done, Lord?' And I tell you, brethren, I suffered such great pain in that place where he took hold of my beard, for thirty days, that I said to him: 'Lord, if your tug in play has given me such pain, what would it be if you had given me a blow?' And he said to me: 'Let it be your concern from now on not to tempt the One who cannot be tempted.'

91 "But Peter and James were angry because I spoke with the Lord, and signed to me to come to them and leave the Lord alone. And I went, and they both said to me: 'That old man who was speaking with the Lord on the top of the mountain — who was he? For we heard both of them speaking.' And I, keeping in mind his great grace, and his oneness that has many faces, and his wisdom that ceaselessly watches over us, said: 'That you shall learn, if you ask him.'

92 "Again, once when all of us his disciples were at Gennesaret, sleeping in one house, I alone, having wrapped myself in my cloak, watched what he would do. And first I heard him say: 'John, go to sleep.' And then I, pretending to sleep, saw another like him, whom I also heard say to my Lord: 'Jesus, those whom you have chosen do not yet believe in you.' And my Lord said to him: 'You say well, for they are men.'

93 "Another glory also I will tell you, brethren. Sometimes when I would lay hold on him, I met with a material and solid body; and at other times again, when I felt him, the substance was immaterial, and as if it did not exist at all. And if at any time he was invited by one of the Pharisees and went to the invitation, we went with him, and one loaf was set before each of us by those who had invited us; and he also received one with us. And his own he would bless and divide among us; and of that little piece everyone was filled, and our own loaves were kept whole, so that those who had invited him were amazed. And often, when I walked with him, I desired to see whether the print of his foot appeared on the earth — for I saw him as it were lifting himself up from the earth — and I never saw it. And these things I speak to you, brethren, for the encouragement of your faith toward him; for at present we must keep silence about his mighty and wonderful works, since they are unspeakable and perhaps cannot be uttered or heard at all."

The Hymn of Jesus

94 Now before he was taken by the lawless Jews, he gathered all of us together and said: "Before I am delivered up to them, let us sing a hymn to the Father, and so go out to what lies before us." He told us, therefore, to make a ring, as it were, holding one another's hands, and he himself, standing in the middle, said: "Answer 'Amen' to me." Then he began to sing a hymn, and to say:

"Glory to you, Father."

[The mystery of the Cross]

97 In this way, my beloved, having danced with us, the Lord went out. And we, like men gone astray or dazed with sleep, fled this way and that. I, then, when I saw him suffer, did not even stay by his suffering, but fled to the Mount of Olives, weeping at what had happened. And when he was crucified on the Friday, at the sixth hour of the day, darkness came over all the earth. And my Lord, standing in the middle of the cave and lighting it up, said: "John, to the crowd below in Jerusalem I am being crucified and pierced with lances and reeds, and gall and vinegar is given me to drink. But to you I speak, and what I speak, hear. I put it into your mind to come up into this mountain, so that you might hear the things which a disciple must learn from his teacher and a man from his God."

98 And having said this, he showed me a cross of light, set up; and around the cross was a great crowd, not having one form; and in the cross was one form and one likeness. And the Lord himself I saw above the cross, having no shape, but only a voice — and a voice not such as was familiar to us, but one sweet and kind and truly God's — saying to me: "John, it is necessary that one should hear these things from me, for I have need of one who will hear. This cross of light is sometimes called by me, for your sakes, the Word; sometimes Mind; sometimes Jesus; sometimes Christ; sometimes a Door; sometimes a Way; sometimes Bread; sometimes Seed; sometimes Resurrection; sometimes Son; sometimes Father; sometimes Spirit; sometimes Life; sometimes Truth; sometimes Faith; sometimes Grace. And by these names it is called as it relates to men; but what it truly is, as conceived in itself and as spoken to you, is the marking-off of all things, and the firm raising-up of things fixed out of things unstable; and the harmony of wisdom, and indeed wisdom in harmony. There are places of the right hand and of the left, powers too, authorities, lordships and demons, workings, threatenings, wraths, devils, Satan, and the lower root from which the nature of the things that come into being proceeded.

99 "This cross, then, is the one which fixed all things apart by the Word, and separated off the things that come into being from the things below it, and then also, being one, streamed forth into all things. But this is not the cross of wood which you will see when you go down from here; neither am I the one who is on the cross, whom now you do not see, but only hear his voice. I was reckoned to be what I am not, not being what I was to many others; but they will call me something else, which is vile and not worthy of me. As, then, the place of rest is neither seen nor spoken of, much more shall I, its Lord, be neither seen nor spoken of.

100 "Now the crowd of one aspect that is around the cross is the lower nature; and those whom you see in the cross, if they do not have one form, that is because not yet has every member of the one who came down been gathered up. But when the human nature is taken up, and the race that draws near to me and obeys my voice — then the one who now hears me shall be united with it, and shall no more be what he is now, but above them, as I also now am. For as long as you do not call yourself mine, I am not what I am; but if you hear me, you, hearing, shall be as I am, and I shall be what I was, when I have you with myself as I am. For from me you are what I am. Do not care, therefore, for the many; and despise those outside the mystery — for know that I am wholly with the Father, and the Father with me.

101 "Nothing, therefore, of the things they will say about me have I suffered. No — even that suffering which I showed you and the rest in the dance, I want it to be called a mystery. For what you are, you see, for I showed it to you; but what I am, I alone know, and no one else. Let me, then, keep what is mine; and what is yours, see through me; and see me truly — what I am, not what I said, but what you are able to know, because you are kin to it. You hear that I suffered, yet I did not suffer; that I did not suffer, yet I suffered; that I was pierced, yet I was not struck; hanged, and I was not hanged; that blood flowed from me, and it did not flow. And in a word: what they say about me did not happen to me; but what they do not say, that I did suffer. Now what those things are, I show you, for I know you will understand. Perceive in me, therefore, the praising of the Word, the piercing of the Word, the blood of the Word, the wound of the Word, the hanging-up of the Word, the suffering of the Word, the nailing of the Word, the death of the Word. And I speak in this way, setting the manhood apart. Perceive, therefore, in the first place the Word; then you shall perceive the Lord; and in the third place the man, and what he has suffered."

102 When he had spoken these things to me — and others which I do not know how to say as he would have me — he was taken up, no one of the multitudes having seen him. And when I came down, I laughed them all to scorn, inasmuch as he had told me the things they had said about him, holding fast this one thing in myself: that the Lord arranged all things symbolically, and by a dispensation toward men, for their conversion and salvation.

103 Having therefore seen, brethren, the grace of the Lord and his kindly affection toward us, let us worship him as those to whom he has shown mercy — not with our fingers, nor our mouth, nor our tongue, nor with any part of the body at all, but with the disposition of our soul — even him who became a man apart from this body. And let us watch, because now also he keeps guard over prisons for our sake, and over tombs — in bonds and dungeons, in reproaches and insults, by sea and on dry land, in scourgings, condemnations, conspiracies, frauds, punishments; and in a word, he is with all of us, and he himself suffers with us when we suffer, brethren. When he is called on by each one of us, he does not endure to shut his ears to us, but, being everywhere, he listens to all of us; and now both to me and to Drusiana — since he is the God of those who are shut up — bringing us help by his own compassion.

104 "Be persuaded, therefore, beloved, that it is not a man whom I preach to you to worship, but God unchangeable, God invincible, God higher than all authority and all power, and older and mightier than all angels and creatures that are named, and all the ages. If, then, you abide in him, and are built up in him, you shall possess your soul indestructible."

105 And when he had delivered these things to the brethren, John departed with Andronicus to walk. And Drusiana also followed at a distance, with all the brethren, so that they might see the deeds done by him, and hear his speech in the Lord at all times.

The poison cup

(Latin XX.) Three fragments

[Here James inserts a notice of three detached fragments of the Acts.]

The first (a) is the upper portion of a papyrus leaf of the fourth century (Oxyrhynchus Papyri no. 850). On one side, a demon disguised as a soldier threatens John — "John, into my hands you shall shortly come" — and is rebuked and vanishes; John is about to pray with the brethren. On the other side, John prays over Zeuxis, who had tried to hang himself, and gives thanks: "…who weepest for the afflicted, who raisest up those who have been put to death, helper of the helpless: Jesus, the comforter of the afflicted — we praise you and worship and give thanks for all your gifts…" The proconsul is present and brings letters from the emperor. The fragment confirms that John was represented as coming before the emperor, with exile or the cauldron of oil as the sequel.

The second (b), preserved in the Latin "Epistle of Titus," records devils confessing to Verus the deacon about John's coming: "Many will come to us in the last times to turn us out of our vessels, saying that they are pure and clean from women and are not held by desire for them — whom, if we desired, we could possess also."

The third (c), also from the Epistle of Titus, is a vehement speech of John's against marriage ("Little children, while your flesh is yet pure… know the mystery of the nuptial union: it is the experiment of the serpent, the ignorance of teaching, the injury of the seed, the gift of death…" — thirty-one clauses follow, many corrupt), ending with the call to wait for "the one true incomparable bridegroom from heaven, Christ." James calls it the most avowedly Encratite passage in the Acts. A third quotation in the same Epistle is taken from an extant part of the Acts — the last prayer of John, below.

The death of John
