## The six faults and their repairs

**Fault 1 — legible James pages declared "garbled," text replaced with summaries and gaps; referent reversed; wrong city; content dropped; Latin XVI reduced to a paraphrase.** Repaired throughout the Craton passage (James's pp. 257–262):
- The three false notes are deleted: "[The djvu OCR of the Latin leaves is severely garbled at this point (see NOTES)…]" (the "(see NOTES)" pointed to no notes anywhere), "[Further OCR-garbled gospel-of-the-rich-man paraphrase and rebuke…]", and "[The OCR of the Latin text grows severely corrupt in this part…]". What was garbled was a scan's OCR layer, never James's page.
- Chapter XIV's ending restored: the faithful's "Amen" stands unbracketed, and Craton falls at the apostle's feet **with his disciples**, "believed from then on and was baptized, with them all" — the disciples and the baptism the shelf had dropped are back, and the doubt-brackets "[Amen]" and "[and believed]" are gone.
- Latin XV, first paragraph: the two honorable men are again "of the city of **the Ephesians**" (the shelf had made them men "of the city of Pergamum"; Pergamum is the city they later entered).
- Latin XV, second paragraph (the desk's headline sub-fault, the reversed referent): restored whole from James. It is **their servants** who walk abroad "arrayed in silken raiment and shining with the glory of this world"; the two men see **themselves** "poor and clad in a single cloak while their own servants were powerful and prosperous." All seven "[…]" gaps in the old paragraph are filled with James's sentences: the rods brought and turned to gold, the pebbles from the seashore turned to gems, the seven days at the goldsmiths' and jewelers' shops, and their report ("they never saw such pure gold… such excellent and precious gems"). The doubt-brackets "sticks [became gold]" and "[has appointed]" disappear with the restored text.
- Latin XVI restored under its own heading, which the book previously lacked entirely: John's sermon opening ("Go, and redeem to you the lands which you have sold, for you have lost the estates of heaven. Buy yourselves silken raiment, that for a time you may shine like the rose…"), the rich man and Lazarus told in full, and the recollection of the mighty works ("you drove out devils and restored light to the blind… this grace is taken from you"), running into the "But wretched and unhappy is the man…" paragraph that already stood in the file.
- Latin XVII completed and Latin XVIII set in as text. The old file ended XVII with the bracket "[he recalled the young man to life]" and replaced what followed — Stacteus's report of what he saw, and chapter XVIII's opening — with one summary bracket. Both are now James's text: the loosing of the swathed body, John's address to Stacteus, Stacteus's speech (the weeping angels, the dwelling-places of shining stones, the souls "blotted out of the book of life"), then XVIII whole (the thirty days of penitence and Atticus and Eugenius's plea). Recorded openly: the desk check enumerated the XVIII summary bracket expressly and XVII's bracket only by its place in the passage; restoring XVII's ending was necessary to remove the summary without deleting the episode's content, since the summary had carried Stacteus's report inside it. The paragraph after XVIII (the holy John's answer) stands as the shelf had it; its wording was not separately audited.
- Chapter 42 (part 1): the gap "[…] was torn apart" is replaced with James's bracketed manuscript reading as text, "and that which seemed good to him was torn apart."

**Fault 2 — chapters 109–111 lost under a false "illegible" note; invented text in their place.** The note "[The Greek text and the copy before us are disordered and partly illegible in this passage (see Translator Notes)…]" is deleted (no Translator Notes exist; James's printed p. 268 is legible and complete). In part 2 the Death of John now runs 106, 107, 108, 109, 110, 111, 112, 113, 114, 115, every chapter present as text and numbered:
- 109 restored whole from James, ending where James ends it ("…being portrayed in your manhood only"). The substituted list is removed: the seven words that are not in James — "your son, your vessel, your altar, your ass, your flower, [your] star, [your] stone" — are gone, and James's actual list stands, including "thy way," which the shelf had dropped, and the full close of the thanksgiving (about two-thirds of the chapter had been cut).
- 110 set in as text: the breaking of the bread, the prayer over each brother, John's own partaking, and "Unto me also be there a part with you, and: Peace be with you, my beloved."
- 111 set in as text: Verus takes two men with baskets and shovels; John goes out of the house and out of the gates and has the trench dug deeper and deeper while he exhorts **those who had come with him out of the house** (the shelf's "[of the city]" carried the wrong word, in a clause it had lifted out of the digging scene it had deleted). Chapter 115's "the trench where he had spread his garments" now has the event it refers to.
- 112's opening restored whole: the shelf had begun "[The prayer continued:] …who proclaimed yourself even among beasts," a clause from the middle; James's opening now stands from "O you who chose us out for the apostleship of the Gentiles" through "…receive also the soul of your servant John."
- 113 now carries its chapter number (its text stood unnumbered in the old file); see Fault 3 for its filled gaps.

**Fault 3 — "[…]" gaps and "[ ]" doubt-brackets on legible words.** All fifteen gap tokens the desk counted are resolved: seven in the old Latin XV paragraph, and the rest in part 2 (the invented distribution sentence "[…] And so let each of you be made worthy," gone with Fault 2's restoration; 113's "who […] and banished him"; 114's "Let angels […]" → "Let angels follow," and "let those of the left hand […]" → "let them of the left hand not remain"). The doubt-brackets on legible words are gone: "[and believed]," "[Amen]," "[became gold]," "[has appointed]," "[your] star," "[your] stone," "[of the city]," "[of sin]," "[toward you]," "[service]." One same-line alignment is recorded for the eyeball gate: in 113, the clause the shelf rendered "who made my journey to you free" is restored to James's "who made my joining to you perfect and unbroken," the line being under repair in any case. One gap stands, deliberately: at chapter 30 the old file's "[…]" ("some are […] and some palsied") reproduces a lacuna James himself marks in print ("some . . . . (a word gone)") — the word is gone in James's own source, so a marked gap is the honest state there and is kept.

**Fault 4 — the Hymn clause dropped (obelized in James).** Chapter 94 again opens as James prints it: "Now before he was taken by the lawless Jews, **who also were governed by (had their law from) the lawless serpent**, he gathered all of us together…" The clause itself, not merely its corruption marks, had been silently absent; it is restored in James's words, his parenthetical alternative carried as he prints it.

**Fault 5 — leftover template text.** Part 1 line 5, "Rare & Obscure Books series — No. ___," stood with the series number never filled in. No series number for this book exists in the shelf index (the number column is blank for this row), so the placeholder is removed rather than filled with an invented number; the line now reads "Rare & Obscure Books series."

**Fault 6 — no Internet Archive identifier named.** The Sources section now names the Internet Archive copies of the 1924 Clarendon Press edition — identifiers `apocryphalnewtes00unse` and `apocryphalnewtes0000unse_b9n3` (archive.org) — together with the Wesley Center transcription used for this repair, so a reader can retrace the source. The Sources paragraph's false sentence about the Craton episode ("the corrupt Latin leaves… are rendered from their legible substance and gaps are marked, not filled") is deleted and replaced with a true one recording the restoration.

**Front matter (Basis and Sources) rewritten to be true of the repaired file.** The Basis paragraph now names the Latin supplements as XIV–XXI, states that chapters 106–115 stand "all present as text," and records the 25 October 2026 repair and what it restored.
