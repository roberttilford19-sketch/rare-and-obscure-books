# The Life of St. Pelagia the Penitent

**as told by Jacob the Deacon (BHG 1478), fifth century**
A fresh English translation, plain and complete

---

**A fresh translation, not a reprint.** The Greek *Life of St.
Pelagia the Penitent* — the story as told by "Jacob the Deacon,"
that begins with Bishop Nonnus preaching at Antioch and ends with
the brothers on the Mount of Olives discovering that the dead
monk "Brother Pelagius" was a woman — is here rendered whole into
plain English, from beginning to end, sentence by sentence from
the Greek, with nothing left out and nothing invented.

Original: Greek legend, ca. 5th century, attributed to Jacob the
Deacon. Basis for this rendering: Hermann Usener's critical
edition, *Legenden der Pelagia* (Bonn, 1879), as digitized by the
Internet Archive, identifier `legendenderpela01pelagoog`
(`legendenderpela01pelagoog_djvu.txt`, 3,884 lines, status
NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT, read 2026-10-11) — the Greek text (OCR lines
~1299–2470) with its Leiden-manuscript variants printed beneath.

**How it is presented:** The Greek in clear modern English, plain
enough for a humble reader to follow. The story is told as a
single continuous narrative, in the order Jacob gives it: the
preface, the bishops' council at Antioch, Nonnus's tears over the
actress Pelagia passing in the street, her conversion, her
baptism, the giving away of her gold, her flight to Jerusalem as
"the monk Pelagius," the visit of Jacob the Deacon, and the
discovery at the cell. Where the century-old print or the
digitization defeats a certain reading, the English says what can
be said and marks the rest — [obscured in the scan] — rather

are noted where they change the sense; the rest are left in

**Reader note:** This is a legend, not a court record — presented
as Delehaye and the Catholic Encyclopedia present it, a story of
the fifth century about repentance, told for edification. It is
given here exactly as legend, and the reader is free to weigh it
as such. Names and places are the legend's own: Antioch, Bishop
Nonnus, the martyr's shrine of St. Julian, the Mount of Olives.
Nothing here is argued for or against; it is presented as Jacob
(or whoever wrote under his name) told it, for the reader to
judge.

## Dedication

**Pelagia of Antioch** — who she was, as the legend tells it: the
most famous actress of Antioch, "drowned in gold," who walked
through the city with her jewels flashing and a train of boys and
girls in fine clothes behind her. One day Bishop Nonnus, a holy
man come from Egypt to a council at Antioch, watched her pass
with her face uncovered — and wept, and turned his weeping into a
sermon on what care for the soul should look like. She broke
under his preaching, was baptized, gave all her fortune to the
poor, and disappeared from Antioch. She shut herself into a small
cell on the Mount of Olives as the monk "Brother Pelagius" — so
thin and wasted by fasting that nobody knew Pelagius was a woman
until, after her death, the brothers washed the corpse. (So the
legend tells it; so the scout's documented account gives it; the
hard-luck story is carried here in the legend's own telling, not
as proved history.)

Its relation to this rescue: the Boneyard gathers the stories
that would otherwise be lost inside the languages that hold them,
and this is one of the great repentance stories of the early
Church — a fifth-century life of a fallen woman become a desert
saint, told by a deacon who claims to have seen it with his own
eyes. It has lived for fifteen centuries in Greek, Syriac, and
Latin, but not in plain English free to all readers. The series
gives Pelagia back her voice, sentence by sentence from the
Greek, in English a humble reader can read.

## The Repentance of Holy Pelagia

**written by the deacon Jacob**

---

The miracle that took place in our days seemed good to me, Jacob
the unworthy deacon, to write down for you, my spiritual
brothers — so that when you hear it you may gain much spiritual
profit, glorify the God who loves mankind, and want no one to
perish but all sinners to be saved. [For truly this story seems
to me profitable and fitting for what is being said: for it is
full of benefit and compunction, this little book.]

Once the archbishop of Antioch called the bishops around him on
account of some business. And there came together bishops to the
number of eight, among whom was also the holy man of God Nonnus,
who was also bishop — a wonderful man and an ascetic, who had
come out of the monastery of Tabennesi. For he had left his own
life and his manly conduct [there], and abandoned the common
order. So when they had come together in Antioch, the archbishop
ordered us to stay in the martyr's shrine of St. Julian. And we
went in there and sat down with the other bishops.

Now one day the bishops were sitting together before the gate of
the martyr's shrine, and they asked our lord Nonnus to speak and
benefit them. And he was speaking through the Holy Spirit with
grace from his lips.

Behold, suddenly there passes by us the chief of the actresses of
Antioch — [and she was also the first of the dancers of the
theater]. And she sat in a carriage, adorned with great splendor,
so that nothing was to be seen on her except gold and pearls and
precious stones, and the nakedness of her feet was adorned with
[gold and] pearls. And there was a great splendor of boys and of
girls with her, in rich clothing — some running before her, some
following after her. And as for the adornment about her and the
gaiety, there was not enough room, especially for the magistrates
of the city: she passed through our midst, and filled all the air
with the fragrance of the musk and of the perfumes. Now when the
choir of the holy bishops saw her with her face uncovered,
walking with such boldness, so that not [even her precious]
garment was drawn around her head but hung down on her
shoulders, as if by great shamelessness they had turned away
their faces — the holy man of God Nonnus fixed the eyes of his
mind sharply upon her, until she had passed by and he could see
her no longer, and he laid his face upon his knees and filled his
whole bosom with tears, and he sighed deeply and said to the
bishops sitting with him: "Tell me, did the beauty of that woman
not delight you?" And they kept silence and gave him no answer.
And again he lifted his face, and sighing and striking his
breast, he filled his whole tunic with tears and wept, saying:
"Then he said, raising his eyes, he says to the bishops: 'Was it
not so, that you did not take delight?' But I was taken and
delighted with her beauty, because God will set her before His
fearful judgment-seat to judge us and our episcopate. Tell me,
beloved, how many hours do you think that wretched woman spent
in her chamber being adorned, being beautified, being decked out
with all care, that she might not miss the proposed mark and
might not appear deformed to her lovers? And this she did to
please men who are here today and gone tomorrow — and we, who
have a bride in the bridal chamber that is not taken away and
abides forever, who have a heavenly and eternal promise of
riches, which eye has not seen and ear has not heard and the
heart of man has not ascended to — which God has prepared for
those who love Him — and what shall I say at length? Having a
promise to behold that divine face forever — and we do not adorn
our own wretched soul."
"And we, having a promise to behold that divine face forever
and that unutterable beauty — into which the cherubim do not
dare to gaze — and having such a profitable servant, neither
beautify ourselves nor wash the filth from our wretched soul,
but have let it lie in neglect."

And having said these things, he took me and we went up to the
cell. And he threw himself upon the floor and first stained the
earth with his face, and wept, saying: "O God, have mercy on me
the sinner and unworthy, for one day's adorning of a harlot has
conquered the adorning of all the years of my life. And with
what face shall I look upon You, O God? And with what words
shall I be justified before You? And what excuse shall I make
before You, who behold my secret things? But I the sinner —
though I wear myself out in the sacrifice of Your temple — do
not offer You beauty as the harlot did, and I
stand at Your fearful [and terrible] table not adorned according
to Your will. O God, who brought lowliness from non-being into
being (and counted me, though unworthy, worthy to serve You), do
not cast me away from Your heavenly altar, nor let the adorning
of the harlot condemn me before Your fearful and dreadful
judgment-seat. For she promised to please men, and was anointed,
but I promised to please You, the lover of mankind, and was
found wanting." And after this, she appears adorned to please her
lovers, but I appear naked upon the earth and in the heavens.
There is no hope for me of salvation from works, but my soul
[and my hope] hangs wholly upon Your mercies, and I have come
to take refuge in the fullness of Your compassion.

While he was saying these things and sighing, we completed that
day, which was before the Sabbath. And on the morrow, after the
completion of the evening prayers [of the holy Lord's day], the
bishop says to me: "Brother deacon, I saw a dream, and I am
altogether perplexed, for I am not able to interpret it; but God
will make it plain and will grant understanding. — Thus he says
to me: 'I saw at night that I stood at the altar, and a black
dove, all befouled with mud, flew around me, and I could not
bear the stench of its mud. And it kept flying around me until
the prayer of the catechumens came; and when the deacon cried,
"As many as are catechumens, come forward," straightway it
became invisible from me. And after the dismissal of the prayers
and the completion of the liturgy, the church departed. And when
I took hold of the door of the house of God, again the same dove
befouled with mud flew around me. And stretching out my hand I
seized it and threw it into the basin [of the saint] in the
porch of the church; and it washed off in the water all its
filth and came up [out of the water] shining like snow, and flew
up into the heavens, and vanished from my eyes.'"

Having said these things, he took me and we went into the great
church with the rest of the bishops, and we greeted the bishop
of the city. And when it came time for the presbytery to go in,
the archbishop of Antioch bade the bishops who had come
together to go in with him. And going in, they sat upon the
throne. And after the reading of the holy Gospel, the bishop of
the city entrusts the holy Gospel to our lord Nonnus [the
bishop, in my company], exhorting him to speak to the people.
And opening his mouth, it was no longer he who spoke, but the
grace of God dwelling in him. And he said nothing worldly or
laughable [or unseemly], for he was not a man who magnified
human anger, but filled with the Holy Spirit he taught the
people, saying sternly concerning the coming judgment and the
good hope laid up for the faithful. And the people were all
pierced to the heart by the words which the Holy Spirit spoke
through him, so that the whole floor of the church was drenched
with the tears of the people.
And by the providence of the God who loves mankind, that
much-talked-of woman came into the church, of whom the report
was; and this strange and wonderful thing — that she, who had
never been catechized nor had taken thought for her sins, but
had come together into the church, was so pierced to the fear of
God by that holy discourse, that she wept over herself as one
despairing, and could not contain the flood of her tears. And
she charges one of her boys, saying: "Hasten and follow the holy
bishop, and learn where he lodges." And the boys did as they
were commanded, and followed and stood before the church, and so
going they announced to their mistress that they lodged in the
martyr's shrine of St. Julian.

Straightway she sends through her boys a tablet containing
thus: "To the holy disciple of Christ, from the sinful disciple
of the devil: I have heard by hearsay about the God whom you
worship, that He bowed the heavens and came down upon the earth
not for the sake of the righteous, but that He might save
sinners — and that He so humbled Himself and consorted with such
and so great sinners that even the cherubim and seraphim do not
dare to look where He mingled with men. And now, master, since
I have great confidence (for although you did not see the Lord
Jesus with bodily eyes, yet you were seen by the Samaritan woman
at the well with boldness — for I heard these things from your
discourse concerning your God), if you are a disciple of such a
God, do not refuse to let me be made alive through you and to be
baptized by your holiness."

Then the bishop writes back to these things thus: "Who are you
that have appeared to God, and what is your purpose? Only I tell
you, do not try to tempt my wretchedness — for I am a sinful
man. But if you truly have this holy desire [and wish to meet
me], there are seven other bishops with me; come in their
presence and meet with [me]; but you cannot meet alone."

Reading these things, straightway with joy she rises quickly
and comes to the martyr's shrine of St. Julian, and we were
afraid at her coming. And the bishop Nonnus, [learning this,]
before she arrived, called the bishops together and ordered the
doors to be shut, and her to come in. And she, coming where they
were gathered, threw herself upon the floor and took hold of the
feet of the holy bishops, of Nonnus, weeping violently, so that
from her many tears the holy one's feet were washed; but she
wiped them with her hair, and lifting the dust from the ground
with her head, she cast it on them, and with crying and shouting
cried out to the saint: "Have mercy on me, master, have mercy on
me the sinner. Imitate your master Jesus Christ, and pour out
your goodness upon me. Do not turn me away who am eager to
become a Christian. For I am, master, the sea of sins, I am the
abyss of lawlessness. I beseech you, disciple of the true God,
do not refuse me who repent, but purify me in the washing of
holiness."

While she was saying these things from a contrite heart, with
many tears, all the bishops who had come together and the clergy
shed many tears at such a gift and wonderful change, so that
many, marveling, said that they had never seen such desire and
faith. The servant of the living God could scarcely persuade her
to rise from his feet, and he says to her: "The canons of the
ecclesiastical order lay it down that a harlot is not to be
baptized without a sponsor, lest she be found again wallowing in
the same things."

And at hearing this word, she threw herself again upon the
floor and took hold of his feet, saying with many tears: "You
will give an account for me to God, and your head shall be
guilty for my misdeeds, if you refuse to baptize me, the
impious; and you shall not find mercy before the Lord, if you do
not make me now a stranger to my works and to the life I have
lived — I take God and His angels as witnesses — if, having
regenerated me today, you do not present me as a bride to
Christ."

Then all the bishops who had come together and those with them,
beholding the God who loves mankind, looking upon her inflamed
by the divine fire and wholly kindled — straightway the holy man
of God sends me, the sinful deacon, to the bishop of the city,
that he might make known to him all things, and that the
holiness of his [bishopric] might send one of the deaconesses.
Going, therefore, I announced these things to the bishop; and
he, hearing, was filled with great joy, and sent, saying to my
lord Nonnus: "Yes, father, for to you the work was left; for I
know that you are the mouth of God, as He said: 'If you bring
forth the precious from the worthless, you shall be as My
mouth.'" And so he sent with me the lady Romana, the first of
the deaconesses.
And coming together, we found her lying as a woman (?) before
the feet of the bishop. And the lady Romana persuaded her,
saying: "Rise, child, that you may be catechized." And the
servant of God says to her: "Confess all your sins." And she
said to him: "If I searched my conscience, I would never find
the good [in me] done; but I know that my sins are more than
the sand of the sea, and all the water of the sea is little
compared to my sins. But I am persuaded concerning your God,
that His love for mankind, being boundless, will not suffer the
multitude of my transgressions." Then the bishop says: "Tell
me, what is your name?" And she says: "[The name from my
birth] Pelagia I was called by my parents, but the city of the
Antiochenes named me Margarita, because of the abundance of
ornaments with which my sins adorned me; for I was the decorated
workshop of the devil." Again the bishop says to her: "Your
name from birth is Pelagia?" She says to him: "Yes, lord." And
after this he catechized her and anointed her with the holy
myrrh, and gave her to partake of the awful body and blood of
Christ. And the lady Romana the deaconess became her spiritual
mother. And so he takes her and goes into the catechumenate,
and ordered us too to remain there with the rest of the
bishops.

Then the bishop says to me: "Come, brother deacon, let us
rejoice today with the angels of God, and taking our meal
together let us drink wine with spiritual gladness for the
salvation of this lost sheep." And while we were eating, behold,
the devil appeared naked, and beat his hands upon his head and
cried, saying: "Woe is me from this old man and this drinker: did not the
thirty myriads of the Saracens suffice you, whom you snatched from me and
brought first to your God? Was not Heliopolis alone enough for
you, that you brought all in it to your God? And the last hope
I had you have cut off from me. Woe is me from this sorcerer. I
cannot bear your plots. Cursed be the day in which you were
miserably born. The river of your tears has run before my
wretched house and washed away all my hopes." All this the
devil said with loud crying and lamentations, all the bishops
and clergy and the deaconess and the newly baptized herself
hearing. Again the devil says to the maiden: "What is this to
me, my lady Pelagia? Do you remember my Judas? For he received
silver and was crowned with honor, and being an apostle betrayed
his own master. So do you too with me." Then Bishop Nonnus says
to the servant of God Pelagia: "Seal yourself with the cross."
And when she did this, straightway he became invisible from
her.

And after two days the devil comes again, as she was sleeping
in the chamber with her mother, and wakes the servant of God
and says: "My lady Margarita, what evil have I done to you? Was
it not with gold and pearls that I honored you? Silver and gold
did I not fill you with? I adjure you — return to me, for I am
now forsaken by you, and I have become a laughingstock of the
Christians." But the servant of God sealed herself and rebuked
him, saying: "The Lord Jesus Christ rebuke you, who snatched me
from the midst of your darkness and made me His own property
through His love for mankind." Then waking Romana the
deaconess, she says to her: "Arise, mother, pray for me, for
like a lion the devil comes against me." And she says to her:
"Be of good cheer, child, and do not fear him; for from now on
he fears and trembles at your shadow."

And on the third day Pelagia calls the boy who was the chief of
her stewards and says to him: "Go to the house and write down
all that is in my wardrobe, and my gold and my ornaments, bring
them to me." And the boy, going, did as was commanded him, and
brought all things to his mistress. Then sending for the holy
bishop Nonnus through her mother, she gave all the power of her
house into his hands, saying: "This is the wealth, master, with
which Satan enriched me through sin; I hand this over to the
judgment of your holiness; for from now the wealth of my
bridegroom Christ suffices me." And calling the boys and the
girls, giving to each one from her hand gold enough, she said
to them: "I have freed you from temporary slavery; but go free
yourselves from the slavery of the sin of the world." And so
she dismissed them. And my holy bishop, calling the steward of
the church, in her presence gave him all the power of her house,
saying to him: "I adjure you by the Holy Trinity, that nothing
at all of this property come into the church nor into the
episcopate, but to poor and needy, and give it to widows and
orphans, that what was ill-gathered may be well dispensed, and
thus lawless wealth may become a treasury of righteousness."

But the servant of God Pelagia kept nothing at all for herself,
not even for seven days; but the lady Romana clothed her, for
she had a vow that nothing of the wealth of sin should come to
her. And when the eighth day was completed, which was the
Lord's day, she puts off the holy robe of baptism and puts on a
hair shirt and a rough cloak, and she becomes a fugitive from
our city, no one knowing. And her spiritual mother wept
bitterly and mourned for her. But my holy bishop Nonnus
comforted her, saying: "Do not weep, but rejoice and exult;
Pelagia has chosen the good portion, like Mary."
After a few days the bishop of the city dismissed all the
visiting bishops to their own places, and we went to the
episcopate.

After the space of three years the desire came upon me to go up
to Jerusalem to worship at the holy Resurrection of our Lord and
God Jesus Christ, and I besought my holy bishop, my lord
Nonnus, and he released me and says to me: "Brother deacon,
when you arrive, seek out Pelagius the eunuch monk, whom I had,
being much tried, shut up, and meet him, and be benefited by
him." But he said nothing to me about the servant of God, and
he did not reveal her to me.

And rising, I came to the holy places and worshiped the
precious cross and the holy Resurrection. And on the morrow I
sought the holy Pelagius, and I found the cell on the Mount of
Olives, where the Lord prayed. And when I saw the cell, it had
no door for going in, but was enclosed all around with mud,
having only one small window, and it was shut; and knocking, I
knew that the servant of God recognized me [but did not reveal
herself to me]. But I did not recognize her at all. And how
could I have recognized that indescribable and wonderful beauty
so withered by severe abstinence and worn away by keeping
vigil? For those beautiful eyes of hers were now like caves,
barely showing; and the bones of her face were loosened by
excessive toil, [and likewise the rest of her body]. And all
Jerusalem had a good opinion of her as of a eunuch man, and no
man supposed her to be a woman; nor did I think any such thing.
I was blessed by her as by a man, and after this she says to
me: "Lord brother, are you not of my lord Nonnus the bishop?"
And I said: "Yes, honored father." And she says to me: "Pray
for me; for an apostle of the Lord is your honored bishop."
Then she said again: "Pray for me, lord brother" — she closed
the window and began to chant the third hour. But I prayed and,
blessed by her cell, departed thence, benefited by the greatest
benefit of her meeting; for I took nothing else concerning her
into my mind. And day by day I went around to the monasteries
for the sake of prayer and to be blessed by the holy fathers;
and there was talk everywhere in the monasteries about the holy
Pelagius.

Going again, and knocking on the window of her cell to be
blessed, I did not obtain an answer. And on the third day I said
to myself: "Behold, I came once and a second time, and I did not
obtain an answer. Has that servant of God perhaps departed?"
And saying this I looked all around; and since the cell was as
it was with no entrance, I came again to another thought,
taking up a better notion, saying to myself: "Has the holy monk
who was here perhaps died?" And I examined closely if somehow I
could see something through the window; but not only did I see
nothing, but I heard no one breathing or chanting as before,
even to the third [day?]. I knew to open the window and see more
closely. And doing this I looked in, and behold, the holy one
had rendered up her spirit and was stretched out upon the
ground. And so again closing the window, and running, I came in
haste to Jerusalem, glorifying God and announcing how the
stewardship of holy Pelagius the wonderworking monk had ended.
Straightway all the monasteries, and from Nicopolis and from
Jericho and beyond the Jordan, a great multitude gathered to
the Mount of Olives. And thus they broke open the door of the
cell, and they brought out the holy and above-gold and very
precious relic, and with all honor and fear all kissed it and
put it on a bier. And the holy bishop of that place and the
honorable fathers, coming likewise to lay him out for burial
and to anoint with myrrh, knew that she was truly a woman by
nature, and all cried out with a loud voice: "Glory to You, O
God, for You have many saints hidden upon the earth, not only
men but also women." And thus the whole people who had gathered
knew of such a miracle. And all the monasteries of the
neighborhood gathered, and with psalms and hymns they carried
her holy relic with holy hands and buried it in honor, with
holy [fathers].

Thus the life of the harlot — which is the instruction of the
despairing; with whom may the Lord also grant us to find mercy
in the day of judgment. Amen.

## Note on the Greek text

The source is the raw OCR of Usener's 1879 Bonn edition — Greek
in polytonic type, with the Leiden manuscript's deviations
printed line by line beneath the text. The Greek reads through
the noise well enough to translate straight through; uncertain
readings are marked [obscured in the scan], never smoothed over.
Variant readings from the Leiden manuscript (Leid.) are noted
only where they change the sense. Usener's German prolegomena
and his "Anmerkungen" (the textual notes) are not translated
here — this rendering is the legend itself, whole, as the Greek
gives it.
