## The repairs, fault by fault

**Fault 1 — the inverted bracket (James I.32).** The shelf read "Hardly [fifty] three,
my beloved," making three the main reading. James prints "Hardly fifty [three] my
beloved": fifty righteous souls a day is the text, "three" the bracketed variant.
Restored: "Hardly fifty [three], my beloved." The next exchange needed no repair: the
shelf's "The [fifty] three enter paradise..." already carries James's own bracket at
I.33 and stands untouched.

**Fault 2 — the rewritten passage, James IV.49-51 (printed p. 178).** Three repairs in
chapter 4, verse 14:
- Bartholomew's prayer was truncated: the self-description that identifies the speaker —
  "as thou didst choose me from the receipt of custom and didst not suffer me to have my
  conversation unto the end in my former deeds" — had been silently dropped, along with
  the second address to the Master. The full prayer is restored in the edition's plain
  register, including James's obelized lacuna (see Fault 3): "...and I, who am unworthy,
  with my lips †…† do praise you, O Master. Hearken to me, your servant; as you chose me
  from the receipt of custom and did not suffer me to go on in my former deeds to the
  end, O Lord Jesus Christ, hearken to me and have mercy on the sinners."
- The Lord's answer was invented wording. James IV.50: "Rise up, suffer him that
  groaneth to arise: I will declare the rest unto thee." The shelf's "Let him who was
  cast down declare this to you" — which also puts the declaring in the wrong hands — is
  replaced: "Rise up. Let him who groans arise; I will declare the rest to you."
- The speaker was swapped. The shelf split James's single speech: Bartholomew dismissed
  Satan, and then a new speaker, "the Lord," commanded "Have mercy on all his world."
  James IV.51 is Bartholomew's one sentence, and its last clause is a statement about
  the Lord, not a command by him. Restored as one speech of Bartholomew: "Go to your
  place, with your angels; but the Lord has mercy on all his world."

**Fault 3 — James's obeli silently resolved or dropped.** The pre-repair file contained
no † anywhere. Each instance the desk check enumerated is restored, with James's
tentative gloss kept as a gloss, in parentheses with his question mark — never adopted
into the text:
- Chapter 4, verse 10 (James IV.38, p. 176): "all whom I †look upon† (deceive?)" — the
  shelf had printed James's gloss "deceive" as certain text.
- Chapter 4, verse 13 (James IV.47, p. 177): "With them †do fly† (are administered?)
  the things in heaven and on earth and under the earth." — same repair.
- Chapter 5, verse 4 (James V, p. 181): "the pleasure of the flesh will †become a
  lover†." — the shelf had supplied a subject and an object ("will make him a lover of
  pleasure") that James's text does not have.
- Chapter 5, verse 3 (James V.5): "woe to him who swears falsely by him †truly†." — the
  shelf had dropped the obelized word altogether; it is restored under its daggers.
- The two lacunae: chapter 4, verse 4 (James IV.9 — see Fault 4) and chapter 4, verse 14
  (James IV.49 — see Fault 2), each shown as †…† where James marks the break.

**Fault 4 — the recensions fused without labels.** James prints the recensions
distinctly; the shelf ran them as one narrative. Repaired at each place the desk check
verified:
- *The openings (chapter 1).* The shelf merged the Greek opening with the Slavonic
  burial-and-rising verse into a single narrative. The file now gives the opening verses
  from each of the three texts separately and labelled, as James gives them — headed
  "The opening verses are given from each of the three texts, as James gives them":
  Greek (verses 1-2; James notes the Greek has no third verse, and this edition says
  so), Slavonic (verses 1-3), Latin 2 (verses 1-3). The narrative then resumes with
  James's Greek verse 4 ("Bartholomew therefore came near to the Lord...").
- *The descent (James I.11-15, p. 168).* The passage stood unmarked in the shelf's
  chapter 1. It is now bracketed and headed with James's own label: "[Slavonic and Latin
  2 continue: ... ]", closing at "...the breath of God."]".
- *The angel who would not go up (James I.25-27, p. 169).* The passage is Slavonic. It
  is now bracketed from "And all the angels begged him to go up with them" through the
  end of the Lord's answer, headed "[Slavonic: ...", and James's Latin 1 variant of the
  flame is restored inside the bracket in parentheses: "(Latin 1: But the flame that you
  saw come out of his hands struck the house of the synagogue of the Jews, as a
  testimony of me, in that they crucified me.)".
- *Bartholomew's prayer (James IV.9, printed p. 174 in the scanned copy).* The shelf had
  set the Latin 2 words "who confirmed the eternal word of the Father" directly into the
  Greek prayer, and the Greek lacuna and its "accomplished the work of the Father" clause
  were gone. The Greek form is restored — "who †…† the heavenly essence by a word, †…†
  accomplished the work of the Father" — and the Latin 2 form is given after the prayer,
  bracketed and labelled: "[Latin 2 gives this part of the prayer thus: who came down
  into the world, who confirmed the eternal word of the Father, who called the sadness of
  Adam to joy, who made the shame of Eve glad, and restored her, in that you were
  contained in the womb.]" (The scan reads "the sadness of (Adam) joy," i.e. the sadness
  of Adam called to joy; the desk check verified the "confirmed the eternal word" clause
  against the same page.)
- *The sign-of-the-cross verse (James IV.22).* Bracketed by James, from the Greek
  (Jerusalem manuscript) and Latin 1 — his footnote: "22. [ ] is from Gk. (Jerusalem
  MS.) and Latin 1." The verse is now bracketed as a whole and headed "[From the Greek
  (Jerusalem manuscript) and Latin 1: ...", closing after "...ask him what his power
  is."]" — see also Fault 5 for the wording restored inside it.

**Fault 5 — small referent and wording changes.**
- Chapter 4, verse 8 (James IV.22): "went to him" restored to James's "went behind him."
- Chapter 1 (James I.17, p. 168): the shelf had Hades answer with no addressee and a
  changed sentence — "'Six thousand years are not yet finished.'" Restored: "But Hades

- Chapter 1 openings (Fault 4): James prints the supplied word in angle brackets in both

  The desk check's locator for this item ("Line 4.2") matched no line as printed; the
  passage it described is the opening, and the repair is made there, as the desk ruled
  the substance sustained.
