Questions of Bartholomew

Chapter 1

1. After the Lord Jesus Christ rose from the dead, Bartholomew came to the Lord and questioned him, saying, "Lord, show me the mysteries of heaven."

Jesus answered him, "If I do not put off this body of flesh, I will not be able to tell them to you."

2. But after he was buried and had risen again, the apostles did not dare to question him, because they could hardly look at him — the fullness of his divine power was now seen in him.

3. Bartholomew therefore came near to the Lord and said, "Lord, I have something to ask you."

Jesus said to him, "I know what you are about to say. Say what you want, and I will answer you."

4. Bartholomew said, "Lord, when you went to be hanged on the cross, I followed you from far off. I saw you hung on the cross, and the angels coming down from heaven and worshipping you. And when darkness came, I looked, and I saw that you had vanished from the cross. I heard only a voice in the places under the earth, and suddenly great wailing and gnashing of teeth. Tell me, Lord, where did you go from the cross?"

5. Jesus answered, "Blessed are you, Bartholomew, my beloved, because you saw this mystery. Now I will tell you everything you ask me. When I vanished from the cross, I went down into Hades to bring up Adam and all who were with him, because Michael the archangel had begged me to do so."

6. Bartholomew said, "Lord, what was the voice that was heard?"

Jesus said to him, "Hades said to Beliar, 'As I can tell, a God is coming here.'

"And the angels cried to the powers, 'Lift up your gates, you rulers; lift up the everlasting doors, for look — the King of glory is coming down.'

"Hades said, 'Who is the King of glory, who is coming down from heaven to us?'

"And when I had gone down five hundred steps, Hades was in distress, saying, 'I hear the breathing of the Most High, and I cannot bear it.' But the devil answered, 'Do not give in, Hades; be strong. God himself has not come down to earth.' But when I had gone down another five hundred steps, the angels and the powers cried out, 'Take them away! Remove the doors, for look — the King of glory is coming down.' And Hades said, 'Woe is me, for I hear the breath of God.'"

7. Beliar said to Hades, "Look carefully who it is that [comes]. It seems to me this must be Elijah, or Enoch, or one of the prophets."

But Hades answered, "Six thousand years are not yet finished. And where would these come from, Beliar? The count of the number is in my hands."

8. [Beliar said to Hades:] "Do not be troubled. Make your gates safe and strengthen your bars. Think of it — God does not come down to earth."

Hades said to him, "These are no good words I hear from you. My belly is torn open, and my insides hurt me. It must be that God is coming here. Alas, where shall I flee before the face of the great King? Let me go inside myself — for I was formed before you."

9. Jesus said, "Then I went in, and I beat him and bound him with chains that cannot be loosed. And I brought out all the forefathers from there, and came back to the cross."

10. Bartholomew said to him, "Tell me, Lord — who was the man the angels carried up in their hands, the man who was very great in size? And what did you say to him, that he sighed so deeply?"

11. Jesus answered, "That was Adam, the first man formed, for whose sake I came down from heaven to earth. I said to him, 'I was hung on the cross for you and for your children's sake.' And when he heard it, he groaned and said, 'So was your good pleasure, O Lord.'"

12. Bartholomew said again, "Lord, I saw the angels going up before Adam and singing praises. But one of the angels, who was very great, greater than the rest, would not go up with them. He held a sword of fire in his hand, and he kept looking steadily at you alone. And all the angels begged him to go up with them, but he would not. But when you commanded him to go up, I saw a flame of fire come out of his hands and go all the way to the city of Jerusalem."

Jesus said to him, "Blessed are you, Bartholomew, my beloved, because you saw these mysteries. That was one of the angels of vengeance who stand before my Father's throne. My Father sent this angel to me. He would not go up because he wanted to destroy all the powers of the world. But when I commanded him to go up, a flame went out of his hand and tore the veil of the temple in two, as a witness to the children of Israel concerning my suffering, because they crucified me."

13. When he had said this, he said to the apostles, "Wait for me in this place, for today a sacrifice is offered in paradise."

Bartholomew answered, "Lord, what is the sacrifice offered in paradise?"

Jesus said, "There are souls of righteous people who have left the body today and are going to paradise. Unless I am present, they cannot enter paradise."

14. Bartholomew said, "Lord, how many souls leave the world every day?"

Jesus said to him, "Thirty thousand."

Bartholomew said to him, "Lord, when you were with us teaching the word, did you also receive the sacrifices in paradise?"

Jesus answered, "Truly I tell you, my beloved, that I both taught the word with you and continually sat with my Father, and received the sacrifices in paradise every day."

15. Bartholomew said, "Lord, if thirty thousand souls leave the world every day, how many of them are found righteous?"

Jesus said to him, "Hardly [fifty] three, my beloved."

Bartholomew said again, "And how do only three enter paradise?"

Jesus said to him, "The [fifty] three enter paradise or are laid up in Abraham's bosom. But the others go into the place of the resurrection, for the three are not like the fifty."

16. Bartholomew said to him, "Lord, how many souls are born into the world every day, over and above the number?"

Jesus said to him, "Only one soul more is born than the number of those who depart."

17. When he had said this, he gave them peace and vanished from them.

Chapter 2

1. Now the apostles were in the place called Cherubim with Mary. Bartholomew came and said to Peter and Andrew and John, "Let us ask her who is highly favored how she conceived the One who cannot be contained, how she carried him who cannot be carried, and how she gave birth to so much greatness."

But they hesitated to ask her. So Bartholomew said to Peter, "You are the chief and my teacher. Go and ask her."

But Peter said to John, "You are a virgin, undefiled and beloved. You must ask her."

2. While they all hesitated and argued, Bartholomew went to her with a cheerful face and said to her, "You who are highly favored, the tabernacle of the Most High, without blemish — we, all the apostles, ask you (all the apostles have sent me to ask you) to tell us how you conceived the One who cannot be contained, how you carried him who cannot be carried, and how you gave birth to so much greatness."

3. But Mary said to them, "Do not ask me about this mystery. If I begin to tell you, fire will come out of my mouth and burn up the whole world."

But they kept on asking her all the more. And because she could not refuse to listen to the apostles, she said, "Let us stand up in prayer."

4. The apostles stood behind Mary. But she said to Peter, "Peter, you chief, you great pillar — do you stand behind us? Did not our Lord say, 'The head of the man is Christ, but the head of the woman is the man'? So stand in front of me and pray."

But they said to her, "The Lord set his tabernacle in you, and it pleased him that you should contain him. You ought to lead the prayer."

She said to them, "You are shining stars. As the prophet said, 'I lifted up my eyes to the hills, from where my help will come.' So you are the hills, and you should pray."

The apostles said to her, "You ought to pray. You are the mother of the heavenly King."

Mary said to them, "In your likeness God made the sparrows and sent them out into the four corners of the world."

But they said to her, "He who is scarcely contained by the seven heavens was pleased to be contained in you."

5. Then Mary stood up before them and stretched out her hands toward heaven and began to pray:

"[Elphue, Zarethra, Charboum, Nemioth, Melitho, Thraboutha, Mephnounos, Chemiath, Aroura, Maridon, Elison, Marmiadon, Seption, Hesaboutha, Ennouna, Saktinos, Athoor, Belelam, Opheoth, Abo, Chrasar] — which in the Greek tongue is: O God, most great and all-wise, King of the ages, who cannot be described, beyond all speech, who established the greatness of the heavens and all things by a word; who out of darkness brought together and fixed the poles of heaven in harmony; who gave shape to matter that was in confusion; who brought order to things that had no order; who parted the misty darkness from the light; who established the foundations of the waters in one place; you who make the beings of the air tremble and who are feared by those on the earth and under the earth; who settled the earth and did not let it perish, and filled it — the nourisher of all things — with showers of blessing: Son of the Father, you whom the seven heavens hardly contained, but who were well pleased to be contained in me without pain; you who are yourself the full word of the Father, in whom all things came to be: give glory to your most great name, and let me speak before your holy apostles."

6. When she had finished the prayer, she began to say to them, "Let us sit down on the ground. Come, Peter the chief, and sit at my right hand and put your left hand under my armpit. And you, Andrew, do the same at my left hand. And you, John the virgin, hold my bosom. And you, Bartholomew, set your knees against my back and hold my shoulders, so that when I begin to speak my bones are not loosed from one another."

7. When they had done so, she began to say:

"When I lived in the temple of God and received my food from an angel, one day a being appeared to me in the likeness of an angel. But his face could not be looked upon, and he did not hold bread or a cup in his hand, as the angel who came to me before had done.

"At once the veil of the temple was torn, and there was a very great earthquake, and I fell to the ground, because I could not bear the sight of him. But he put his hand under me and raised me up. I looked up into heaven, and a cloud of dew came and sprinkled me from head to foot, and he wiped me with his robe.

"He said to me, 'Hail, you who are highly favored, the chosen vessel, grace that cannot be exhausted.' Then he struck his garment on the right side, and a very great loaf came out. He set it on the altar of the temple, and first ate of it himself, and then gave to me also. Again he struck his garment on the left side, and a very great cup full of wine came out. He set it on the altar of the temple, and first drank of it himself, and then gave to me also. And I looked, and I saw the bread and the cup still whole, as they had been.

"He said to me, 'Yet three years, and I will send my word to you, and you will conceive my Son, and through him the whole creation will be saved. Peace be with you, my beloved, and my peace will be with you continually.'

"When he had said this, he vanished from my eyes, and the temple was restored as it had been before."

8. As she was saying this, fire came out of her mouth, and the world was about to come to an end. But Jesus quickly appeared and said to Mary, "Do not utter this mystery, or this very day my whole creation will come to an end." And the apostles were seized with fear, in case the Lord might be angry with them.

Chapter 3

1. He went with them to the Mount Mauria and sat in the midst of them. But they were afraid to question him.

Jesus answered and said to them, "Ask me whatever you want me to teach you, and I will show it to you. For in seven days I will ascend to my Father, and I will no longer be seen by you in this likeness."

But still hesitating, they said to him, "Lord, show us the deep — the abyss — just as you promised."

Jesus said to them, "It is not good for you to see the deep. All the same, if you want it, according to my promise — come, follow me, and see."

2. He led them to a place called Cherubim, that is, the place of truth. He beckoned to the angels of the West, and the earth was rolled up like the scroll of a book, and the deep was revealed to them.

3. When the apostles saw it, they fell on their faces to the earth. But Jesus raised them up, saying, "Did I not tell you, 'It is not good for you to see the deep'?"

And again he beckoned to the angels, and the deep was covered up.

Chapter 4

1. He took them and brought them again to the Mount of Olives.

Peter said to Mary, "You who are highly favored, beg the Lord to show us the things that are in the heavens."

Mary said to Peter, "O stone cut out of the rock, did not the Lord build his church on you? You go first and ask him."

Peter said again, "O tabernacle spread abroad — it is for you to ask."

Mary said, "You are the image of Adam. Was not Adam formed first, and then Eve? Look at the sun: according to the likeness of Adam it is bright. And look at the moon: because of Eve's transgression it is full of clay. God placed Adam in the east and Eve in the west. He appointed the lights so that the sun would shine on the earth for Adam in the east in his fiery chariots, and the moon in the west would give light to Eve with a face like milk. And she defiled the commandment of the Lord. Therefore the moon was stained with clay, and its light is not bright. So you, since you are the likeness of Adam, should ask him. But he was contained in me, so that I might recover the strength of the female."

2. When they came to the top of the mount and the Master had withdrawn a little from them, Peter said to Mary, "You are the one who undid the transgression of Eve, changing it from shame into joy. Therefore it is right for you to ask."

3. When Jesus appeared again, Bartholomew said to him, "Lord, show us the adversary of men, so that we may see him — what he looks like, what his work is, where he comes from, and what power he has, that he did not spare even you, but caused you to be hanged on the tree."

But Jesus looked at him and said, "You bold heart! You ask for something you are not able to look at."

4. Bartholomew was troubled and fell at Jesus' feet and began to speak:

"O lamp that cannot be put out, Lord Jesus Christ, maker of the eternal light, who gave to those who love you the grace that makes all things beautiful, and who gave us the eternal light by your coming into the world — who confirmed the eternal word of the Father; who turned the shame of Adam into joy; who did away with the sorrow of Eve and gave her a cheerful face by your birth from a virgin: do not hold evil against me, but grant me the word I ask for."

5. As he spoke, Jesus raised him up and said to him, "Bartholomew, do you want to see the adversary of men? I tell you, when you see him, not you only, but the rest of the apostles and Mary will fall on your faces and become like dead corpses."

But they all said to him, "Lord, let us see him."

6. He led them down from the Mount of Olives. He looked wrathfully at the angels who guard hell, and beckoned to Michael to sound the trumpet in the height of heaven. Michael sounded, and the earth shook, and Beliar came up, held by 660 angels and bound with fiery chains.

His length was 1,600 cubits and his breadth 40 cubits. His face was like a lightning-flash of fire, and his eyes were full of darkness. Out of his nostrils came a stinking smoke. His mouth was like the gulf of a cliff, and one of his wings was eighty cubits.

7. As soon as the apostles saw him, they fell to the earth on their faces and became as if dead. But Jesus came near and raised the apostles and gave them a spirit of power.

He said to Bartholomew, "Come near, Bartholomew, and trample on his neck with your feet, and he will tell you what his work is, and how he deceives men."

Jesus stood at a distance with the rest of the apostles.

8. Bartholomew was afraid. He raised his voice and said, "Blessed be the name of your immortal kingdom, from now on and forever."

When he had spoken, Jesus gave him permission, saying, "Go and tread on the neck of Beliar."

Bartholomew ran quickly to him and trod on his neck, and Beliar trembled.

Then Bartholomew was afraid and fled, and said to Jesus, "Lord, give me the edge of your garment, so that I may have courage to come near him."

But Jesus said to him, "You cannot take the edge of my garment, for these are not the garments I wore before I was crucified."

Bartholomew said, "Lord, I am afraid he may swallow me up also, just as he did not spare your angels."

Jesus said to him, "Were not all things made by my word? And by the will of my Father, were not the spirits made subject to Solomon? You, therefore, being commanded by my word, go in my name and ask him whatever you want."

Bartholomew made the sign of the cross and prayed to Jesus and went to him. Jesus said to him, "Draw near." And as Bartholomew drew near, fire was kindled on every side, so that his garments looked fiery.

Jesus said to Bartholomew, "As I told you: tread on his neck and ask him what his power is."

Bartholomew went and trod on his neck and pressed his face down into the earth as far as his ears.

9. Bartholomew said to him, "Tell me who you are and what your name is."

Beliar said to him, "Loosen me a little, and I will tell you who I am, and how I came here, and what my work is and what my power is."

Bartholomew loosened him and said to him, "Say all you have done and all you do."

Beliar answered, "If you want to know my name: at first I was called Satanael, which means 'a messenger of God.' But when I rejected the image of God, my name was called Satan — that is, an angel who keeps hell."

Bartholomew said to him again, "Show me everything and hide nothing from me."

Beliar said to him, "I swear to you by the power of the glory of God that even if I wanted to hide something, I cannot, for he is near who would expose me. If I were able, I would have destroyed you like one of those who were before you.

"Indeed, I was formed the first angel. When God made the heavens, he took a handful of fire and formed me first, Michael second, Gabriel third, Uriel fourth, Raphael fifth, Nathanael sixth, and other angels whose names I cannot tell. For they are the rod-bearers of God, and they strike me with their rods and chase me seven times in the night and seven times in the day. They do not leave me alone at all, and they break all my power to pieces.

"These are the angels of vengeance who stand before the throne of God. These are the angels who were formed first. After them, all the angels were formed.

"In the first heaven there are a hundred myriads, and in the second a hundred myriads, and in the third a hundred myriads, and in the fourth a hundred myriads, and in the fifth a hundred myriads, and in the sixth a hundred myriads, and in the seventh a hundred myriads. Outside the seven heavens is the first firmament, where the powers that work on men are.

"There are also four other angels set over the winds. The first angel is over the north. He is called Chairoum. He holds a rod of fire in his hand, and holds back the overflow of moisture so the earth is not made too wet.

"And the angel over the north is called Oertha. He holds a torch of fire and puts it to his sides, and they warm his great coldness so that he does not freeze the world.

"And the angel over the south is called Kerkoutha, and they break his fierceness so that he does not shake the earth.

"And the angel over the south-west is called Naoutha. He holds a rod of snow in his hand and puts it into his mouth, and quenches the fire that comes out of his mouth. If the angel did not quench it at his mouth, it would set the whole world on fire.

"And there is another angel over the sea, who makes it rough with its waves. But the rest I will not tell you, for he who stands by does not let me."

10. Bartholomew said to him, "How do you punish the souls of men?"

Beliar said to him, "Do you want me to declare the punishment of the hypocrites, the backbiters, the jesters, the idolaters, the covetous, the adulterers, the sorcerers, the fortune-tellers, and those who believe in us, and all whom I deceive?"

Bartholomew said to him, "I want you to declare it in a few words."

11. Beliar gnashed his teeth together, and there came up out of the bottomless pit a wheel with a sword flashing with fire, and in the sword were pipes.

Bartholomew asked him, "What is this sword?"

Beliar said, "This sword is the sword of the gluttonous. Into this pipe are sent those who, through their gluttony, devise every kind of sin. Into the second pipe are sent the backbiters who backbite their neighbor in secret. Into the third pipe are sent the hypocrites and the rest whom I overthrow by my plotting."

12. Bartholomew said, "Do you do these things by yourself alone?"

Satan said, "If I were able to go out by myself, I would have destroyed the whole world in three days. But neither I nor any of the six hundred go out. We have other swift ministers whom we command. We give them a hook with many points and send them out to hunt, and they catch souls of men for us, enticing them with the sweetness of different baits — by drunkenness and laughter, by backbiting, hypocrisy, pleasures, fornication, and the rest of the trifles that come out of their treasures.

"And I will tell you also the rest of the names of the angels. The angel of the hail is called Mermeoth. He holds the hail on his head, and my ministers call on him and send him wherever they want. And there are other angels over the snow, and others over the thunder, and others over the lightning. And when any spirit of ours wants to go out by land or by sea, these angels send out fiery stones and set our limbs on fire."

13. Bartholomew said, "Be still, you dragon of the pit."

Beliar said, "Many things will I tell you about the angels. Those who run together through the heavenly places and the earthly are these: Mermeoth, Onomatath, Douth, Melioth, Charouth, Graphathas, Oethra, Nephonos, Chalkatoura. With them the things in heaven and on earth and under the earth are governed."

Bartholomew said to him, "Be still and lie low, so that I may beg my Lord."

14. Bartholomew fell on his face and threw earth on his head and began to say:

"O Lord Jesus Christ, the great and glorious Name. All the choirs of the angels praise you. Hearken to me, and have mercy on the sinners."

When he had said this, the Lord said to him, "Rise up. Let him who was cast down declare this to you."

Then Bartholomew raised up Satan and said to him, "Go to your place, with your angels."

But the Lord said, "Have mercy on all his world."

15. But the devil said, "Let me stay, and I will tell you how I was cast down into this place, and how the Lord made man.

"I was going to and fro in the world. God said to Michael, 'Bring me a clod of earth from the four corners of the earth, and water from the four rivers of paradise.' When Michael brought them, God formed Adam in the regions of the east. He shaped the shapeless clod, and stretched sinews and veins on it, and set it together with joints. And he himself worshipped him first, for his own sake, because Adam was the image of God. Therefore he worshipped him.

"When I came from the ends of the earth, Michael said, 'Worship the image of God, which he has made according to his likeness.' But I said, 'I am fire of fire. I was the first angel formed. Shall I worship clay and matter?'

"Michael said to me, 'Worship, so that God is not angry with you.' But I said to him, 'God will not be angry with me. But I will set my throne above his throne, and I will be as he is.'

"Then God was angry with me and cast me down. He commanded that the windows of heaven be opened. And when I was cast down, he also asked the six hundred who were under me whether they would worship. But they said, 'Just as we have seen the first angel do — we will not worship one who is less than ourselves.' Then the six hundred were also cast down by him with me.

"When we were cast down to the earth, we were senseless for forty years. And when the sun shone seven times brighter than fire, I suddenly awoke. I looked, and I saw my six hundred who were under me, senseless. I awoke my son Salpsan and took counsel with him on how I might deceive the man for whose sake I was cast out of heaven.

"This is how I worked it out. I took a vial in my hand and scraped the sweat from my breast and the hair of my armpits, and washed myself in the springs of water from which the four rivers flow out. And Eve drank of it, and desire came upon her. For if she had not drunk that water, I could not have deceived her."

16. Then Bartholomew commanded him to go into hell.

Bartholomew came and fell at Jesus' feet and, with tears, began to say:

"Abba, Father, who cannot be found out by us; Word of the Father, whom the seven heavens hardly contained, but who were pleased to be contained easily and without pain in the body of the Virgin — whom the Virgin did not know that she bore: you by your thought have ordained all things to be. You give us what we need before we ask you.

"You wore a crown of thorns, so that you might prepare for us who repent the precious crown from heaven. You hung on the tree. You drank wine mixed with gall, so that you might give us the wine of sorrow for sin to drink. You were pierced in the side with a spear, so that you might fill us with your body and your blood.

"You gave names to the four rivers: to the first, Phison, because of the faith (pistis) which you came into the world to preach; to the second, Geon, because man was made of earth (ge); to the third, Tigris, because through you the Trinity, of one substance, was revealed to us in the heavens; to the fourth, Euphrates, because by your presence in the world you made every soul rejoice (euphranein) through the word of immortality.

"My God and Father, the greatest, my King: save the sinners, Lord."

17. When he had prayed, Jesus said to him, "Bartholomew, my Father named me Christ, so that I might come down to earth and anoint every man who comes to me with the oil of life. And he called me Jesus, so that I might heal every sin of those who do not know, and give to men the truth of God."

Bartholomew said to him again, "Lord, may I reveal these mysteries to every man?"

Jesus said to him, "Bartholomew, my beloved, to as many as are faithful and able to keep them to themselves — to them you may entrust these things. For there are some who are worthy of them. But there are others to whom it is not right to entrust them, for they are vain boasters, drunkards, proud, unmerciful, sharers in idolatry, makers of fornication, slanderers, teachers of foolishness, doing all the works of the devil. Therefore they are not worthy that these things should be entrusted to them.

"And these things are also secret because of those who cannot contain them. For as many as can contain them will have a part in them. Until now, my beloved, I have spoken to you. For blessed are you and all your kindred who of their own choice have this word entrusted to them. For all who can contain it will receive whatever they want in the day of my judgment."

18. Then I, Bartholomew, who wrote these things in my heart, took hold of the hand of the Lord who loves men, and began to rejoice and to speak:

"Glory to you, O Lord Jesus Christ, who give your grace to all, which we have all received. Alleluia.

"Glory to you, O Lord, the life of sinners.

"Glory to you, O Lord — death is put to shame.

"Glory to you, O Lord, the treasure of righteousness. For to God we sing."

19. As Bartholomew spoke again, Jesus put off his mantle and took a kerchief from Bartholomew's neck and began to rejoice and say:

"I am good. Alleluia.

"I am meek and gentle. Alleluia.

"Glory to you, O Lord, for I give gifts to all who desire me. Alleluia.

"Glory to you, O Lord, world without end. Amen. Alleluia."

20. When he had finished, the apostles kissed him, and he gave them the peace of love.

Chapter 5

1. Bartholomew said to him, "Tell us, Lord, what sin is heavier than all sins?"

Jesus said to him, "Truly I tell you that hypocrisy and backbiting are heavier than all sins. Because of them the prophet said in the psalm: 'The ungodly shall not rise in the judgment, nor sinners in the council of the righteous' — nor the ungodly in the judgment of my Father. Truly, truly I tell you, every sin will be forgiven to every man, but the sin against the Holy Spirit will not be forgiven."

2. Bartholomew said to him, "What is the sin against the Holy Spirit?"

Jesus said to him, "Whoever speaks against any man who has served my holy Father has blasphemed against the Holy Spirit. For every man who serves God in worship is worthy of the Holy Spirit, and he who speaks anything evil against him will not be forgiven."

3. "Woe to him who swears by the head of God — yes, woe to him who swears falsely by him. For there are twelve heads of God the Most High, for he is the truth, and in him there is no lie and no false swearing.

"Therefore go and preach the word of truth to all the world. And you, Bartholomew, preach this word to everyone who desires it. As many as believe in it will have eternal life."

4. Bartholomew said, "O Lord, if anyone sins with a sin of the body, what is their reward?"

Jesus said, "It is good if he who is baptized presents his baptism blameless. But the pleasure of the flesh will make him a lover of pleasure. A single marriage belongs to sober living. For truly I tell you, he who sins after the third marriage is unworthy of God."

5. "But you — preach to every man that they keep themselves from such things. For I do not depart from you, and I supply you with the Holy Spirit."

6. Bartholomew worshipped him with the apostles and glorified God earnestly, saying, "Glory to you, Holy Father, Sun that cannot be quenched, beyond all understanding, full of light. To you be glory, to you honor and adoration, world without end. Amen."


Notes: A cubit is about 18 inches (the length from elbow to fingertip). A myriad is ten thousand. "Abraham's bosom" means the place of rest for the righteous dead.
