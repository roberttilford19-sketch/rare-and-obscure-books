# The Life of Olympias the Deaconess, and Sergia's Account of the Return of Her Relics

**two Greek texts from Constantinople — the fifth-century Life by an anonymous sister of her house, and the seventh-century account of the translation of her relics by the abbess Sergia**
A fresh English rendering, plain and complete

---

**A fresh translation, not a reprint.** The two texts in this
booklet belong together, and either alone is thin. The first is
the *Life of Olympias the Deaconess* — the whole story, from her
birth to an heiress's fortune in Constantinople, through her
days-long marriage, her refusal of a second husband, her years as
deaconess and the right hand of John Chrysostom, her trial and
exile when he fell, to her death in banishment at Nicomedia and
the miracles at her tomb. The second is the *Account of Sergia*
— how, more than a century after Olympias died, the abbess
Sergia recovered her relics from the ruined church of St. Thomas
at Brochthoi and brought them home to the rebuilt monastery in
the city, with the signs and healings that attended them.
Both are here rendered whole into plain English, sentence by
sentence from the Greek, the Life first and Sergia's Account
after it.

Originals: Byzantine Greek, Constantinople — the Life from the
fifth century, written by an unnamed sister of Olympias's own
house; Sergia's Account from the seventh, written by Sergia
herself, abbess of Olympias's monastery after Justinian rebuilt
it. Basis for this rendering: the critical Greek texts printed
by Hippolyte Delehaye in the *Analecta Bollandiana* — the Life
in vol. 15 (1896), pp. 400–423, and Sergia's *Narratio de
translatione* in vol. 16 (1897), pp. 43–62 — as digitized by the
Internet Archive, identifiers `AnalectaBollandiana1896`
(OCR `Analecta_Bollandiana_15_djvu.txt`, read 2026-10-11; the
Life's Greek at OCR lines ~22463–23370) and
`analecta-bollandiana_1897_16` (the Narratio's Greek at OCR
lines ~2284–2890; journal pp. 43–62 re-read from the page
images, leaves 47–66, for a clean Greek text).

**How it is presented:** the Greek in clear modern English, plain
enough for a humble reader to follow. The chapter numbers are
the editor's (Prologue and chapters 2–18 for the Life; chapters
1–16 for Sergia's Account). Scripture citations the old print
carries in its margins are kept where they stand. Where the old

**Reader note:** This is a working saint's book from the early
Church: how a rich young widow gave away a fortune, ran a
house of holy women at the shoulder of the great church of
Constantinople, stood by her bishop when the court destroyed
him, and was destroyed with him — and how her sisters kept her
memory for a hundred years until an abbess brought her bones
home. Nothing in it is praised or modernized — it is presented
as the two sisters wrote it, for the reader to judge. Readers
should note that this is a historical record, not a modern
history: names, dates, and wonders belong to their century.

## Dedication

**Olympias (d. 408)** — orphaned heiress of Constantinople,
deaconess, and the financier and friend of John Chrysostom. She
was left an orphan young, married for a matter of days to
Nebridius the prefect, and widowed before the year was out; she
refused every second marriage, gave her fortune to the poor and
the churches, and was ordained deaconess. When Chrysostom fell
she refused communion with his successor, saw her convent
seized, and died in banishment at Nicomedia on 25 July 408. Her
*Life*, and the account of the return of her relics written more
than a century later, were set down by sisters of her own house
whose names were never kept.

---

## A word on the two writers

The *Life* names no author. It was written, by its own account,
by one who knew the house from inside — the Bollandist editor
judged the writer to belong to the fourth or fifth century,
nearer Olympias's own time than not. The *Account* names its
writer on nearly every page: *I, Sergia the sinner*, abbess of
the monastery of St. Olympias at Constantinople after the
Emperor Justinian rebuilt it following the Nika fire of 532, in
the days of the Patriarch Sergius (610–638). She writes as one
who did the things she tells — who opened the tomb, lifted the
relics, and carried them home.

## THE LIFE OF OLYMPIAS THE DEACONESS

*The Life, that is, the conduct and deeds, of the holy, blessed
and righteous Olympias, who became deaconess of the most holy
Great Church of Constantinople. Bless, father.*

### Prologue

The kingdom of our Saviour Jesus Christ — which was before the
ages and shines through unending ages — makes its own
shield-bearers immortal: those who finished the race, and those
who kept their faith toward God spotless and unbent. [II Tim.
4:7]

For some worked at hospitality, the crown of good deeds, like
the holy forefather Abraham and his nephew Lot; some contended
for chastity, like holy Joseph; some strove in labors with
patience, like blessed Job; and some gave their bodies to fire
and torments, to receive the crown of incorruption — not fearing
the tortures of the tyrants, but fighting nobly and trampling
the devil, and were shown to be heirs of the kingdom of heaven.

Among them also Thecla, citizen of heaven, the much-contending
martyr, the holy one among women — who despised riches, who
hated the fleeting and bitter pleasures of this world, who
refused a costly marriage and confessed her resolve to stand a
pure virgin before the true Bridegroom — who followed the
teachings of Paul the blessed apostle and embraced the
God-breathed Scriptures in her heart — received the crown of
incorruption from the Master and our Saviour Jesus Christ, and
rests through unending ages with all the saints who from the
beginning pleased the Lord Jesus Christ.

Walking in her footsteps, and in every virtue of the God-given
way of life, the most reverend and most zealous Olympias took
the road that leads to heaven; in all things she followed the
mind of the divine Scriptures, and in them she was made perfect.

### 2. Her birth and her marriage

She was, in the flesh, the daughter of Seleucus, who had been a
count — but a true child of God in the spirit. She is said to
have been the granddaughter of Ablabius, who had been prefect,
and for a few days the bride of Nebridius, prefect of the city
of Constantine. But in truth, as far as the marriage-bed goes,
she was wife of no man: for she is said to have slept
undefiled, a virgin — having become the wedded companion of the
divine Word, and the bedfellow of all true lowliness of mind,
and a sharer and deaconess of God's holy catholic and apostolic
Church.

For she was left an orphan, and was joined to a husband; yet by
God's love toward mankind she was kept uncorrupted in flesh and
in spirit. For by the all-seeing God, who foresees the outcomes
of men's lives, her fleeting husband was not counted worthy to
live with her even a year — the debt of nature being swiftly
required of him — and she was kept a spotless virgin to the
end.

### 3. She refuses a second marriage

She could have used the apostle's law, which says, "I would have
the younger widows marry, and rule their households" [I Tim.
5:14] — but she would not bear it. Though she was adorned with
birth and riches and costly schooling, with a gifted nature and
the bloom of youth, like a gazelle she bounded clear over the
trap of a second marriage. For law is not laid down for the
righteous, but for the unruly, the profane, and the insatiable.
[I Tim. 1:9]

But it came about, by a certain satanic envy, that her
surpassing widowhood was made public. She was slandered before
the emperor Theodosius as scattering her substance recklessly —
and since she was his kinswoman, he made it his business to join
her in marriage to a certain young Elpidius, a kinsman of the
emperor himself. He pressed the man hard, but failed, and was
grieved.

The holy Olympias declared to the emperor Theodosius: "If my
king, the Lord Jesus Christ, had wished me to live with a
husband, He would not have taken my first from me. But since He
knew me unfit for the mingled life of marriage — unable to
please a husband — He freed him from the bond, and freed me from
the heaviest yoke and from a husband's slavery, laying upon my
mind His own good yoke of continence."

### 4. Her goods are put under guard; her answer

When she had thus declared these things to the emperor
Theodosius — this was before the plotting against the most holy
John of Constantinople — the emperor, hearing what the holy
Olympias had declared to him, ordered Clementinus, the prefect
of the city at that time, to keep her property under guard until
she should complete her thirtieth year, that is, the age of
bodily maturity.

And the prefect, taking the emperor's commission — and egged on
by Elpidius — pressed her so hard that she had no leave even to
meet with the notable bishops, nor to set foot in church, so
that, straitened by despondency, she might fall into the snare
of marriage.

But she, giving thanks to God the more, answered back: "You
have shown toward me, your lowly servant, a virtue befitting a
king and becoming a bishop, master and emperor, in ordering my
heaviest burden — about which I was anxious, how it might be
managed — to be kept under guard. You will do a greater thing
if you order it to be scattered among the poor and the
churches. For I long ago renounced the vainglory that comes of
giving, lest I neglect the riches of my nature, dragged down as
I am among the stuff of this world."

---

### 5. The emperor frees her goods; she gives all away

When the emperor came back from the war against Maximus, hearing
the pitch of her ascetic life, he ordered her to have the free
use of her property. And she, scattering that boundless and
unmeasured wealth, supplied all men simply and without
distinction — and for many reasons she surpassed that Samaritan
of the holy Gospels, who once found the half-dead man beaten by
robbers on the way down to Jericho, set him on his own beast and
bore him to the inn, and mixing the oil of mercy with the sharp
wine, healed his swellings.

So straightway, when her substance was released and unsealed, she
kindled the divine love in herself with fresh fire and fled to
the harbor of salvation — to God's holy, great, catholic and
apostolic Church of this imperial city — following most wisely
and purely the God-breathed teachings of John, the most holy
archbishop of that same holy church. And to him, for that same
holy church — imitating in this too those burning lovers and
disciples of Christ who, at the beginning of the saving
preaching, laid their possessions at the apostles' feet — she
gave ten thousand pounds of gold, twenty thousand pounds of
silver, and all the landed estates that belonged to her, lying
in the provinces of Thrace and Galatia and First Cappadocia and
Bithynia; and besides, the houses in the imperial city that were
hers — the one next to the most holy Great Church, called
"Olympias's," with its tribunal-hall and its full bath and all
the buildings around it and the *silignarion*; and the house of
hers near the public Constantinian baths, where she herself
lived; and her other house, called [obscured in the scan]; and
all her country estates.

### 6. She is made deaconess; she builds the monastery

By God's will, then, she is ordained deaconess of that same
holy Great Church of God, and she builds a monastery on its
southern portico — for all the buildings next to the holy church
and all the workshops on what is called the southern portico
were hers. She made the entrance of the monastery open into the
narthex of the holy church. First of all she shut up in it her
own fifty household women, all of whom had lived in chastity
and virginity. Then Elisanthia, her kinswoman, seeing the good
and God-pleasing work which God had granted her to accomplish —
being herself also a virgin — caught the divine zeal, renounced
the fleeting and vain affairs of this life, together with
Martyria and Palladia, her sisters, themselves also virgins; and
the three came in with all the rest and made over to that same
holy monastery all that they had. Likewise Olympias — the niece
of the said saintly Olympias — with many other women of
senatorial family, by the grace and good pleasure of God "who
would have all men to be saved" [I Tim. 2:4], nourished the
divine love in themselves and chose the heavenly kingdom,
despising these groveling, earth-dragged things; and they too
came in with the rest — so that all who were gathered by God's
grace in that holy fold of Christ numbered two hundred and
fifty, all crowned with the crown of virginity and living the
highest life, befitting saints.

### 7. She gives the rest, and her kinswomen are made deaconesses

When these things had thus gone forward with God's help, the
genuine handmaid of God, Olympias, again offered to the said
holy church, through the most holy Patriarch John, all her
remaining landed possessions, lying in every province, and the
civic bread-rations that came to her. And he ordained as
deaconesses of the holy church her three kinswomen too —
Elisanthia, Martyria, and Palladia — so that the holy monastery
she had founded should keep its four deaconesses in perpetuity.

---

### 8. The life of the sisterhood; her care for John in exile

It was a sight to move wonder in any who saw it — the holy
choir and angelic order of those holy women: their unceasing
self-denial and night-watches, their unbroken glorifying of God
and thanksgiving, the love which is the bond of perfection, the
stillness. For no one from outside, man or woman, could come in
to them — no one but the most holy Patriarch John, coming in
continually and strengthening them with his all-wise teaching.
And so, built up daily by his God-breathed lessons, and
kindling the divine love in themselves with their great and
poured-out godly love toward him, the holy and blessed Olympias
— imitating in this too the Lord's women disciples, who
ministered to Him of their substance — made ready and sent
John's daily expenses to the bishop's house. For there was not
much between the bishop's house and the monastery — only a
single wall. And this she did not only before the plot was laid
against him, but also after he was exiled, and until his death —
providing his whole expenses, his and those who were with him in
exile.

### 9. The devil moves; John is exiled; she is put on trial

The devil, then, could not bear the great and wondrous way of
life of those holy women — wrought, in the first place, by God's
grace, and in the second by the unceasing teaching of the most
holy patriarch. He put it into the minds of wicked men, who
hated and loathed the saintly John for his unsparing rebuke of
wrongdoers, and the hater of good, the demon, kindled in them
the dart of slander. They made ready their devilish engine
against him and against that holy woman. They slandered him —
not about her only, but about church matters, as seemed good to
them — and exiled him. But the herald and teacher of the truth,
like a noble athlete, took the onset of his adversaries and
carried off the prize of victory; he slipped out from the
turmoil of this present life and was translated to the calm
above. [II Tim. 4:8]

As for the holy woman — since, after his exile, she kept up an
unbending agitation, through every imperial and priestly person,
for his recall — her enemies ringed her with many evils,
stitching together ill-timed slanders and revilings against
her, until they had her haled before the prefect of the city to
be examined by him.

### 10. She is exiled to Nicomedia; her death

Seeing her boldness for the truth, and unable to bear the
nobility and unchangeableness of her love toward God, and
wishing to stop her unceasing agitation for the recall of the
saintly John, they sent her too into exile, to the metropolis
of the Nicomedians in the province of Bithynia. She had tasted
God's grace, and nobly and manfully she bore, for love toward
God, the snowstorms of the trials and manifold afflictions that
came upon her. All the rest of her life she spent in the
metropolis of the Nicomedians, fulfilling there too, unchanged,
all her ascetic practice and her rule. Having fought the good
fight, [she received] the crown of patience; and she commended
her flock to Marina — holy, and fallen asleep in God — her
kinswoman and spiritual daughter, whom she had received from
the spotless and saving baptism, beseeching her to take up the
care of the souls there and to keep herself unshaken in all
things. And this Marina did — not only for the rest of the
time the saintly Olympias spent in the metropolis of the
Nicomedians, but also after her death.

### 11. Her last charge to Marina

For when the holy woman was about to be added to the holy
fathers, and to depart from this present life and be with
Christ, she once more charged in writing the said Marina —
asleep in God — to take all the more thought and oversight,
commending to her, after God, all the sisters and the care of
them. And having done this, she slipped out from the winter of
human affairs, putting in to the calm harbor of our souls,
Christ God.

---

### 12. Her burial at St. Thomas's at Brochthoi; her death-day

But before her holy body was buried, she appeared in a dream to
the metropolitan of that city of the Nicomedians, saying: "Put
my relic in a coffin, and put it in a ship, and let it go on
its voyage; and in whatever place the ship comes to rest, go
ashore there and lay me down."

The metropolitan did what she told him in the vision: he put
the body with the coffin in the ship and let it go on its
voyage. At the hour of midnight the ship reached the shore
before the porch of the venerable house of the holy apostle
Thomas at Brochthoi, and stood still there, going no farther.

At that same hour an angel of the Lord appeared in a dream to
the abbot and the steward of that venerable house, saying:
"Rise, and take the coffin which you will find in the ship that
is putting in at the shore before the porch, and lay it in the
sanctuary."

When this was told them, they saw all the gates of the church
opened of themselves. But they were still dreaming, and thought
what had happened was a fantasy, and barred the gates again —
and he who had appeared before appeared to them again, up to
three times pressing them with great vehemence and saying:
"Go out and take up the coffin of the holy Olympias — for she
endured much for God's sake — and lay it in the sanctuary."

Then they rose, and saw again the gates of the church opened,
and doubting no longer, they took the holy Gospels and a cross
and hand-candlesticks, with candles and incense, and went out
in procession to the porch. Finding her holy relic in the ship
[obscured in the scan], with candles and great glorifying of
God and thanksgiving they laid her holy relic in the sanctuary
of the said venerable house of the holy apostle Thomas at
Brochthoi. Many healings were to be seen taking place at her
holy tomb — unclean spirits driven out, and many manifold
diseases fleeing from those oppressed by them.

The holy and saintly and blessed handmaid of God, Olympias,
died on the twenty-fifth of the month of July, in the reign of
Arcadius, the most divine and most pious emperor — numbered
with the choir of the holy confessors, and reigning together
through the unending ages with the immortal king, Christ our
God.

After her death, Marina — the true handmaid of God, her
Christ-loving kinswoman and spiritual daughter, whom, as was
said, she had received from the holy and spotless and saving
baptism — made the love she bore that blessed soul plain to
all. She fulfilled her bequest and all that had been charged
upon her, and embraced and cherished and steered the whole
company of her flock, which Olympias had commended, after God
and our lady the all-holy Mother of God, into her hands — so
that none of the sisters felt any lack from the parting with
that saint. And after the holy woman's death, the aforesaid
Elisanthia the deaconess, her kinswoman, was appointed hegumene
of that holy flock of Christ — keeping unchanged all the rule
she had received from that holy and blessed soul, and imitating
all her virtues.

### 13. Her alms and her ascetic life

So much be said. But I judged it necessary and most useful to
set down in this account, one by one, the holy virtues of the
saintly and genuine handmaid of God, Olympias, for the profit
of many. For no place, no country, no desert, no island, no
far corner was left without a share of this famous woman's
gifts: she supplied churches with vessels for the holy service,
and monasteries and common houses of monks, and the poor, and
those in prison, and those in exile — sowing, in a word, the
whole inhabited world with her alms. And the blessed Olympias
herself leapt up to the highest bound of almsgiving and
lowliness of mind, beyond which nothing further could be found:
a life without vainglory, a form without affectation, an
unfeigned character, an unadorned face, a sleepless wakefulness,
a body made thin as spirit, a mind that never crawled, a
thought without arrogance, an untroubled heart, a spirit
without meddling, a love without measure, a fellowship past
understanding, clothing easy to despise, self-denial without
measure, straightness of mind, hopes in God unceasing, alms
past telling — the ornament of all the lowly. And she was
duly honored by the most holy Patriarch John. She abstained
from meat, and for the most part went unwashed; if need arose
through sickness, she bore it — for her stomach troubled her
continually — and she went down into the water in her little
tunic, ashamed, as they say, even before herself.

---

### 14. She serves the bishops

With many fathers, as I said, she served the most blessed John
the archbishop, and showed herself worthy of his virtue. When
the plot was laid against him and he was exiled, as was said
before, the holy woman supplied his needs and those of his
companions unceasingly — which is no small thing for Christ's
workers, who night and day care for the things of Christ. It is
as when Paul greets Persis, Tryphaena, and Tryphosa [Rom.
16:12]: that same greeting the holy and God-imitating Olympias
too, it may be, received.

I know that this all-virtuous and God-breathed Olympias served
also the blessed Nectarius, archbishop of Constantinople — who
in church matters put great trust in her — and Amphilochius,
bishop of Iconium, and Optimus, and Peter, and Gregory the
brother of holy Basil, and Epiphanius, archbishop of
Constantia in Cyprus, and many other of the holy and
God-bearing fathers who sojourned in the imperial city. Why
speak of those to whom she gave fields and money besides? When
Optimus, named above, died then in Constantinople, with her
own hands she closed his eyes, as one does for a great man.
And besides these she refreshed the afflicted not a little in
all things — Antiochus of Ptolemais, and Acacius the bishop of
Beroea, and the holy Severianus the bishop of Gabala — and in a
word she served, one by one, all the clergy who sojourned
there, and countless ascetics and virgins.

### 15. Her trials and her virtues

For the sake of their sympathy with them, she drained many
temptations to the dregs through the working of the one willing
in evil and unpracticed in good; she fought no small fights for
the truth of God; night and day she lived inseparably with
boundless tears; submitting to every human ordinance for the
Lord's sake [I Pet. 2:13], full of all reverence, bowing to the
saints, honoring bishops, revering the presbyters, respecting
the clergy, welcoming asceticism, cherishing virginity,
sustaining widows, rearing orphans, shielding the aged,
visiting the sick, mourning with sinners, guiding the strayed,
pitying all, unsparingly tender to the poor — and she catechized
many unbelieving women and helped them with the means of life.
An unforgettable name for kindness she left through her whole
life. The ten-thousand swarm of her household slaves she called
back from slavery to freedom, and showed them equal in honor to
her own nobility — or rather, to tell the truth, in the holy
woman's dress they looked nobler still. For nothing could be
found meaner than her garments: the coverings of this brave
woman were unworthy of the very meanest. The gentleness she
nurtured surpassed in innocence even that of little children.
No blame was ever found in this Christ-bearer, not even toward
her neighbors; all her unworldly life was spent in compunction
and a constant flow of tears. One might sooner see a spring
failing of its waters in the summer heat than see her eyes —
ever downcast, ever beholding Christ — cease from tears. Why
should I linger? However much leisure I give my mind in telling
the contests of her fired soul and her virtues, words will be
found far behind deeds. Let no one think I speak in fine
phrases about the passionless Olympias, now wholly a precious
vessel of the Holy Spirit — I speak as an eyewitness who beheld
the life and angelic conduct of this blessed one, as her true
spiritual friend and nearest kin — and many things were
dispensed at her direction.

### 16. Exhortation

This divine and God-breathed Olympias, then, no longer minding
the things of the flesh — submitting herself, obeying rulers,
bowing to the authorities of the Church, honoring bishops and
presbyters with reverent speech, esteeming the whole clergy —
was counted worthy of confession for the truth, receiving many
snowstorms of ill-timed slanders. Her life is judged among the
confessors by all the godly who dwell in Constantinople: for
she came within a hair of death in her struggles for God. And
dying in these, she took up the blessed glory — crowned in the
unending age, dancing in the undefiled dwellings with holy
souls, dwelling forever with those like herself, and boldly
demanding from the Master God the rewards of her good works.

---

### 17. A prayer for the readers

Let us ask in our prayers the all-merciful and man-loving God
to turn aside the torments of our sins, and to quench the
Gehenna kindled by our own offenses — and to draw near in
repentance to the compassionate God, and receive from Him the
great mercy. The holy and blessed woman charges us who read
and who hear, in Christ Jesus, teaching from the voice of holy
Paul, the chief of the holy apostles: "Hold the traditions"
[I Cor. 11:2]; "and see how you walk, not as unwise but as
wise, buying up the time, for the days are evil" [Eph.
5:15–16]; "for our wrestling is not against blood and flesh,
but against the principalities, against the powers, against
the world-rulers of the darkness of this age, against the
spiritual hosts of wickedness in the heavenly places" [Eph.
6:12] — that is, he speaks not of men only, but of the unclean
spirits that exceedingly press in upon each of the erring and
undermine the unstable. "Therefore take up the panoply of God"
[Eph. 6:13] — that is, chastity in body and in spirit,
lowliness of mind, meekness, self-denial. "Let no one render
evil for evil to anyone" [I Thess. 5:15]; but if you see your
brother stumbling in anything, or about to be bruised by the
devil, do not pass him by in silence and let him fall — but,
as the divine Scripture teaches us, "reprove, rebuke, exhort"
[II Tim. 4:2], "watching yourself, lest you also be tempted"
[Gal. 6:1]. Let no one deceive you, then, with persuasive
flattery of empty and vain words; but live honorably toward
all, and most of all "toward those of the household of faith"
[Gal. 6:10]. Consider this too: if a woman joined to a fleeting
and corruptible husband is found being corrupted by another,
she is subjected to punishments and penalties and banishments
— how much more shall he who was betrothed to our Master and
Lord Jesus Christ, and left Him, and clung to this fleeting
and vain life, be counted worthy of worse punishments? But may
the Lord make us blameless and spotless before Him in love, by
the prayers of our holy glorious lady, the Mother of God and
ever-virgin Mary, and of the holy Olympias — giving, to those
who read and those who hear with their whole soul, grace and
mercy in the day of defense, and delivering one and all from
the working of the devil.

### 18. The writer's request

I too, the sinner who also wrote this, ask you and adjure you,
by the man-loving God the Almighty, and by our Lord Jesus
Christ, and by the Holy Spirit — you who read in peace, and
you who hear in good hope — pray that forgiveness of sins and
a good defense be granted to my poor soul, and to all who read,
by the man-loving Saviour, our true and living God, Jesus
Christ. For to Him belongs glory, honor, and worship, with the
Father and the Holy Spirit, now and ever and to the ages of
ages. Amen.

*Here ends the Life of the holy Olympias the deaconess.*

## SERGIA'S ACCOUNT OF THE RETURN OF HER RELICS

*The Account of the holy and most God-loving abbess Sergia
concerning the holy Olympias. Bless, father.*

### 1. Why she writes

Though we have come to know in small part — the godly and
virtuous asceticism and conduct of the holy Olympias being sure
through what was set forth before — yet I, the sinner Sergia, to
whom by God's allowance the governance, that is the abbacy, of
this venerable monastery has been entrusted, wish to hand down
a few things of what I received from my holy mothers and
teachers who have fallen asleep before me, to those who after
me shall succeed to the governance of the monastery. I thought
it good, then, to put this too into the present book, for the
safety and profit of our souls — and so that all, men and
women, may know the removal that came about through sins, and
the restoration again through God's grace in this present
monastery.

### 2. The Nika fire; the sisters at St. Menas

Let it be plain to all, then, that the foundation and the
monastery built by the holy and memorable Olympias — and with
it that godly and unceasing and virtuous rule of hers — held
unbroken until the Nika riot that happened in the reign of the
saintly Justinian. But when, through sins, the burning came
about at the Nika, as was said, and the Great Church was
burned, then the said monastery of the holy Olympias, lying
next to it, was burned with it; and all things went to ruin
together in that burning. Under that necessity and the violent
calamity, all the souls dwelling in her said monastery fled
with nothing, and went to St. Menas, and lived there six years
— because near St. Menas lay the house called "of the Mangana"
and its bakery, and from this they had a little comfort for
their need — the same house belonging to this day to the
oft-mentioned monastery of the holy Olympias.

After that burning, the holy and all-venerable Great Church
was built by the saintly Justinian. Likewise the now-surviving
monastery of the holy Olympias — which to this day, as you all
know, is called "Olympias's" after her holy name, as if from
above and from the beginning the holy Olympias herself had
become the founder and establishment and salvation of her said
monastery and of the souls in it. And to this day she does not
cease, awfully, through her wondrous visions, to visit and care
for and shelter and preside over in all things her own
monastery and the souls in it, as was said — as will be shown
in what follows, through her vision speaking in her own words:
"I am with you all the days of your life." [Matth. 28:20]

### 3. Justinian rebuilds; the dedication at Christmas

After the said monastery was built by the saintly Justinian, he
restored all those souls again from St. Menas to their own
monastery — granting to the monastery three ounces of water
daily, and civic loaves, and precious furnishings — for the
burning, as was said, had consumed the first things with
everything else. The saintly Justinian held the dedication of
the Great Church on the eve of the holy and all-glorious
Nativity of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ; and on the next
day — that is, on the all-venerable feast itself of Christ's
holy Nativity — he held the dedication of the monastery of the
holy and righteous Olympias, which survives to this day,
granting it, as was said, the three daily ounces of water, and
the rest.

### 4. The Persians burn St. Thomas's; the relics on the waters

Some years after I, the sinner and unworthy Sergia, had
succeeded by God's allowance to the abbacy of that monastery,
the godless Persians made their raid, and the monastery of St.
Thomas across the water at Brochthoi was burned — where, as was
shown before, the precious and venerable relic of the holy
Olympias was kept — so that the coffin itself and her holy
relics were carried about upon the waters. I judged it fitting,
for the profit and stirring of many souls — especially of those
devoted to God — to make this too plain in the present book.

---

### 5. Sergia gathers the relics from the waters

When I, the sinner and unworthy Sergia, learned that through
sins the said monastery of St. Thomas had been burned, I fell
into great despondency. Crossing over with great haste, I
gathered her holy relics, soaked through with the waters — and
the waters where they floated, believe me, were filled with
blood — so that I was struck with wonder, and glorified the
man-loving God who gives grace to His saints and works wonders
through them in life and after their falling asleep — who also
glorifies those who glorify Him, as the divine Scripture says
[I Kings 2:30] — so that in this holy and blessed woman was
fulfilled the thirty-third psalm, which says through the holy
prophet and hymn-writer David: "The Lord keeps all their bones;
not one of them shall be broken" [Ps. 33:21]. So I, the sinner
Sergia, as was said, took and gathered them all with fear and
great carefulness — held at the same time by boundless joy —
and brought them to her lowly monastery.

### 6. Olympias appears to a sister in a dream

So greatly did the grace of the saint consent to dwell with us,
her sinful and unworthy servants — impressing in this, as it
were, the stamp of her life and converse in the flesh — that
while I, the sinner Sergia, was there across the water at
Brochthoi, as was said, to gather and bring back her precious
and holy relics, in that very night she appeared within the
monastery in a dream to one of the sisters, saying to her:
"Behold, after so many years I have come to dwell with you, and
I will not leave you henceforth." When I returned from across
the water with her holy relics and learned this, I glorified
the man-loving God still more — who, as was said, alone works
great wonders through His saints, for the salvation of our
sinful souls, and for the stirring of our sluggishness.

### 7. Patriarch Sergius sends John; the relics bleed at the anointing

Straightway, then, I reported to our most holy and most blessed
ecumenical Patriarch Sergius, that her precious relic should be
laid in her lowly monastery. And he sent John the presbyter,
surnamed "of the bakers," with other most reverent clergy, to
perform the laying. Believe me, then — me, the wretched and
sinful Sergia — that I say nothing outside the truth; only I
cannot tell the virtues of the holy and blessed woman as they
deserve. But when the said papa John the presbyter came to
anoint her holy relics in the basin of the holy baptism, in the
presence of the said lords and the most reverent clergy and all
of us sinful nuns, her holy relics gushed forth before them all
so much blood that the hands of papa John himself were filled;
and he touched his hands to his own face for faith and
sanctification, and his face too was filled with blood. And the
linens were filled besides, in which her holy relics were
wrapped. Nor was this all: one of the most reverent clergy
standing by, receiving those same holy and all-venerable relics
from the hands of papa John in that anointing — from the
abundance of the precious blood his hands too became full of
blood, and his face likewise, for he too rubbed his hands on
his own face for sanctification. At all this, fear and trembling
and amazement, so great and of such a kind, seized all, small
and great, that all said with weeping: "Never have we beheld
such wondrous marvels."

### 8. The healings; the demons' testimony

But of the wondrous and memorable healings that took place at
the laying of her holy relics, there is no need to pass this
over either without marking it — lest we be called to account
as having written the virtues of the saint carelessly. For you
who have the divine love and light upon this book do not ignore
what profit and stirring it puts into God's contenders and
God-lovers among you, to read the memories and virtues of
saints — especially of those who passed the whole time of their
life in asceticism and affliction and patience, and reached the
calm harbor of unfailing salvation. What concerns the godly and
virtuous asceticism and conduct and patience of this holy
Olympias has already been set forth to you above, as you know;
but now it is needful and most profitable to take up the word
again and make plain to you a few of the healings worked by the
precious relics of the ever-memorable and blessed woman. But,
for the Lord's sake, all you who light upon this present book,
receive with all assurance what is written by me, the sinner
Sergia; and let no one think I have marked down anything
outside the truth and what is fitting — for this too tends to
the profit of your souls. For as you know, our Lord Jesus
Christ says to the holy apostle Thomas, when he disbelieved:
"Be believing, and not unbelieving" [John 20:27]; and elsewhere
again: "Blessed are those who have not seen, and have believed"
[John 20:29]. I say this because the holy and precious relics of
this holy and virtuous Olympias healed very many, men and
women, of unclean demons and other diseases. Those healed of
the unclean spirits had kept them hidden for many years — as
the unclean spirits themselves cried out, going forth and being
driven: "So many years have we lain hidden in men, and no one
exposed us — until now, when this wicked old woman did." One
demon she drew up from the very ankles of the woman and cast
out, after nine years.

---

### 9. More healings, to this day

Why speak one by one of the healings and wonder-workings of
this godly and virtuous Olympias? For I know well that if I
wished to write her virtues in detail, time would wholly fail
me in the telling; and I fear lest, with the multitude of
things said, I weary the God-loving hearers and those who light
upon this present book. To speak simply: many other healings
took place as her holy relics were laid. And to this day we see
them wrought upon many men and women and children — through
her manifest and wondrous visions and overshadowings, and
through her holy relics, as was said — she granting, to those
who come to her with all their soul and unfeigned faith,
release from afflictions together with healing of pains.

### 10. Glory to God for His saints

For all this, then, we must glorify the man-loving and merciful
God, who gives grace to His saints — especially to those who
through much affliction and patience walked the strait and
narrow way that leads to life. For as you know, the divine
Scripture says: "Strait and narrow is the way that leads to
life, and few are those who enter by it" [Matth. 7:14]. So much
for this; and of His giving grace to His saints and to all who
fear Him, our holy and righteous forefather David the prophet
says thus in his songs: "The Lord is near to all who call upon
Him in truth; He will do the will of those who fear Him, and He
will hear their supplication and save them" [Ps. 144:18–19].

### 11. Her charge to the sisters

You, then, my little children and sisters, I, the wretched and
sinful Sergia — called by God's allowance your mother —
beseech, by the mercies of God: knowing what a holy and saintly
and blessed woman's children and servants you are called, so
strive in all things for the Lord's sake, and imitate to your
power her godly conduct in ways and in asceticism and in
patience — so that through her holy prayers you may be counted
worthy, without condemnation and without shame, with unveiled
face [II Cor. 3:18] — all of you, and those who by God's
command after my death shall join you and contend with you in
the angelic and God-pleasing conduct — unceasingly, all, small
and great, bearing your lamps filled with oil [Matth. 25:7] —
to stand before His fearful and dread tribunal, and to be
numbered with those blessed and wise virgins.

### 12. Pray for me

I beseech all of you, for the Lord's sake, to pray earnestly
also for me, the sinful and pitiable Sergia, your mother by
God's allowance — that the compassionate God may overlook the
multitude of my grievous sins, and count me worthy of mercy,
and give me strength — so that I too, having contended with you
to my power and lived well in love toward mankind, when it
pleases His goodness, may slip out of this human life and have
boldness in His heavenly and eternal kingdom, numbered with the
holy and righteous souls who pleased Him, together with all of
you — so that I may say to Him: "Behold, I and the children
whom Thou hast given me, Lord."

### 13. Keep the commandments; the two warnings

Yes, my blessed ladies and sisters, hear me beseeching you: let
us fear God from our soul and love Him, keeping in all things,
with earnestness and readiness and much care and wakefulness,
with much lowliness of mind and a contrite heart, with unroving
and downcast eye, His righteous and good commandments. For as
you know, no small condemnation lies upon those who serve Him
with contempt and carelessness — and above all upon us, who
have renounced the world and all its cares, and pledged
ourselves to serve Him alone, the man-loving God, and to cleave
to Him always, together with our all-holy and blessed and
spotless lady, the Mother of God, who bore Him without seed,
and with all His saints. Let us love one another sincerely from
the heart, honor one another, and submit to one another for the
Lord's sake; and, to speak simply, let us bear one another's
burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ [Gal. 6:2]. For as you
all know, for this we left the world, as was said, and all
things in it, and promised to bear His good and light yoke
[Matth. 11:30] — so that through small affliction, and the
contending and patience amid the terrors that, as is likely,
come upon us, we may all be counted worthy, unceasingly, pure
and clean in soul and in body, having no spot or wrinkle at all,
at any time, nor our conscience condemning us in anything
[Ephes. 5:27] — bearing, as was said, our lamps bright and
unquenched, to meet our Bridegroom Jesus Christ, and with Him,
clothed in white and spotless robes, to enter into the
marriage-feast of the kingdom of heaven, and to be counted
worthy to hear that blessed voice crying: "Come, you blessed of
My Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the
foundation of the world" [Matth. 25:34]. And may we never hear:
"How did you come in here, not having a wedding garment?"
[Matth. 22:12] Nor may we hear that other: "Depart from Me; I
do not know who you are" [Matth. 25:12] — but, passing without
hindrance and without obstacle by all the opposing and adverse
powers, may we be counted worthy to encamp in the eternal
tents, and to be in enjoyment, with all His righteous, of His
eternal good things, by the prayers of the saints. Amen.

---

### 14. Trust in her prayers

For I believe my Christ, that through the prayers of the
handmaid of God, the holy Olympias — our lady and mother, who
after the compassionate and merciful God became the salvation
and establishment and succor of us all (and becomes it to this
day, as you all know) — through her unceasing converse and
protection and stirring of our sluggishness, we shall be counted
worthy to obtain the good things He promised to all who pleased
Him — we, as was said, waking to our power out of the sleep of
laziness that holds us, and keeping the rule handed down to us
with all readiness, unwounded and undiminished. For in all
these God is well pleased. [Rom. 13:11]

### 15. Forgive me

But if I, the sinner, your mother Sergia, ever in anything
grieved you — whether by word or by manner or by deed — for the
Lord's sake forgive me. For you know what care and affliction
those who preside have, and how they are constrained, as those
who shall give account, if they neglect anything of the flock,
or overlook anything that tends to the establishment and
salvation of those under them. And when it goes well, we ought
not to grieve. But since we are all weak, may God have mercy on
us all. Pray, then, for me, for the Lord's sake, that I may be
counted worthy, together with you, to stand without
condemnation before His fearful and dread tribunal.

### 16. Her promise

And I, the sinner, making bold, say to you: if I find boldness
there, I will not cease importuning the merciful and
compassionate God — that He shelter and steer you all, both in
this present life, and after your departure hence count you
worthy of His heavenly and immortal kingdom, and number you
together with me, your mother by God's allowance — so that
then, as was said before, I may say to Him: "Behold, I and the
children whom Thou hast given me, Lord." Amen. So be it, in
Christ Jesus our Lord, to whom be glory and might to the ages
of ages. Amen.

*Here ends the Account of Sergia concerning the holy Olympias.*

## Note on the Greek texts

The *Life* is printed from several manuscripts (sigla P, F, E in
the apparatus) in *Analecta Bollandiana* 15 (1896), pp. 400–423.
The Internet Archive OCR of vol. 15 gives clean Greek with only
light noise — *k* for *kh*, *o* for *s*, and the like — which the
translation reads through. The print carries Latin marginal
summaries and Scripture references; the references are kept
where they stand. Its Latin critical apparatus and its long
Latin footnotes are the editor's, not the text's, and are not
translated here.

Sergia's *Account* is printed in *Analecta Bollandiana* 16
(1897), pp. 43–62. The Archive's OCR of vol. 16 renders the
Greek type in Latin lookalike characters and cannot be read as
it stands; the Greek was therefore re-read from the page images
themselves (journal pp. 43–62, scan leaves 47–66), with a fresh
Greek OCR checked by eye against the printed page at every
difficult spot. A handful of readings stay uncertain and are
flagged in the English: the *silignarion* named among Olympias's
gifts (a word the dictionaries do not know), the name of her
second city house (lost in the scan), one clause at the finding
of the relics in the ship at Brochthoi, and two nonstandard
forms in Sergia (*klētoreuesthe*, *arembastō* — given as the
codex has them). The print's own chapter numbers are kept; the
OCR's misreadings of them (13 as 18, 10 as 40, 16 as 17) are
corrected silently. The succession of Marina and Elisanthia at
the end of the *Life*'s chapter 12 is confused in the source
itself; it is translated as printed.
