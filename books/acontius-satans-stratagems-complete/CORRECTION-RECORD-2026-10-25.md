## The claims withdrawn, and their corrections

1. **Completeness (title, Part I).** Was: "SATAN'S STRATAGEMS — THE COMPLETE EIGHT BOOKS." The desk's recount from the 1631 Latin (IA b3033018x): Books V–VIII hold 41,435 Latin words; the shelf's English for them is 22,187 words — roughly forty percent of a full rendering — with 33 "…" jump-cuts and 18 bracketed summary lines. Now: the title reads "THE EIGHT BOOKS IN PART (1648 ENGLISH, I–IV · ABRIDGED DRAFT, V–VIII)"; a status note at the head of Part I states the corrected position; the Basis note describes the abridgement plainly where "paragraph for paragraph" stood before.
2. **Primacy (five places).** Were: "Books V–VIII here Englished for the first time" (subtitle); "For 378 years, half of Acontius's book had no English" (editor's opening); "The last four books of Acontius have waited three hundred and seventy-eight years for an English voice... completed here" (dedication); "no complete English translation of these books has been found... the first Englishing of the second half" (Basis note); "Now first Englished" (Part II head); "the first English Satan's Stratagems complete in eight books" (Part VI closing). The falsifying record: Walter T. Curtis's complete translation of all eight books, Sutro Branch, California State Library, 1940 — verified at the desk in the Folger catalogue, HathiTrust record 007126972, and the Hofstra Radin finding aid. Every one of the five places is corrected in the text, naming Curtis and 1940 (1648 → 1940 is 292 years, and the editor's opening now says so). The word "Complete." standing alone in the editor's opening ("both men got their books back. Complete. In English.") is removed.
3. **The false preservation note (Part II, Book V head).** Was: "[The book opens mid-argument, the opening lines being imperfectly preserved in the scan...]" The desk read the page images (leaves 246–247, pp. 221–222): clean, complete, legible; Book V's programmatic first sentence stands whole across them, and the draft simply begins mid-sentence at its second limb. The note now states that truth and withdraws the scan excuse expressly.
4. **The wanting leaf (Basis note).** Was: "One leaf of the Latin copy (pp. 193–194, in Book IV) is wanting in the scan; it does not touch the translation, which rests on Books V–VIII complete." Now: the Archive's note and the desk's inspection (pages present) are both stated, the point flagged for re-inspection, and the completeness tail removed.

## What was NOT changed

The body of all six parts — the 1648 English of Books I–IV and the abridged draft of Books V–VIII — stands word for word as shelved, including the EEBO ligature damage in Books I–IV and the 33 jump-cuts and 18 summary brackets in Books V–VIII, which the corrected Basis note now describes honestly instead of defending. The editor's signed opening essay is corrected only at its falsified facts (the 378 years; the standing "Complete."); its voice and its Boneyard framing are his and are untouched. **The folder's name** still carries the word "complete" from the withdrawn claim; renaming it is deferred to the rebuild's landing, and this record names the leftover so it is not hidden.

Curtis's translation is cited here as the prior complete translation. Its copyright status is NOT VERIFIED (no quotable renewal record found, 2026-10-25 ferry return), and it is therefore check-only for the rebuild: the commissioned translation is made from the public-domain Latin (Oxford, 1631), never from Curtis.
