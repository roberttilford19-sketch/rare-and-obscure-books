## The six faults and their repairs

**Fault 1 — the mood changed at verse 26 (line 31), the book's worst fault.** James: "we were wounded in our minds and **would have hid ourselves**" — a wish or impulse under grief. The shelf had dropped the auxiliary and printed a deed done: "wounded in mind **we hid ourselves**." Line 31 now reads "wounded in mind **we would have hidden ourselves**," restoring James's conditional in this edition's modern register ("hidden" for James's "hid"). Recorded in line, as the desk check carried it: the Roberts-Donaldson rendering in the Ante-Nicene Fathers also reads "we hid ourselves," so the shelf's sentence had pedigree in the tradition; it is nonetheless a fault on this file's own terms, because the file names James, and James alone, as its basis. Which rendering stands closer to the Greek is a question neither audit settled and this repair does not claim to settle.

**Fault 2 — no Internet Archive source named.** The Sources gave a full citation of James 1924 and no road back to any scan. The first Sources bullet now names the item, in the same form as the sibling files for this volume: "Internet Archive identifier: **JAMESApocryphalNewTestament1924**." The identifier was verified, not assumed: it is the identifier both sibling files (Apocalypse of Peter; Questions of Bartholomew) already name for this same volume, and the archive.org metadata for the item was resolved at the desk on the repair date — the item stands under that identifier, titled "JAMES - The Apocryphal New Testament," dated 1924, its description listing "Gospel of Peter, Frag I" among the Passion Gospels.

**Fault 3 — leftover template text at line 3.** The line read "Rare & Obscure Books series — **No. ___**," a placeholder never filled and never removed. The shelf index assigns this book no series number (its number column stands blank), so, following the now-standing precedent of the Acts of John, Apocalypse of Peter, and Questions of Bartholomew repairs, the placeholder was removed rather than filled with an invented number. The line now reads "Rare & Obscure Books series."

**Fault 4 — the dangling Translator Notes reference (line 54).** The second Sources bullet sent the reader to testimonies "summarized in the Translator Notes accompanying this edition." No Translator Notes exist — not in this file, and not in any second file in this folder. The false pointer is struck. The bullet's true substance stands: "The ancient testimonies to this Gospel (Origen; Eusebius quoting Serapion of Antioch, c. A.D. 190; Theodoret) are printed by James at pp. 13–14 of the same volume." A reader is now sent to James's pages, which exist, and nowhere else.

**Fault 5 — the Basis line (line 7), sustained in part only, repaired to exactly that extent.** The old first clause, "rendering of the Greek Akhmim fragment," could be heard in isolation as a claim to have rendered the Greek directly; the same sentence's second clause already corrected the impression ("made from M. R. James's translation"), and the desk graded the fault as phrasing, not a false basis claim. The line is reordered so James leads, removing the isolated misreading without changing anything the file claims: "Basis: Fresh clear modern English rendering **made from M. R. James's translation of the Greek Akhmim fragment** in The Apocryphal New Testament (Oxford, 1924)." The remainder of the Basis paragraph is untouched.

**Fault 6 — the minor wording at verse 56 (line 43), harmless but not James's.** James: "look in and see the place where he lay, **that he is not here**: for he is risen…" The shelf had turned his subordinate clause, which depends on "see," into an independent reason: "…where he lay, **for he is not here**; for he has risen…" The subordinate construction is restored: line 43 now reads "look in and see the place where he lay, **that he is not here**; for he has risen and has gone away to the place from where he was sent." The rest of the verse stands as the edition had it.
