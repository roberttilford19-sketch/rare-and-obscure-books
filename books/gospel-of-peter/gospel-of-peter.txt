THE GOSPEL OF PETER

Rare & Obscure Books series — No. ___

Original language: Greek (Akhmim fragment, c. late 2nd century A.D.)

Basis: Fresh clear modern English rendering of the Greek Akhmim fragment, made from M. R. James's translation in The Apocryphal New Testament (Oxford, 1924). The Greek text survives in a little book found in a tomb at Akhmim in Egypt, together with part of the Book of Enoch and the description of Heaven and Hell printed by James as part of the Apocalypse of Peter. James's parenthetical alternative renderings have been resolved to the plainest sense; the verse numbers are those of his edition (sections I–XIV; verses 1–60).

THE GOSPEL OF PETER

(Fragment I — the Passion and Resurrection)

I. 1 But of the Jews no one washed his hands — neither Herod nor any of his judges. And when they would not wash, Pilate rose up. 2 Then Herod the king commanded that the Lord be taken into their hands, saying to them: "Do all to him that I commanded you to do."

II. 3 Now there stood there Joseph, the friend of Pilate and of the Lord; and knowing that they were about to crucify him, he came to Pilate and begged the body of Jesus for burial. 4 And Pilate sent to Herod and begged his body. 5 And Herod said: "Brother Pilate, even if no one had begged for him, we ourselves would have buried him, since the Sabbath is also dawning; for it is written in the Law that the sun should not set upon one that has been slain."

III. 6 And he delivered him to the people, before the first day of Unleavened Bread, their feast. And they took the Lord and pushed him along as they ran, and said: "Let us drag along the Son of God, now that we have got power over him." 7 And they put a purple robe on him, and made him sit upon the seat of judgment, saying: "Judge righteously, O King of Israel." 8 And one of them brought a crown of thorns and set it on the Lord's head; 9 and others stood and spat in his eyes, and others struck his cheeks; others pricked him with a reed, and some of them scourged him, saying: "With this honor let us honor the Son of God."

IV. 10 And they brought two criminals and crucified the Lord between them. 11 But he held his peace, as if he felt no pain. And when they had set the cross upright, they wrote on it: "This is the King of Israel." 12 And they laid his garments before him, and divided them among themselves and cast lots for them. 13 But one of those criminals reproached them, saying: "We are suffering this for the evils we have done; but this man, who has become the Savior of men — how has he injured you?" 14 And they were angry with him, and commanded that his legs not be broken, so that he might die in torment.

V. 15 Now it was midday, and darkness covered all Judea; and they were troubled and in distress, in case the sun had set, since he yet lived; for it is written for them that the sun should not set upon one who has been slain. 16 And one of them said: "Give him gall with vinegar to drink." And they mixed it and gave him to drink, 17 and they fulfilled all things and completed their sins upon their own heads. 18 And many went about with lamps, supposing that it was night, 19 and some fell down. And the Lord cried out aloud, saying: "My power, my power, you have forsaken me." And when he had said this, he was taken up. 20 And in that same hour the veil of the temple at Jerusalem was torn in two.

VI. 21 And then they pulled the nails from the hands of the Lord and laid him on the earth; and the whole earth was shaken, and great fear came upon all. 22 Then the sun shone out, and it was found to be the ninth hour. 23 And the Jews rejoiced, and gave his body to Joseph to bury it, because he had seen all the good things that he did. 24 And he took the Lord and washed him and wrapped him in linen and brought him into his own tomb, which is called the Garden of Joseph.

VII. 25 Then the Jews and the elders and the priests, when they saw what evil they had done to themselves, began to lament and to say: "Woe to our sins! The judgment and the end of Jerusalem have drawn near." 26 But I, with my companions, was in grief, and wounded in mind we hid ourselves, for we were hunted by them as criminals, and as men who meant to set the temple on fire. 27 And besides all these things we fasted, and sat mourning and weeping night and day until the Sabbath.

VIII. 28 But the scribes and Pharisees and elders gathered together, because they had heard that all the people were murmuring and beating their breasts, saying: "If such very great signs have come to pass at his death, see how righteous he was!" 29 And the elders were afraid and came to Pilate, entreating him and saying: 30 "Give us soldiers, so that we may watch his tomb for three days, in case his disciples come and steal him away, and the people suppose that he has risen from the dead, and do us harm." 31 And Pilate gave them Petronius the centurion with soldiers to watch the tomb. And the elders and scribes came with them to the tomb, 32 and when they had rolled a great stone, along with the centurion and the soldiers, all who were there together set it against the door of the tomb, 33 and smeared seven seals on it; and they pitched a tent there and kept watch.

IX. 34 And early in the morning, as the Sabbath dawned, a crowd came from Jerusalem and the country round about, to see the tomb that had been sealed. 35 Now in the night in which the Lord's day dawned, while the soldiers were keeping guard two by two in each watch, 36 there came a great sound in the heavens, and they saw the heavens opened, and two men descend from there, shining with great light, and draw near to the tomb. 37 And that stone which had been set against the door rolled away of itself and moved to one side, and the tomb was opened, and both the young men went in.

X. 38 When the soldiers saw this, they woke the centurion and the elders — for they too were there keeping watch. 39 And while they were still telling them what they had seen, again they saw three men come out of the tomb, two of them supporting the third, and a cross following after them. 40 And the heads of the two reached up to heaven, but the head of the one who was being led by them went up beyond the heavens. 41 And they heard a voice out of the heavens saying: "Have you preached to those who sleep?" 42 And an answer was heard from the cross, saying: "Yes."

XI. 43 Those men then took counsel together 44 to go and report these things to Pilate. And while they were still considering, again the heavens were opened, 45 and a man descended and entered into the tomb. When those who were with the centurion saw this, they hurried by night to Pilate, leaving the tomb which they were guarding, and told him all they had seen, in great distress, saying: "He truly was the Son of God." 46 Pilate answered and said: "I am clear from the blood of the Son of God; but this is what seemed good to you." 47 Then they all came and begged him and urged him to order the centurion and the soldiers to say nothing about what they had seen: 48 "For," they said, "it is better for us to incur the greatest sin before God, than to fall into the hands of the people of the Jews and be stoned." 49 Pilate therefore ordered the centurion and the soldiers to say nothing.

XII. 50 Now early on the Lord's day Mary Magdalene, a disciple of the Lord — who, being afraid because of the Jews, since they were inflamed with anger, had not done at the tomb of the Lord the things which women are accustomed to do for those who die and are beloved by them — 51 took the women her friends with her 52 and came to the tomb where he had been laid. And they were afraid in case the Jews should see them, and said: "Even if we were not able to weep and lament over him on the day when he was crucified, let us do so now at his tomb. 53 But who will roll away the stone for us from the door of the tomb, so that we may go in and sit beside him and do what is due? 54 For the stone is great, and we are afraid someone may see us. And if we cannot, let us at least set down at the door the things we bring as a memorial of him, and we will weep and lament until we come to our house."

XIII. 55 And they went and found the tomb open; and they drew near and looked in, and saw there a young man sitting in the middle of the tomb, of a fair countenance and clothed in very bright clothing, who said to them: 56 "Why have you come? Whom do you seek? Not him who was crucified? He has risen and has gone away. But if you do not believe it, look in and see the place where he lay, for he is not here; for he has risen and has gone away to the place from where he was sent." 57 Then the women were frightened and fled.

XIV. 58 Now it was the last day of Unleavened Bread, and many were leaving the city and returning to their own homes, since the feast was at an end. 59 But we, the twelve disciples of the Lord, were weeping and in sorrow, and each of us, grieving over what had happened, 60 went to his own house. But I, Simon Peter, and Andrew my brother, took our nets and went to the sea; and there was with us Levi the son of Alphaeus, whom the Lord—

(Fragment I ends here. For Fragment II — the description of Heaven and Hell from the same ancient manuscript — see James, The Apocryphal New Testament (1924), under "The Apocalypse of Peter.")
