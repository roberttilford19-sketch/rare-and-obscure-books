THE MARTYROLOGY OF ELIŠE: THE EIGHTH CHAPTER ON THE SUFFERINGS OF THE HOLY PRIESTS

> In Memoriam > > Karl Friedrich Neumann (1793–1870) > > Member of the Armenian Academy of the Mechitaristes at St. Lazaro; of the Asiatic Society of Paris; corresponding member of the Royal Academy of Sciences at Berlin; translator of Eliše's History of Vartan (London, 1830).

Dedicated and completed from the labors of Karl Friedrich Neumann (1793–1870), who in 1830 gave English readers Eliše's History of Vartan, and who left this Martyrology — the eighth chapter that the editors of Eliše appended to his seven — for a second volume that never came. His words stand at the head of this book; the labor he deferred is here, at last, done.

In 1830 Neumann told his readers why this chapter is not in his book:

> "The description of the humiliations and persecutions to which the Armenian priests and princes were subjected in Persia, forms a small work by itself, to which we shall have occasion to refer when translating Lazarus of Barb. On the present occasion, we only submit to the learned world a translation of the larger and far more important work."

The second volume never appeared, and no passage of the Martyrology was ever given in it. This book supplies the missing piece of Neumann's volume — nothing more, and nothing less. It is the first free English of the Neumann unit's missing crown. It is not, and does not claim to be, the first complete Eliše in English: complete modern versions exist, in copyright, and are named above with respect. What is offered here is the small work by itself, translated fresh from the Armenian, and given away free, as Neumann's readers have waited nearly two hundred years to read it.

A note on the title. Eliše, in his own preface, promises seven chapters. Every printed edition appends an eighth, under a heading like the one translated below: an eighth chapter, after the seventh, on the same battle and on the sufferings of the holy priests. Whether Eliše wrote it himself has been questioned; the Armenian Church has always read it with his book. It is translated here as the editions give it, and the question is left where Neumann left it — with the learned world.

An Eighth Chapter, Appended to the Seventh: On the Battle of Vardan and the Sufferings of the Holy Priests

In the sixteenth year of the reign of Yazkert, king of kings, the king went out on campaign against the land of the Kushans, and he returned from that campaign in great anger, his heart swollen with wrath. All the Armenian princes who were at the royal court he bound in fetters, hand and foot, and along the whole road of their journeying they were guarded by a company of soldiers, who loaded them with insults and blows as they dragged them along, and deliberately, in mockery of their sufferings, did the king's servants lay these humiliations upon them.

So the holy priests were brought in custody to the city of Nishapur, in the district of Apar, and there they were kept in prison, bound in iron fetters, hand and foot. And guards were set over them, men of the king's own servants, to keep watch over them day and night; for there was no rest for the prisoners by day, and by night they found no sleep, because of the weight of the iron upon their bodies.

But the holy company, according to the commandment they had received, gave themselves night and day to fasting and to prayer, and they did not cease from the service of psalmody at any hour. For they knew that the Lord of all sees the sufferings of his servants and repays to each according to his deeds. They were not troubled by the things that are seen, but they looked to the things that are unseen, and they counted the tortures of the body as nothing beside the crown that was prepared for them.

Now the man who had been set over the prisoners, the keeper of the prison, was a magus by religion, a chief of the magi, and a man honored at the royal court. Seeing the patience of the saints, and hearing, in the silence of the night, the sound of their psalmody rising from within the prison, he was struck with wonder. For every night, at the appointed hours, he heard them sing; and when he looked in upon them secretly, he saw their faces shining, and a light in the midst of the darkness of the prison, like the light of the sun at noonday.

One night he came to the door of the prison in secret and listened. And he heard the blessed Leontius the priest exhorting the company, and all of them answering with one voice; and the sight pierced his heart. He knocked softly, and when the door was opened to him, he fell at the feet of the blessed Joseph the catholicos, and said to him: "Man of God, I have seen your works, and I have heard your prayers in the night. I know that the God whom you worship is the true God, the Maker of heaven and earth. Teach me also, and count me among your disciples; for I desire to serve the God of the Christians all the days of my life."

The blessed Joseph answered him with joy: "Blessed are you, for flesh and blood has not revealed this to you. Come near, and we will show you the way of life." And the priests rejoiced greatly over him, as over a sheep that was lost and is found. And they instructed him in the faith of Christ over many days, coming to him secretly and he to them, and they taught him the Scriptures and the order of prayer.

Then, on a certain night, when all was quiet, the magus came again to the prison, bringing with him what was needed. The priests arose, and by the light of a lamp in the darkness they baptized him in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and they gave him also of the holy communion, the Body and Blood of Christ. And he sat with them at their poor table and ate bread with them, rejoicing; and he wept for joy, and said: "Now I know that I too shall live, though I die; for I have received the seal of the living God."

From that night he ministered to the prisoners in secret, bringing them food and whatever they needed, and he sat with them in the hours of prayer. And the saints prayed for him, and for all who would believe.

After these things the chief eunuch of the court, Nenshapuh, the king's storekeeper, came to the place where the priests were kept, having received charge over them; for the king had delivered them into his hand. And he said to the holy Joseph: "You are a bishop, and the head of these men. Why do you persist in this stubbornness? The king is merciful; if you will bow down to the sun, as the king commands, you shall live, and you shall return to your country with honor, and the princes who are bound for your sake shall be loosed."

But the blessed Leontius the priest answered before all, and the bishops interpreted his words: "Do not speak to us of two commands. You tell us first to bow down to the sun and then to fulfill the will of the king. Know this: we did not learn from our law to give the honor of God to any creature. The sun is a creature of God, set in the heavens to give light to the world; it rises upon the just and the unjust by the command of its Maker, and it neither hears prayer nor knows those who bow before it. If we were to worship it, we should deny the God who made it, and there would remain for us no hope of life. We honor the king, as our law commands; we pray for him and for his kingdom; but the worship of God we will not give to another."

And Nenshapuh said: "It was you who brought ruin upon the land of the Armenians. It was because of your teaching that the princes took up arms, and that the armies of the king were troubled."

Leontius answered: "You wrong us. Our law does not teach us to rebel against the king. We taught the people to honor the king and to obey him in all things that belong to this life, even to death for his sake; for so the apostle commanded us. But when the king commanded what belongs to God alone, then we could not obey; for we must obey God rather than men. The soldiers of your king who fell in the war fell by the king's own command, not by ours; and the princes who are now bound in prison are bound, not because they rebelled, but because they would not deny their God."

Then Nenshapuh reasoned with them a long while, speaking now with threats and now with promises of honor and of gifts, if only they would perform the king's command and bow to the sun; and so they would save their lives, and the lives of the princes, and the land of Armenia would be at peace. But the saints, with one mind and one voice, answered: "Our life is in the hand of God. Do with us what you will. We will not bow to the sun, and we will not deny Christ our God, who died for us and rose again. For we have a King who does not die, and a kingdom that has no end."

And when Nenshapuh saw their firmness, he went away and reported all their words to the king.

The king, when he heard it, was filled with anger, and he gave command that the holy priests should be put to death. And the executioners were called, and the saints were led out to the place of execution, to a desert place outside the city, a rough and stony ground. And they bound ropes about their bodies, and dragged them over the stones, so that their flesh was torn; and they asked them once more whether they would obey the king's command.

But the saints encouraged one another, and the blessed Joseph spoke to the company: "Brothers, this is the hour for which we were born. Do not fear those who kill the body. Remember the Lord, who for our sake endured the cross. Look up: the crown is ready."

And he lifted up his voice and prayed: "Lord Jesus Christ, receive our spirits, and join us to the company of your saints. You know that we did not choose this death in hatred of the king, but in love of you. Forgive those who slay us, for they know not what they do."

And with him the blessed Leontius cried aloud: "Lord, receive this voluntary sacrifice which we offer you with our whole heart, and mingle our blood with the blood of your martyrs." And all the saints said, "Amen."

Then the executioners struck. The sword flashed above the neck of the blessed Joseph, and one by one, in order, they were perfected by the sword. They were seven in number in that place, counting also the magus who had believed in Christ and was joined to their company: the blessed Joseph the catholicos, of Vayots Dzor; Leontius the priest, of Vanand; Sahak, bishop of Rshtuniq; Tatik, bishop of Basen; Mushegh the priest; Arshen the priest, of the village of Ełegik in Bagrevand; and the deacon Kachach, of Rshtuniq. And the two others, Samuel and Abraham, had been taken away to the district of Apar, to the village of Vardget, and there they received the crown; and the blessed Tatik, as some relate, was perfected in Khuzestan. So the holy company fulfilled their martyrdom and passed to Christ, whom they loved.

And when the deed was done, the chief of the executioners, who was himself secretly a Christian, gave command to his men to guard the bodies of the saints for ten days and more, until the royal caravan should pass by, that no one might steal the relics and scatter them abroad. And in the nights that followed, a great terror fell upon the guards: for they heard sounds as of an earthquake beneath the earth, and they saw flashes of light round about the bodies, and they saw the forms of the saints standing, as it were, alive, and speaking words that the guards could not understand. And the guards fled in fear, and told all that they had seen.

And the princes who were still bound in the prison heard of the martyrdom of the priests, and they grieved and rejoiced together: grieved, because they were left as sheep without shepherds; and rejoiced, because their fathers in God had received the crown. And they said to one another: "Let us also be ready; for perhaps our hour also is near."



Of Samuel and Abraham

Now there were with the saints, in the prison, two young disciples, Samuel and Abraham, who served them. After the martyrdom, the chief executioner called them out before him and said: "What are your names?" And one answered: "I am Samuel, a servant of Christ and a disciple of the blessed ones whom you slew; and this is Abraham." And the executioner said: "You speak boldly. Do you not fear death?" Samuel answered: "Our teachers taught us better than to fear you. You call us stubborn; but it is you who are stubborn in evil. The sun to which you bow is a creature; and the idols you serve are the work of men's hands. Our God made the heavens and the earth; him only we will serve."

Then the executioner was angry, and he commanded that they should be beaten, and they were beaten so cruelly that those who saw it thought they must die. But when they were half dead, they lifted up their voices and cried: "We thank you, Lord, that you have counted us worthy to suffer for your name. Finish quickly what you have begun," they said to the executioner, "and send us after our teachers."

But their sentence was otherwise: for it was decided to send them away to Assyria, to labor there in the king's service, as a punishment sharper than death, because they longed for death. And they went on their way with grief and with longing, saying: "Why were we left behind? Why did we not receive the crown with our teachers?"

In Assyria they labored in their appointed service for many years, and the blessed Abraham remained there, gathering alms from the faithful and carrying relief to those in need, and serving the confessors who were scattered in that land. And so the years passed, until the twelfth year of their punishment; and Abraham begged those who had charge over him that he might go up to the land of Armenia, to see the places where his teachers had confessed and died; and his request was granted him.



The Release of the Princes

Meanwhile the princes of Armenia lay in their bonds in the prison, thirty-five men of the great houses, besides many others of lesser rank who shared their captivity. These are the names of the princes who willingly, for the love of Christ, gave themselves into the royal prison:

From the house of the Artsrunis: Nershapuh, and Shavosp, and Shngin, and Mehruzhan, and Pargev, and Tajad. From the house of the Mamikonians: Hamazaspian, and Hamazasp, and Artavazd, and Mushegh. From the house of the Kamsarakans: Arshavir, and Tat, and Varaz, and Nerseh, and Ashot. From the house of the Amatunis: Vahan, and Arnak. From the house of the Gnunis: Atom, and Sam. From the house of the Artsrunis also: Shmavon, and Zuaren, and Aravan. From the house of the Mandakunis: Sahak, and Varsman. From the house of the Tashratsis: Arnak, and Shmavon. From the house of the Khorkhorunis: Abik, and Khosrov. And with them many other free men, some from the royal house and some from the houses of the princes, companions in arms and companions in suffering with the martyrs.

These men endured their imprisonment with great patience. No word of complaint or of blasphemy was heard from their mouths; but with thanksgiving they bore all, as men who knew for whose sake they suffered. They were dragged from place to place, bound hand and foot, through the deserts of the east, sleeping without beds on the bare ground, like wild beasts, through the heat of the summer and the cold of the winter, for the space of many years.

And the king, thinking that suffering would bend them, at last sent to them the great hazarapet of the court, saying: "Go to them, and tell them: From this day, govern yourselves; do not persist in your stubbornness. Bow down to the sun, and I will loose you from your cruel bonds, and each man shall return to his father's house and live."

The blessed princes answered: "You question us as though the king had truly sent you. We know that no such word has come from the king's mouth. We have all been taught the truth, and we shall not turn from it, neither for life nor for death. If the king wishes to ask us of these things, let him ask us himself; his sentence upon us is already accomplished, and we are ready for whatever he commands."

When the hazarapet heard this, he praised in his heart the firmness of their faith. And from that day he began to love them as men beloved of God. And though he was afterwards removed from his office and suffered loss himself because of the ruin of the land of Armenia, he never spoke evil of the prisoners to the day of his death.

At last, after many years, the king gave command that their bonds should be loosed. The iron was taken from their hands and feet, and clothing was given them, and they were assigned provision from the royal store, and they were placed with the army of the court, to go with the king's host to war.

And it came to pass in those days that Yazkert the king died, in the nineteenth year of his reign. And his two sons contended for the kingdom, and there was civil war between them for two years. The younger son, Peroz, took refuge with the king of the Hepthalites, and with his help he returned, and slew his brother, and took the throne. And Peroz remembered the Armenian princes who were in bonds, and he commanded that they should be released and sent back to their own land with honor. And so, after long years of suffering, the confessors returned to Armenia, and the churches received them with joy, as men who had come back from the dead.



The Wives of the Captives

But what shall we say of the wives and households of the captives, who were left behind in the land of Armenia? The tender women of Armenia, who had been brought up in softness, in chambers hung with silk, now went on foot to the houses of prayer, and with unceasing tears begged God to give their husbands strength to endure. They had been nourished from their childhood on rich foods; now they received coarse food with great thanksgiving, and reckoned their former delicacies as nothing. By day the summer sun burned their faces, and by night they slept on the hard ground; and psalms were continually in their mouths, and the reading of the prophets was their comfort.

They bound up one another's weakness, two and two, like yoke-fellows evenly matched in love, striving to reach without stumbling the harbor of peace. They forgot the frailty of their flesh, and became fellow-soldiers in the spiritual warfare; they fought and conquered with the weak things of the world, and cut off the death-bearing root of sin. With humility they smote down pride, and by that same humility they reached the heavenly height. They cut off the root of greed, and its death-bearing fruits withered on the branches. In their distant exile they became consolers of the afflicted; they labored with their own hands and were fed, and they shared the royal provision that was assigned to them with those who bore them company in captivity.

Like birds of passage they sang sweetly without bitterness, and their life was a living image of the world to come. Many winters melted like snow; spring came, and the swallows returned; but they could no more see the faces they longed for. Each spring reminded them of their beloved husbands; and their eyes yearned to see again the beauty of those faces. Yet they did not murmur; for they knew that the men for whom they waited were kept, not by the power of the king, but by the will of God, for the salvation of many.



Conclusion

These things we have written concerning the sufferings of the holy priests and the patience of the blessed princes, that those who read may know the power of faith, and may be stirred up to imitate their courage. For they did not love their lives unto death, but chose rather to die for the name of Christ than to live by denying him. And their blood, shed in a far country, became a seed of blessing for the land of Armenia; and their names are written in the book of life.

Pray for us, holy martyrs and confessors, that we too may be found worthy, not in word only but in deed, of the faith which you confessed with your blood. To Christ our God, who crowned you, be glory forever. Amen.

THE END.
