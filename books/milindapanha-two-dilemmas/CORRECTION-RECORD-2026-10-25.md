## Per-fault repairs

1. **Dilemma 21 referent swap (line 143) — the worst fault.** The shelf read: "the Tathāgata went to the brother of the elder Cūḷapanthaka, who had been driven out and was grieving and downcast, and gave him a fine piece of cloth." The Pali (*bhātarā*, instrumental — "by his brother") and the file's own note [14] agree: the Buddha went to the elder Cūḷapanthaka himself, expelled by his brother Mahāpanthaka. Repaired to: "the Tathāgata went to the elder Cūḷapanthaka, who had been driven out by his brother and was grieving and downcast, and gave him a fine piece of cloth." The actors are restored: the expelled man receives the visit, the cloth, and the comfort.

2. **False count of Book IV's dilemmas (lines 17, 185; and the same figure in the Sources, line 195).** Book IV (the Meṇḍakapañha) holds eighty-two dilemmas; Rhys Davids translated eighty. "Forty of the forty-two" is corrected to "eighty of the eighty-two" at line 17 and "two of the eighty-two" at line 185. The Sources' "the forty dilemmas he translated" is the same false figure in another form and is corrected to "the eighty dilemmas he translated" — disclosed here as part of this fault's repair: leaving a third "forty" standing would have left the repaired file contradicting itself on the very count the check sustained.

3. **False "in silence" characterisation (lines 17, 25, 27, 185, 187).** Rhys Davids did not pass over the two dilemmas in silence: at each omission he printed a titled, bracketed placeholder in place, with his reason — "[THE DILEMMA AS TO CONCEPTION. … This dilemma goes into details which can be best consulted in the Pâli]" (SBE XXXV, printed p. 185) and "[THE SECRETS OF A TATHÂGATA.] … it is best read in the original" (printed p. 237). All four "in silence" assertions and their companion claims are corrected to state the true disposition: line 17 now reads "omitted two, marking each omission in its place with a titled, bracketed note that gives his reason and directs his readers to the Pali"; line 25 quotes the two placeholders in place of "as if the question had never been put" and "the English falls silent"; line 27's "no erratum confesses them" and "bury them" are replaced with the placeholders' actual standing ("each stands in its place in his text, titled and bracketed, with his reason given in so many words" — two register words, "fall" and "feel the pinch," adjusted to carry the corrected sense, disclosed here); line 185 conforms; and line 187's "He gave his reasons nowhere that we can find" is replaced by the reasons as printed: "His reasons stand in the two placeholders themselves — Victorian delicacy stated in so many words."
